# Indian Retail Trader Outcomes: SEBI and Exchange Evidence (intraday cash, F&O, algos, investing)

Research date: 23 Oct 2026. Primary source for the most recent F&O data is the SEBI DEPA study "Profitability of Individual Traders in the Equity Derivatives Segment (FY25–FY26)", August 2026 (91 pages), read in full text — [SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf). Older studies were verified through SEBI press-release titles plus Indian financial press, because the SEBI press-release pages and Business Standard returned only headers or 403 errors to the fetch tool.

Timeline of SEBI retail-outcome studies (note: there was no January 2023 *intraday* study; January 2023 was the first F&O study):
- Jan 2023: F&O study, FY22 (top-10 brokers).
- 24 Jul 2024: Intraday equity cash segment study, FY19 / FY22 / FY23 (PR No. 37/2024 per SEBI URL; Taxmann cites PR 13/2024).
- 23 Sep 2024: Updated F&O study, FY22–FY24 (PR No. 37/2024).
- 7 Jul 2025: F&O study, FY24–FY25 (top-13 brokers).
- 20 Aug 2026: F&O study, FY25–FY26 (top-15 brokers, ~90% of individual traders; PR No. 50/2026 per press summaries), plus a companion "trading behaviour" study.

## 1. SEBI intraday equity cash-segment study (released 24 July 2024; FY19, FY22, FY23)

### Takeaway
About 7 in 10 individual intraday (MIS-style) cash traders lost money in FY23 (71%, up from 65% in FY19); loss-makers paid trading costs equal to a further 57% of their trading losses, and loss rates rise with trade frequency (80% for >500 trades/year) and are worst for the young (76% for under-30s). Intraday cash has a clearly negative expected outcome for the typical retail trader, though losses are far smaller in rupee terms than in F&O.

### Cited Findings
- Headline: "7 out of 10 individual intraday traders in equity cash segment make losses"; SEBI press release dated 24 Jul 2024 — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/jul-2024/sebi-study-finds-that-7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses_84948.html)
- 71% of individual intraday traders in the equity cash segment incurred a net loss in FY23 — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-says-sebi/422657); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)
- Proportion of loss-makers rose from 65% in FY19 to 71% in FY23 — [Motilal Oswal summary](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2024/7/sebi-study-reveals-key-insights-on-individual-intraday-traders-in-india); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report). (FY22 loss-maker share not found in any accessible source.)
- Costs: over and above trading losses, loss-makers spent an additional 57% of their trading losses on trading costs (brokerage, exchange fees, SEBI fee, stamp duty, GST) in FY23; profit-makers spent 19% of their trading profits on trading costs — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-says-sebi/422657); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report)
- Frequency: loss-makers averaged more trades than profit-makers; the loss-maker share rose to 80% among traders with more than 500 trades a year — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-says-sebi/422657); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report)
- Age: loss-maker share among traders under 30 was 76%; under-20s had the highest at 81%. Share of intraday traders under 30 rose from 18% (FY19) to 48% (FY23) — [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2024/7/sebi-study-reveals-key-insights-on-individual-intraday-traders-in-india); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)
- Experience: traders with 3+ years of intraday experience had a 54% loss-maker share vs 71% overall (as reported by Outlook Money; not cross-checked) — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report)
- Turnover bucket: 76% of traders with annual intraday turnover above ₹1 crore were loss-makers. Average loss for that bucket reported as ₹34,977 by [Business Standard (via search snippet)](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); contradicted by [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report), which gives average loss ₹74,575 and average profit ₹89,172 for the >₹1 crore bucket. Treat bucket averages as unverified.
- Average loss of loss-makers overall in FY23 reported at about ₹5,371 (Business Standard, via search snippet only — not verified against the SEBI text) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)
- Participation: individual intraday traders rose >300% from FY19 to FY23; counts 14.9 lakh (FY19), 78.3 lakh (FY22), 68.9 lakh (FY23); about 1 in 3 equity cash traders trade intraday; top 5.6% of traders generated >90% of intraday turnover — [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-study-finds-that-7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-made-losses); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/most-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-half-of-traders-under-30-years-herere-detailed-findings-from-sebi-report)
- Sample: individual clients of top-10 brokers, ~86% of individual client count in equity cash segment in FY23; study peer-reviewed by a working group of academics, brokers and market experts — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-says-sebi/422657)

### Inferences
- Costs are decisive in intraday cash: costs equal to 57% of trading losses means a large part of the aggregate damage is friction, so any intraday algo must clear a per-trade cost hurdle (brokerage + STT on sell side + exchange + GST + stamp) before it has any edge. Profit-makers still give up ~19% of gross profits to costs.
- The rise in loss rate with frequency (80% at >500 trades/yr) is a direct warning against high-turnover intraday strategies for retail; lower frequency is better.
- The intraday loss rate (~71%) is meaningfully lower than F&O (~88–93%), and rupee losses are much smaller, so intraday cash is "less negative" than F&O but still negative EV for the median participant.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full SEBI intraday PDF; FY22 loss-maker share, gender split, full trade-count buckets and aggregate rupee losses are not verified.
- No SEBI update of the intraday study beyond FY23 was found as of Oct 2026.
- Conflicting average-loss figures for the >₹1 crore turnover bucket (₹34,977 vs ₹74,575).

## 2. SEBI F&O (equity derivatives) studies: Jan 2023 (FY22), Sept 2024 (FY22–FY24), July 2025 (FY25), Aug 2026 (FY25–FY26)

### Takeaway
Across every SEBI study, roughly 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lose money: 89% (FY22), 93% (FY22–FY24 cumulative), 91.1% (FY24), 90.9% (FY25), 87.7% (FY26). Individuals lost ₹3.85 lakh crore net over FY22–FY26, paid ~₹1 lakh crore in transaction costs, and 92% of losses come from options; higher turnover and higher leverage relative to portfolio raise both loss probability and size.

### Cited Findings
**Jan 2023 study (FY22)**
- 89% of individual equity F&O traders incurred losses in FY22, average loss ₹1.1 lakh; loss-makers spent an additional 28% of net trading losses on transaction costs; only 11% made a profit, averaging ₹1.5 lakh; sample = all individual clients of top-10 brokers, 67% of individual F&O turnover — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-study-suggests-89-retail-traders-in-equity-f-o-suffered-losses-123012501466_1.html); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/loss-makers-of-dalal-street-9-out-of-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy22-says-sebi-367628-2023-01-25)

**Sept 2024 study (FY22–FY24), SEBI PR 23 Sep 2024**
- 93% of over 1 crore individual F&O traders incurred losses over FY22–FY24, average ~₹2 lakh per trader including transaction costs; aggregate losses exceeded ₹1.8 lakh crore over three years — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi)
- Top 3.5% of loss-makers (~4 lakh traders) lost an average ₹28 lakh each over FY22–FY24 incl. costs; only 1% of individuals earned profits above ₹1 lakh after costs — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/93-of-1-crore-fo-traders-saw-rs-2l-avg-loss-while-top-3-5-faced-rs-28l-avg-loss-incl-costs-sebi/); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)
- Individuals spent on average ₹26,000 per person on F&O transaction costs in FY24 — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)
- FY24: proprietary traders and FPIs booked gross trading profits of ₹33,000 crore and ₹28,000 crore; 97% of FPI profits and 96% of prop profits came from algorithmic trading — [Benzinga India](https://in.benzinga.com/24/09/40994647/sebi-study-unveils-93-of-individual-f-o-traders-suffered-losses-between-fy22-and-fy24); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)

**July 2025 study (FY25, top-13 brokers, ~96 lakh traders)**
- ~91% of individuals lost money in FY25; aggregate net loss ₹1,05,603 crore vs ₹74,812 crore in FY24 (+41%); average loss ~₹1.1 lakh — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html); [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/225398/average-trader-lost-rs-1-1-lakh-fo-fy25/)
- Note: the Aug 2026 study revised FY25 net loss to ₹1,11,788 crore after expanding the broker sample from 13 to 15 — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study, p.5 and p.46](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)

**Aug 2026 study (FY25–FY26) — most recent** (all from [SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf) unless noted)
- Loss-maker share: 87.7% in FY26 vs 90.9% FY25 and 91.1% FY24; on a gross (pre-cost) basis 82.1% lost in FY26. SEBI cautions the improvement coincided with a 20% fall in active traders.
- Aggregate net losses by year: FY22 ₹40,824 cr; FY23 ₹65,747 cr; FY24 ₹74,812 cr; FY25 ₹1,11,788 cr (revised); FY26 ₹91,685 cr. Cumulative FY22–FY26: ~₹3.85 lakh crore.
- Average net loss per trader ₹1.17 lakh in FY26 (₹1.13 lakh FY25). Average loss of loss-makers ₹1.47 lakh vs average profit of profit-makers ₹1.22 lakh (losses 21% bigger). Average losses exceeded average profits every year FY22–FY26; gap peaked in FY23 (loss 86% larger than profit).
- Options vs futures: options = 92% of FY26 individual losses; FY26 loss-maker share 87.7% in options vs 66.0% in futures. Over FY25–FY26: 91.0% of options traders lost vs 68.4% of futures traders; but average futures loss (₹1.97 lakh) was 29% larger than average options loss (₹1.53 lakh). 99.3% of individuals traded options; 93% traded only options; only 6.6% traded futures (down from 10.6% in FY22).
- Press summaries add: ~97% of individuals were predominantly option buyers, ~2% predominantly option sellers; ~85% of trader-quarters were loss-making — [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/nearly-9-in-10-individual-derivatives-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-losses-hit-rs-91685-crore-despite-fall-in-participation-sebi/)
- Market direction: individuals' futures outcomes tracked Nifty (net-long bias; largest quarterly futures loss ₹8,081 cr in Q4 FY26 when Nifty fell ~15%), while options losses occurred in almost every quarter regardless of market direction, even when Nifty rose ~9% in Q1 FY26.
- Transaction costs: individuals paid ~₹24,800 crore in each of FY25 and FY26 (₹22,451 cr FY24; ₹16,982 cr FY23; ₹9,924 cr FY22); ~₹1 lakh crore cumulative FY22–FY26. Average TC per trader rose from ₹26,027 (FY25) to ₹31,628 (FY26). FY26 split: brokerage 44%, STT 27%, exchange charges 16%, GST 11%, stamp duty ~1%, SEBI fee ~1%. STT paid rose 35% from ₹4,920 cr to ₹6,645 cr after the 1 Oct 2024 STT hike; STT share rose from 13% (FY22) to 27% (FY26).
- TC as share of P&L: in FY26 transaction costs equalled 35% of loss-makers' gross losses vs 21% of profit-makers' gross profits; for loss-makers this ratio was 27% (FY24), 44% (FY25), 35% (FY26); for profit-makers 18–23% over FY24–FY26.
- Costs flip winners to losers: 5.3 lakh traders (FY25) and 4.4 lakh (FY26) who were gross-profitable became net loss-makers after costs (~5–6% of net loss-makers).
- Concentration of losses: ~23% of traders account for ~90% of losses (FY26). Over FY25–FY26, ~4.4 lakh traders (3.5%) lost more than ₹10 lakh each (avg ~₹30 lakh); ~14,000 lost >₹1 crore each (avg ~₹2.2 crore). Only ~1.1% earned profits above ₹1 lakh over FY25–FY26 (unchanged from FY22–FY24).
- Turnover (frequency proxy): large-turnover traders (>₹1 crore/yr) = 20% of traders, 96% of turnover, 82% of net losses, loss-maker share 89.7%; medium (₹1 lakh–1 crore) 87.8%; small (<₹1 lakh) 86.4%. By two-year options turnover, loss share rises from ~86% (₹10k–1 lakh) to ~95% (>₹10 crore).
- Experience: loss incidence was nearly identical for new (87.81%) and regular (87.69%) traders in FY26 (~91% both in FY25), but regular traders lost more on average (₹1,35,716 vs ₹58,620 in FY26) and traded 45.7x their portfolio value vs 19.2x for new traders. Press reports add that among traders who lost two consecutive years and kept trading, ~90% lost again the next year (from the companion "trading behaviour" study; not found in the profitability PDF) — [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/nearly-9-in-10-individual-derivatives-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-losses-hit-rs-91685-crore-despite-fall-in-participation-sebi/)
- Age: under-30s were 43% of individual F&O traders in FY26 (31% in FY22); 89% of under-30s lost vs 81% of over-60s.
- Income: ~75% of traders declared income below ₹5 lakh; that group had 43% of turnover but 53% of losses; 88% loss rate vs 81% for income >₹1 crore (income data available for 55% of sample).
- Equity portfolio link: 35% of FY25–FY26 F&O traders had no equity portfolio; 78% had portfolios below ₹1 lakh and caused 70% of losses with 51% of turnover. Loss rate fell with portfolio size: 89.7% (small) / 82.0% (medium) / 62.9% (large) in FY26; 88% for ₹1–10 lakh portfolios, 72% for ₹50 lakh–1 crore, 58% for >₹10 crore. Traders with portfolios <₹1 lakh and turnover >₹1 crore were 13% of traders but 52% of losses.
- Expiry concentration: 70% of index-option turnover was on expiry day (0DTE) in FY25, moderating to 59% in FY26; 97–98% within 7 days of expiry.
- Participation: active individual F&O traders fell 18% from 106.2 lakh (FY25) to 87.5 lakh (FY26), first decline since FY16; new entrants fell ~40% to 20.8 lakh. (The 15-broker profitability sample shows 98.1 → 78.6 lakh, a ~20% drop; the two counts differ because one is whole-exchange population, the other the broker sample.)
- Global context cited by SEBI: Brazil index-futures day traders persisting >300 days — 97% lost (Chague et al. 2020); Korea KOSPI 200 retail day traders ~75% lost; Taiwan day traders lost avg NT$61,500; ESMA CFD accounts 74–89% lose.

### Inferences
- F&O, and especially short-dated index option buying, is the most negative-EV retail style in India by every measure (loss share, rupee loss, persistence). Options losses are direction-independent, i.e., the losses look structural (premium decay, spreads, costs) rather than bad market calls.
- Futures are less bad on loss incidence (66–68% losers) and track market direction, which suggests a net-long futures position behaves like leveraged beta; the outcome depends on the market, not on skill, and downside losses were larger than upside gains.
- Experience does not lower the probability of loss in F&O, but it raises loss size because experienced traders scale up turnover; "learning by doing" is not supported.
- Traders with large equity portfolios lose less often (58–63% losers), consistent with wealthier/hedging users, but even the best bucket loses money more often than not.

### Gaps
- Sept 2024 study's exact transaction-cost-to-loss ratio (FY22–FY24) not verified from primary text; the FY26 PDF gives FY24 = 27%.
- No breakdown of individual outcomes for option *sellers* vs *buyers* in rupee terms was found in accessible text (only the 97%/2% participation split).

## 3. Algorithmic trading share and profitability (SEBI/NSE)

### Takeaway
F&O profits accrue almost entirely to algorithmic institutions: 99% of FPI and prop gross profits in FY25–FY26 came from algo entities (97% FPI / 96% prop in FY24). Prop traders earned ~₹90,400 crore gross over FY25–FY26 while individuals lost ~₹1.70 lakh crore gross. Individuals using algos still lost in aggregate.

### Cited Findings
- FY26 gross trading profits: prop ~₹44,000 crore (exact ₹44,483 cr), FPIs ~₹14,000 crore (₹13,896 cr), corporates ~₹8,000 cr, MFs ~₹3,000 cr, partnership/LLPs ~₹3,000 cr; individuals' gross loss ~₹72,000 crore (₹72,243 cr), down from ~₹98,000 cr in FY25 — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- FY25–FY26 combined gross P&L: prop ₹90,437 cr, FPI ₹44,980 cr, corporates ₹14,242 cr, MFs ₹8,208 cr, partnership/LLP ₹7,690 cr; individuals −₹1,70,125 cr (the only major loss-making category). SEBI notes derivatives are largely zero-sum pre-costs, so individual losses broadly match institutional profits — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- Algo vs non-algo (FY25–FY26 gross P&L): prop algo ₹89,452 cr vs non-algo ₹985 cr; FPI algo ₹44,689 cr vs non-algo ₹291 cr; individuals algo −₹51,728 cr vs non-algo −₹1,18,396 cr. 99% of FPI and prop gross profit came from algo entities ("at least one algo order in a year"). SEBI flags that individual "algo" counts are inflated because broker auto-square-off (after ~3:20 pm) counts as algo — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- Algo adoption FY26: 372 of 432 FPIs (86%) and 323 of 693 prop traders (47%) traded via algos; only 15% of 84.2 lakh individuals (overstated, as above) — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- Profit concentration FY26: top 10 prop traders (2.3% of the category) earned ₹33,124 cr = 74.5% of prop gross profit; top 5 = 47.6%, top 20 = 82.4%; top 10 prop entities account for ~85% of options and ~65% of futures activity. Top 10 FPIs (1.4%) earned ₹6,852 cr = 49.3% of FPI profit. Top 20 individual clients by turnover posted a small net loss (−₹77 cr) — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- FY24: 97% of FPI profits and 96% of prop profits came from algorithmic trading — [Benzinga India on Sept 2024 SEBI study](https://in.benzinga.com/24/09/40994647/sebi-study-unveils-93-of-individual-f-o-traders-suffered-losses-between-fy22-and-fy24)

### Inferences
- The edge in Indian F&O sits with a handful of large, low-latency, low-cost algorithmic prop/FPI desks (top-10 prop earn ~75%). Retail algos do not share those advantages (co-location, exchange-member cost structure, scale), and individual "algo" users as a group still lost ₹51,728 cr gross over two years. Automation alone does not confer edge.

### Gaps
- No SEBI/NSE data isolating genuine retail API-algo users (excluding broker auto-square-off) and their profitability.
- No NSE-published algo share of turnover number was verified in this pass (widely cited ~50–60% of cash/F&O turnover figures were not sourced here).

## 4. SEBI retail algo trading framework (Feb 2025 circular; in force from 1 Apr 2026)

### Takeaway
SEBI's 4 Feb 2025 circular formally legitimises API-based retail algos but routes them through brokers: static-IP whitelisting, unique algo IDs, daily 2FA/OAuth, and exchange registration only above 10 orders per second per exchange. After deadline extensions, it became mandatory from 1 April 2026. Low-frequency DIY algos (well under 10 OPS) are largely unaffected beyond setup chores; black-box algo sellers must register as research analysts.

### Cited Findings
- SEBI circular 4 Feb 2025; NSE implementation standards 5 May 2025; SEBI 30 Sep 2025 circular set a glide path with mandatory compliance from 1 Apr 2026 — [Fyers notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026/); [Fyers support](https://support.fyers.in/portal/en/kb/articles/what-are-the-new-sebi-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026); [Business Standard on NSE standards](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/nse-lays-down-new-ground-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-to-boost-safety-and-compliance-125050600690_1.html)
- Original timeline in the Feb 2025 circular: operational standards by 1 Apr 2025, implementation from 1 Aug 2025 (later extended) — [Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/sebis-latest-algo-trading-rules)
- Order-per-second threshold: 10 orders per second per exchange; strategies below it need no exchange registration, strategies above must be registered via the broker; exchanges may revise the threshold; brokers may impose stricter client limits — [Fyers support](https://support.fyers.in/portal/en/kb/articles/what-are-the-new-sebi-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026); [Tradejini](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/what-sebis-new-algo-trading-rules-mean-for-you)
- Conflict: some broker notices describe orders above 10 OPS as being rejected, whereas regulatory summaries describe 10 OPS as a registration trigger; one broker quotes 25 OPS for registered algo apps — [Fyers notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026/); [Liquide blog](https://blog.liquide.life/sebi-algo-trading-regulations-2026/)
- Static IP: orders accepted only from a registered App ID mapped to a whitelisted static IP; static IP applies only to order-placement APIs, not market-data APIs; daily 2FA; market orders converted to market-price-protection (MPP) orders (broker implementation) — [Fyers support](https://support.fyers.in/portal/en/kb/articles/what-are-the-new-sebi-rules-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026); [Fyers community post](https://fyers.in/community/blogs-gdppin8d/post/sebi-s-new-algo-trading-rules-kick-in-on-april-1-here-is-what-changes-Yew3vdG4CgoXk1q)
- Algo providers are empanelled by exchanges (SEBI does not regulate them directly), brokers must do due diligence; white-box (transparent logic) algos get faster registration; black-box algo providers must register as SEBI research analysts, keep research reports, and re-register on material changes; brokers must use OAuth-based daily authentication — [Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/sebis-latest-algo-trading-rules)

### Inferences
- For a personal, self-coded, low-frequency algo on one's own account: obtain a static IP (e.g., VPS), register it with the broker, handle daily login/2FA, keep order rate far below 10 OPS, and avoid raw market orders (expect MPP conversion). The framework does not change the economics shown in sections 1–3.

### Gaps
- No post-April 2026 data found on how many retail algos registered or any measured effect on retail outcomes.
- Exact handling above 10 OPS (reject vs register) varies by broker; check the specific broker's documentation.

## 5. Evidence on long-term investing vs trading for Indian retail

### Takeaway
I found no SEBI or NSE study that directly measures returns of Indian retail buy-and-hold investors against traders. The strongest indirect evidence is from the SEBI F&O study: traders with larger equity portfolios lose far less often, losses are concentrated among those with no or tiny equity holdings, and futures (net-long) outcomes simply track the index. Mutual-fund holding periods are lengthening.

### Cited Findings
- 35% of F&O traders in FY25–FY26 had no equity portfolio; 78% had portfolios below ₹1 lakh and accounted for 70% of losses; F&O loss rate falls from ~90% (small portfolios) to 58% (portfolios above ₹10 crore) — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- Individuals as a group made net profits in futures in quarters when Nifty rose and lost when it fell; options lost in nearly every quarter regardless — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- Beyond-top-30-city investors are ~2/3 of F&O traders and ~half of turnover, but only ~1/4 of individual mutual-fund assets — [SEBI FY25–FY26 study](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1787233506209.pdf)
- As of March 2025, 33% of regular-plan and 19% of direct-plan SIP assets were in accounts older than five years, vs 12% and 4% five years earlier — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/mutual-fund/mutual-fund-sip-holding-periods-rise-investors-go-long-term-125051901491_1.html)
- SEBI WTM Amarjeet Singh (Aug 2026) cited a higher share of >5-year SIP assets in regular plans than direct plans, attributing it to distributors as a "behavioural anchor" — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/retail-mutual-fund-assets-stay-invested-beyond-two-years-sebi-wtm-amarjeet-singh)

### Inferences
- Since F&O is roughly zero-sum before costs and intraday cash is heavily cost-taxed, while delivery holding of equities earns the equity risk premium with low turnover cost, the expected-outcome ranking for retail is: delivery/long-horizon investing (positive, market-linked) > low-frequency intraday cash (negative median, smaller losses) > futures (market-direction dependent, leveraged) > short-dated options buying (strongly negative). This ranking is an inference from SEBI cost and outcome data, not a directly measured comparison.

### Gaps
- No NSE Market Pulse article or Indian academic paper quantifying realised retail delivery-investor returns vs traders was located in this pass; long-run Nifty/Sensex total-return figures were not fetched and should be sourced separately.
