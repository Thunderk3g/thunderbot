## 1. Equity ETFs (Nifty 50, Next 50, Midcap 150, momentum): rates, exemption, rebate, losses, and what Budget 2026 changed

### Takeaway
Nothing changed for equity ETFs in Budget 2026: short-term gains (held 12 months or less) are taxed at 20%, long-term gains at 12.5% above an aggregate ₹1.25 lakh per year, each plus 4% cess (20.8% and 13% effective, before surcharge). From 1 April 2026 the same rules sit in sections 196 (old 111A) and 198 (old 112A) of the Income-tax Act 2025, and the section 87A rebate cannot be used against these special-rate gains.

### Cited Findings
- For transfers in FY 2026-27, qualifying short-term gains on equity-oriented units are taxed at 20% under section 111A, renumbered section 196 under the Income-tax Act 2025, plus surcharge and cess. Long-term gains above an aggregate ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 12.5% under section 112A, renumbered section 198. The general section 112 becomes section 197. — FY 2026-27 guides: [Amass Capital](https://amasscapital.in/blogs/mutual-fund-taxation-india-fy-2026-27-equity-debt-sip-redemption/); [MeraSIP](https://www.merasip.com/resources/taxation); [Dhanvantree](https://dhanvantree.com/knowledge-base/income-tax/); [ebizfiling on sections 196 and 198](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/section-196-and-198-short-term-and-long-term-capital-gains/); [Income Tax Dept, section 197](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-197-76)
- Holding period: more than 12 months is long-term; 12 months or less is short-term at a flat 20% regardless of slab. A 4% health and education cess applies on top. — [MeraSIP](https://www.merasip.com/resources/taxation); [Outlook Money, Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/tax-reckoner-2026-27-how-mutual-fund-investments-will-be-taxed-after-budget)
- The ₹1.25 lakh exemption is one aggregate limit per financial year across listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual fund units together, not per fund. — [ICICI Prudential Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf); [Amass Capital](https://amasscapital.in/blogs/mutual-fund-taxation-india-fy-2026-27-equity-debt-sip-redemption/)
- AMC reckoners for 2026-27 state that surcharge on capital gains from mutual fund units is capped at 15%. — [Sundaram MF Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.sundarammutual.com/pdf2/TaxReckoner/Tax_Reckoner_2026_27.pdf); [HSBC MF Tax Reckoner FY 2026-27](https://www.assetmanagement.hsbc.co.in/assets/documents/mutual-funds/en/da5d1d18-343c-41d2-aaf9-e6967d009ee8/tax-reckoner-fy-2026-2027.pdf). A second search of the same PDFs could not find the cap wording, so treat the exact scope as unconfirmed.
- Section 87A rebate: the Finance Act 2025 bars the rebate against special-rate income (section 111A short-term gains, section 112 and 112A long-term gains) from AY 2026-27, that is FY 2025-26 onward. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/section-87a-rebate-stcg-111a-tax-planning-fy-2025-26.html); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/tax/budget-increases-section-87a-rebate-limit-excludes-capital-gains-and-special-income-from-benefits); [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=8484)
- For the earlier year (AY 2025-26, FY 2024-25) an ITAT Delhi ruling allowed the full rebate against section 111A gains, on the reasoning that the bar was introduced only prospectively from AY 2026-27. It does not help for FY 2026-27. — [Upstox News](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/income-tax-dept-denies-section-87-a-rebate-on-20-lakh-stcg-under-the-new-tax-regime-taxpayer-wins-at-itat-know-the-full-case/article-200126/)
- Budget 2026 kept the rebate unchanged: income threshold ₹12 lakh and maximum rebate ₹60,000 under the new regime, with slabs unchanged. — [Business Today, tax slabs FY 2026-27](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/tax-slabs-fy-2026-27-what-budget-2026-changed-for-individual-taxpayers-and-which-regime-works-best-514044-2026-02-01); [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/special-coverage/budget-2026-no-change-in-tax-rates-in-budget-2026-rebate-continues-for-income-up-to-%e2%82%b912-lakh-88610.html)
- Budget 2026 left headline rates alone (12.5% long-term, 20% short-term on equity). Its market-related changes were: STT raised on derivatives only (futures 0.02% to 0.05%, options to 0.15%), with equity delivery and mutual fund STT unchanged; buyback proceeds taxed as capital gains instead of dividends from FY 2026-27; interest on money borrowed to invest no longer deductible against dividend or mutual fund income. — [ClearTax Budget 2026 highlights](https://cleartax.in/s/budget-2026-highlights); [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2026/2/top-budget-2026-announcements-for-the-stock-market); [Tata MF blog](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/post-budget-2026-new-tax-map-equity-investors); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/how-will-budget-2026-announcements-impact-individual-taxpayers-from-now-on/article-188775); [Fyers](https://fyers.in/blog/union-budget-2026-highlights/)
- Losses: a capital loss not absorbed in the year can be carried forward 8 assessment years, only if the return is filed on time. A short-term capital loss can be set off against any capital gain (short- or long-term). A long-term capital loss can be set off only against long-term gains. Capital losses cannot be set off against salary or business income. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/glossary/capital-loss-carryover); [Taxmann FAQs](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-income-tax-returns-itr-set-off-and-carry-forward-of-losses-under-income-tax-act-house-property-capital-loss); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/how-to-set-off-long-term-and-short-term-capital-losses-against-capital-gains-in-fy-2025-26/article-164071)
- Under the Income-tax Act 2025 the carry-forward and set-off of capital losses is in section 111. — [EZTax, section 111](https://eztax.in/income-tax-act-2025/section-111); [Income Tax Dept, section 111](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-111-98)
- Sources disagree on ordering: one view applies the ₹1.25 lakh exemption first and then sets off losses; the more recent commentary sets off losses first and applies the exemption only to the remaining net long-term gain. — [fnotax article](https://fnotax.pages.dev/articles/ltcl-set-off-vs-rs-1-lakh-exemption-fo-tax-audit-rules-ay-2026-27/); contradicted by [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=790375)

### Inferences
- Effective rates with 4% cess and no surcharge (total income under ₹50 lakh): short-term 20% × 1.04 = **20.8%**; long-term 12.5% × 1.04 = **13.0%** on the amount above ₹1.25 lakh.
- Grandfathering (31 January 2018 fair value) is irrelevant for units bought now.
- A monthly-rotation strategy will almost never use the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, because it applies only to long-term gains.
- The earlier project note's capital-gains section is still current for FY 2026-27. One item in it needs correcting for ETFs: it models STT at 0.1% on both legs, which is right for shares but 200 times too high for equity ETF units (see section 7).

### Gaps
- Not sourced this session (general knowledge, verify before relying): the "equity-oriented fund" test (at least 65% in listed domestic equity; for a fund of funds, at least 90% in such ETF units); the surcharge thresholds (10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore); the rule that a resident whose other income is below the basic exemption limit can use the unused limit against these gains; the new-Act section number for the rebate (I believe section 156) and for the new regime (I believe section 202).
- I did not read the Finance Act 2026 or the 2025 Act. The section mapping comes from secondary guides and AMC reckoners that agree with each other.

## 2. Gold and silver ETFs: post-July-2024 regime and the transitional rules

### Takeaway
For any sale in FY 2026-27, a listed gold or silver ETF is long-term after more than 12 months and taxed at 12.5% without indexation and without the ₹1.25 lakh exemption; a sale within 12 months is short-term at slab rate. Budget 2026 did not change this. The only real disagreement among sources is about units bought between 1 April 2023 and 31 March 2025.

### Cited Findings
- CBDT's FAQ of 24 July 2024 says the holding period for all listed assets is now one year, and for gold and unlisted securities (other than unlisted shares) it falls from 36 to 24 months. — [PIB, CBDT FAQs](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2036604&reg=48&lang=2); [Freefincal summary](https://freefincal.com/budget-2024-faq-on-capital-gains-taxation/)
- Gold ETFs are listed, so they take the 12-month period; physical gold takes 24 months; gold fund-of-funds are unlisted and take 24 months. — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/taxation-of-gold-in-india-explained); [Appreciate](https://appreciatewealth.com/blog/how-are-gold-etfs-taxed-in-india)
- Long-term gain on gold ETFs is 12.5% with no indexation; short-term gain is taxed at slab rate. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-etf-tax-in-india-2026-post-finance-act-2024-rules); [Aditya Birla Capital](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/tax-on-gold-etf)
- The amended definition of "specified mutual fund" in section 50AA covers (a) a fund investing more than 65% of total proceeds in debt and money market instruments, or (b) a fund investing 65% or more in units of such a fund. The earlier test was a fund with not more than 35% in domestic equity, which had caught gold ETFs. — [AMFI tax regime page](https://www.amfiindia.com/investor/knowledge-center-info?zoneName=TaxRegimeForMutualFunds); [TaxTMI note](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=1216)
- The official section text marks the substitution as made by the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 "w.e.f. 1-4-2026". Most secondary sources say "from 1 April 2025". — [Income Tax Dept, section 50AA](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-50aa-3); [TaxTMI note](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=1216)
- Units bought between 1 April 2023 and 31 March 2025 and sold before 1 April 2025 were short-term at slab rate whatever the holding period. This is undisputed. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-etf-tax-in-india-2026-post-finance-act-2024-rules); [HDFC MF leaflet, August 2024](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2024-08/Gold%20and%20Silver%20simplified%20taxation%20Leaflet%20(August%202024).pdf)
- **Conflict on the same units sold on or after 1 April 2025.** One reading: the sale date decides, so units bought in 2024 and sold in 2026 get the 12-month, 12.5% treatment. — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/gold-etf-tax-in-india-2026-post-finance-act-2024-rules). The other reading: units bought in that window stay slab-taxed however long they are held. — [TMGM](https://www.tmgm.com/en-in/academy/trading-academy/gold-ETF-taxation). A third source says units bought between 23 July 2024 and 31 March 2025 needed 24 months. — [Appreciate](https://appreciatewealth.com/blog/how-are-gold-etfs-taxed-in-india)
- Budget 2026 made no change to gold or silver ETF rates or holding periods. It did restrict the Sovereign Gold Bond redemption exemption to individuals who subscribed at original issue and hold to maturity, effective 1 April 2026. — [BasuNivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/taxation-of-gold-and-silver-in-india-in-2026-explained-simply/); [Arihant Plus](https://www.arihantplus.com/blogs/gold/gold-silver-tax-rules-after-budget-2026/); [Outlook Money on SGBs](https://www.outlookmoney.com/retirement/plan/tax/budget-2026-tax-exemption-on-sovereign-gold-bonds-only-for-individual-investors)
- STT does not apply to gold ETFs. — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/stt-etfs)

### Inferences
- The "1-4-2026" on the official text and the "1 April 2025" in commentary are the same thing: amendments to the 1961 Act are dated by assessment year, so "w.e.f. 1-4-2026" means AY 2026-27, which is income of FY 2025-26, that is transfers from 1 April 2025.
- That supports the sale-date reading: section 50AA is tested when the unit is transferred, and for transfers from 1 April 2025 a gold ETF no longer meets the definition. The sources taking the purchase-date view are trading-education blogs that cite no provision. For this project the dispute is moot: units bought now and sold in FY 2026-27 are outside it on every reading.
- The 12.5% long-term rate is under old section 112, new section 197. The ₹1.25 lakh exemption belongs only to old section 112A (new 198), so it does not apply. Effective long-term rate with cess: 13.0%. The section 87A rebate is not available against it (section 1).
- Short-term gold ETF gains are ordinary slab-rate income: 31.2% at the 30% slab, against 20.8% for an equity ETF.

### Gaps
- I could not open the ICICI, Mirae or Tata 2026-27 reckoner PDFs. The search extract of the ICICI reckoner shows 12.5% for listed gold, silver and international ETFs with holding periods "that depend on the purchase date", but the table was garbled, so its exact transitional rows are unconfirmed.
- No CBDT circular addressing the transitional units was found.

## 3. Liquid, gilt, debt and Bharat Bond ETFs; LIQUIDBEES dividends; after-tax yield

### Takeaway
A fund with more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments is a "specified mutual fund": for units acquired on or after 1 April 2023, every gain is deemed short-term and taxed at slab rate whatever the holding period, with no exemption. Daily-dividend liquid ETFs such as LIQUIDBEES are taxed at slab rate on each dividend, with 10% TDS once mutual fund income from that fund exceeds ₹10,000 in the year.

### Cited Findings
- Specified mutual funds are always taxed as short-term gains; gains on units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are short-term at slab rate. — [Outlook Money, Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/tax-reckoner-2026-27-how-mutual-fund-investments-will-be-taxed-after-budget); [Dhanvantree](https://dhanvantree.com/knowledge-base/income-tax/); [Nippon India Tax Reckoner FY 2025-26](https://mf.nipponindiaim.com/LearnAndInvest/TaxRateDocuments/Tax-Reckoner-for-FY-2025-26.pdf) (prior year)
- Under the Income-tax Act 2025 the provision is section 76; the ICICI reckoner cites section 76(5)(b) for the "more than 65%" definition. — [ICICI Prudential Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf); [SBI MF Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.sbimf.com/docs/default-source/pdf/sbi-mf-tax-reckoner-fy-2025-26.pdf?sfvrsn=f6bf4c79_8)
- One reckoner extract appeared to show 12.5% in the short-term row for specified funds. It conflicts with every other source and is probably a garbled table. — [Tata MF Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/system/files/2026-02/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf)
- Liquid ETFs and Liquid BeES may distribute income as dividend units instead of cash; those units are taxable as dividend income at slab rate and must be reported. — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees); [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2023/7/how-are-dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees-taxed)
- When dividend units are later sold, the cost of acquisition is the value taxed as dividend at allotment. — [TradingQnA thread](https://tradingqna.com/t/how-does-taxation-work-on-liquid-bees-since-all-gains-are-in-terms-of-dividends-and-price-remains-the-same/8526)
- TDS on mutual fund income (old section 194K) is 10%, and Budget 2025 raised the threshold from ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 a year from 1 April 2025. Under the 2025 Act it is section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 4(i). Without PAN the rate is 20%. Redemptions and capital gains are not subject to this TDS. — [TDSMAN](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/05/tds-on-income-in-respect-of-units-of-a-specified-mutual-fund-section-3931-194k/); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/budget-2025-tds-limit-on-dividend-income-from-mutual-funds-increased-to-10000/486288); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/tax-reckoner-2026-27-how-mutual-fund-investments-will-be-taxed-after-budget)
- Older pages still quote the ₹5,000 threshold; they are out of date. — [Motilal Oswal, 2023](https://www.motilaloswal.com/learning-centre/2023/7/how-are-dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees-taxed)
- Forum users report brokers showing dividend units as short-term equity gains, which can cause double counting in tax reports. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/clarification-needed-on-liquidbees-taxation/150823/14)
- STT does not apply to liquid and gilt ETFs. — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/stt-etfs)
- The section 87A rebate reduces tax on slab-rate income only; tax under sections 111A, 112 and 112A is never reduced by it. — [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=8484); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/tax/budget-increases-section-87a-rebate-limit-excludes-capital-gains-and-special-income-from-benefits)

### Inferences
- **Does the rebate cover debt-fund gains? Yes, by inference.** Gains from a specified mutual fund are taxed at normal slab rates, not at a special rate, so they are the kind of income the rebate can reduce. A resident in the new regime with total income up to ₹12 lakh would pay nil tax on them. I found no source stating this in terms for post-April-2023 debt funds, so it is a reasoned reading, not a cited rule.
- Unresolved detail on the ₹12 lakh test: explainers say special-rate income is left out when testing the limit ([Greater Kashmir explainer](https://m.greaterkashmir.com/article/explainer-tax-free-limit-of-rs-12-lakh-excludes-special-income/424363/amp)). My understanding of the statute is that total income including capital gains must be within ₹12 lakh, with the rebate capped at the slab-rate tax. Check before relying on either.
- **After-tax yield, illustrative 6.0% gross** (the 6% is my assumption, not a sourced current yield; cess 4%, no surcharge):

  | Marginal slab | Effective tax | After-tax yield |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rebate applies (new regime, total income ≤ ₹12 lakh) | 0% | 6.00% |
  | 5% | 5.2% | 5.69% |
  | 10% | 10.4% | 5.38% |
  | 15% | 15.6% | 5.06% |
  | 20% | 20.8% | 4.75% |
  | 30% | 31.2% | 4.13% |

- Daily-dividend versus growth: with LIQUIDBEES the tax arises every day a dividend is declared, and each dividend allotment is a separate cost lot. With a growth-type liquid ETF nothing is taxed until sale, when the whole gain is short-term at slab rate. The rate is the same; the growth type defers tax and avoids the dividend TDS and the per-lot bookkeeping.
- Bharat Bond and gilt ETFs follow the same rule as liquid ETFs for units bought now. Being listed does not give them the 12-month long-term treatment, because the specified-mutual-fund rule overrides the holding period.

### Gaps
- The search for growth-type liquid ETFs (LIQUIDCASE and similar) did not run, so their treatment above is derived from the general rule, not from an AMC or broker source.
- Not found: how the AMC actually deducts TDS on LIQUIDBEES dividend units, how fractional units are settled, and the current dividend-credit frequency.
- No current liquid ETF yield was sourced.

## 4. India-listed international ETFs (Nasdaq 100, S&P 500, FANG+) and fund-of-funds

### Takeaway
An India-listed international ETF is a non-equity fund: no ₹1.25 lakh exemption and no STT. The better-supported position is long-term after more than 12 months at 12.5% (because the units are listed) and slab rate before that; unlisted international fund-of-funds need more than 24 months. Several distributor blogs state 24 months for the ETFs too, so sources conflict.

### Cited Findings
- CBDT FAQ: the holding period of all listed assets is one year; unlisted non-share securities are 24 months. A summary of the FAQ lists all ETFs as listed assets under the one-year rule. — [PIB, CBDT FAQs](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2036604&reg=48&lang=2); [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/budget-2024-faq-on-capital-gains-taxation/)
- The ICICI 2026-27 reckoner extract shows 12.5% long-term for listed gold, silver and international ETFs held beyond 12 months. — [ICICI Prudential Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf) (garbled extract)
- ETF units trade on an exchange and take 12 months; fund-of-fund units take 24 months. — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/taxation-of-gold-in-india-explained)
- **Conflict:** one broker article puts India-listed international ETFs at 12.5% only after two years, with slab rate under two years. — [m.Stock](https://www.mstock.com/articles/how-are-etfs-taxed-in-india); similar framing in [Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/stocks/international-etfs-india/)
- International fund-of-funds, previously taxed as debt funds at marginal rate, are now taxed at 12.5% if sold after 24 months. — [ProfitGuru](https://www.profitguruonline.com/learn/taxation/tax-on-international-fofs); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/indian-amcs-reopen-subscriptions-for-overseas-exchange-traded-funds-fund-of-funds-ahead-of-new-tax-rules-from-april-1--news-274053)
- Budget 2026 made no major direct change to international fund taxation. — [Share.Market](https://www.share.market/buzz/stocks/international-etfs-india/)
- STT does not apply to some international ETFs (the Nasdaq 100 ETF is named). — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/stt-etfs); [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/whats-the-rate-of-stt-applicable-for-etfs/53648)

### Inferences
- These funds hold foreign shares, so they are neither equity-oriented (which needs domestic listed equity) nor specified mutual funds (which need more than 65% debt). They fall in the residual "other funds" bucket: listed units 12 months, unlisted units 24 months, long-term at 12.5% under old section 112 (new 197), short-term at slab.
- The 24-month statements for ETFs look like the fund-of-fund rule applied loosely to ETFs. The CBDT FAQ and the AMC reckoner are the stronger sources.
- For a monthly-rotation strategy the dispute does not matter: every exit is short-term on either reading, taxed at slab rate (31.2% at the 30% slab).

### Gaps
- The search aimed at Mirae and Motilal Oswal's own tax notes for their international ETFs did not run, so there is no AMC statement specific to MAFANG, MON100 or MASPTOP50.
- Premiums to NAV on international ETFs caused by overseas-investment limits were not researched.

## 5. Summary table by ETF category (resident individual, units bought now, sold in FY 2026-27)

### Takeaway
Only equity ETFs get the flat 20% short-term rate and the ₹1.25 lakh exemption. Gold, silver and international ETFs are slab-taxed short-term and 12.5% long-term with no exemption. Debt and liquid ETFs are slab-taxed always.

### Cited Findings
- The table rows are drawn from the cited findings in sections 1 to 4 and section 7; the principal sources are the [CBDT FAQs](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2036604&reg=48&lang=2), the [ICICI Prudential Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf), the [Outlook Money reckoner summary](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/tax-reckoner-2026-27-how-mutual-fund-investments-will-be-taxed-after-budget) and [Zerodha Support on ETF STT](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/stt-etfs).

| Category | Long-term after | Short-term rate | Long-term rate | ₹1.25 lakh exemption | STT on delivery sale | Section (1961 Act → 2025 Act) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equity ETF (Nifty 50, Next 50, Midcap 150, momentum) | more than 12 months | 20% (20.8% with cess) | 12.5% (13.0% with cess) | Yes, shared with all listed equity and equity funds | 0.001% on sell; nil on buy | 111A → 196; 112A → 198 | No 87A rebate on these gains |
| Gold ETF, silver ETF (listed) | more than 12 months | Slab (up to 31.2% with cess at 30%) | 12.5% (13.0%), no indexation | No | Nil | 112 → 197 | Conflict only for units bought Apr 2023 to Mar 2025 |
| International ETF (listed) | more than 12 months (some sources say 24) | Slab | 12.5% (13.0%), no indexation | No | Nil | 112 → 197 | Not equity-oriented, not a specified fund |

### Inferences
- Cess of 4% applies to every row. Surcharge applies only above ₹50 lakh total income; reckoners say it is capped at 15% on capital gains from fund units.
- Slab-rate rows can benefit from the section 87A rebate where total income is within ₹12 lakh under the new regime; the 20% and 12.5% rows cannot.
- New-regime slab rates with cess, for the slab rows: 5.2%, 10.4%, 15.6%, 20.8%, 26.0%, 31.2%. The slab boundaries themselves were not sourced this session.

### Gaps

## 6. How a monthly-rebalanced rotation strategy is taxed in practice

### Takeaway
Nearly every exit is short-term, so the asset class of the ETF decides the rate: 20.8% on equity ETF gains but up to 31.2% on gold and international ETF gains for a 30%-slab investor. Short-term losses from any ETF can offset gains from any other, and unused losses carry forward 8 years. The CBDT circular that protects capital-gains treatment covers holdings over 12 months, so it does not strictly cover a monthly rotation.

### Cited Findings
- CBDT Circular 6/2016 (29 February 2016) was issued to reduce litigation over whether income from shares and securities is capital gains or business income. If the taxpayer opts to treat holdings as stock-in-trade, the income is business income whatever the holding period. For listed shares and securities held more than 12 months, if the taxpayer wants capital-gains treatment the Assessing Officer should not dispute it, and the stand must then be followed consistently in later years. — [Circular text (Finodha copy)](https://finodha.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/CBDT-Circular-No.-62016.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/income-tax/497496/clarification-by-cbdt-on-taxability-of-income-arising-out-of-transfer-of-shares); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-clarification-by-cbdt-on-taxability-of/)
- Commentators advise frequent traders to document their policy and apply it consistently. — [EZTax](https://eztax.in/questions/capital-gains-vs-business-income-frequent-traders)
- Short-term capital losses set off against both short- and long-term gains; long-term losses only against long-term gains; 8-year carry-forward requires timely filing; no set-off against salary or business income. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/glossary/capital-loss-carryover); [Taxmann FAQs](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-income-tax-returns-itr-set-off-and-carry-forward-of-losses-under-income-tax-act-house-property-capital-loss)
- The rebate cannot shelter the 20% equity short-term gains. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/section-87a-rebate-stcg-111a-tax-planning-fy-2025-26.html)

### Inferences
- The safe-harbour limb of Circular 6/2016 is for holdings over 12 months. For shorter holdings the question stays fact-based (intent, frequency, volume, own versus borrowed funds). A delivery-only rotation of a few ETFs once a month with own money is commonly reported as capital gains, and the risk of reclassification looks low, but the circular does not guarantee it.
- If the income were treated as business income, all gains would be slab-taxed (worse for equity ETFs at the 30% slab, neutral for gold and international ETFs), while expenses including STT would become deductible.
- Tax drag is asymmetric across sleeves. A ₹10,000 short-term gain costs ₹2,080 in an equity ETF and ₹3,120 in a gold or international ETF at the 30% slab. A backtester should apply the rate by instrument category, not one blended rate.
- A short-term loss is worth more when used against slab-taxed gains (31.2%) than against equity short-term gains (20.8%). How the return utility orders the set-off was not verified.
- Loss harvesting: selling a losing ETF before 31 March and re-entering realises the loss for set-off. Costs at Groww for the round trip are in section 7.
- Costs other than STT (brokerage, exchange fees, stamp duty, DP charges, GST on them) reduce the capital gain; STT does not. This is carried over from the earlier note and was not re-sourced.

### Gaps
- The FIFO rule for demat holdings was not sourced: that search did not run. My understanding, unverified this session, is that CBDT Circular 768 of 24 June 1998 and section 45(2A) of the 1961 Act require first-in-first-out matching within each demat account, so partial exits always sell the oldest lot. No URL captured.
- India has no wash-sale rule as far as I know; not sourced.
- No source specific to algorithmic or bot-driven trading and the business-income question was found.

## 7. Groww charges on an ETF delivery buy and sell, with worked all-in costs

### Takeaway
Groww bills ETFs like shares: brokerage of 0.1% of order value, minimum ₹5, maximum ₹20 per order, and a DP charge of ₹20 plus GST (₹23.60) per ETF per sell day. The big difference from shares is STT: 0.001% on the sell side only for equity ETFs and nil for gold, liquid, gilt and some international ETFs. A round trip costs about ₹49 on ₹10,000 (0.49%), ₹94 on ₹1 lakh (0.09%) and ₹188 on ₹5 lakh (0.04%); I found no liquid-ETF brokerage or DP waiver at Groww.

### Cited Findings
