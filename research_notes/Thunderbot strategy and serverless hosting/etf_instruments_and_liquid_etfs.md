## Nifty 50 ETFs: which are cheapest and most liquid?

### Takeaway
NIFTYBEES (Nippon) and SETFNIF50 (SBI) are both reported at 0.04% TER; SBI's is by far the largest by AUM (about Rs 2.1 lakh crore, mostly institutional money) while NIFTYBEES has the best evidence of retail trading volume. For a Rs 1-20 lakh retail account NIFTYBEES is the default choice on liquidity; the other large ETFs are within about 1 basis point on cost.

## Overseas ETFs on NSE: RBI/SEBI limits, halts, status in late 2026, and the premium problem

### Takeaway
Overseas ETFs on NSE are unusable for a rules-based bot in late 2026: unit creation is frozen by the industry caps, so prices have detached from NAV, with exchange warnings in September-October 2026 and reported premiums ranging from about 17-20% (MON100, MAFANG, MASPTOP50 in mid-2026) to over 100-235% (Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50). The premium can collapse overnight if limits are raised, and SEBI's planned move to a NAV-based price band from 1 April 2027 is a second known trigger.

### Inferences
- The blow-out in September 2026 coincides with the new ETF price-band framework reported effective 1 Sep 2026, under which the band is anchored to the ETF's own previous closing price rather than to NAV (see "Practical traps"). Under the old rule the band was plus or minus 20% around NAV, which capped the premium near 20%; Mirae's phrase "can exceed the earlier 20% level" fits this. A price rising from Rs 142 to Rs 396 in ten sessions is roughly a 10% upper circuit every day. This is my reading of the timing, not something a source states.
- Price history of MON100, MAFANG and MASPTOP50 on Yahoo therefore includes a premium component that is unrelated to the index. A backtest on those prices overstates returns in 2024-2026 and a live bot buying them takes on premium-collapse risk the backtest cannot see. For backtesting US exposure, use NAV (AMFI) rather than price.
- For Rs 1-20 lakh on Groww there is no clean NSE-listed route to US equities in November 2026. The honest options are to drop the overseas sleeve or model it but not trade it.

## Liquid ETFs in depth (two kinds, per-fund data, costs, tax, bot suitability)

### Takeaway
Use a growth-NAV liquid ETF, not LIQUIDBEES, as the bot's cash leg. LIQUIDCASE (Zerodha) is about as large as LIQUIDBEES now (roughly Rs 11,000-11,800 crore each in Nov 2026) at about a third of the cost (0.23-0.28% against about 0.6-0.7%), its price is a clean total-return series (Rs 100 at launch, Rs 116.73 on 4 Nov 2026), and it has no dividend units, no fractions and no dividend TDS. - **LIQUIDCASE realised yield.** If the unit started at Rs 100 in mid-January 2024 (launch date is recollection, not sourced), Rs 116.73 on 4 Nov 2026 is about 5.7% a year net over 2.8 years.
- **TER drag.** LIQUIDBEES at about 0.6-0.7% against LIQUIDCASE at 0.23-0.28% is a 0.35-0.45 percentage point yearly handicap on the same overnight rate, consistent with LIQUIDBEES's 4.81% 5-year figure.
- **Tick size arithmetic** (assuming the usual Rs 0.01 tick). One paisa is 0.1 bp on a Rs 1,000 unit and about 0.86 bp on a Rs 116.73 unit. At a 5.5% yield one day's accrual is about 1.5 bps, which is about 1.8 paise a day on LIQUIDCASE: the price should step up 1-2 paise a day and about 5 paise over a weekend. A one-tick spread on LIQUIDCASE therefore costs roughly half a day of yield; on a Rs 1,000 unit it is negligible.
- **Round-trip cost on Groww in days of yield** (brokerage only, both legs, before GST and exchange/DP charges, at 5.5% yield = about 1.5 bps a day): Rs 10,000 position pays 0.1% each way = 20 bps = about 13 days; Rs 1 lakh pays Rs 40 = 4 bps = about 3 days; Rs 5 lakh pays 0.8 bp = about half a day; Rs 20 lakh pays 0.2 bp. Add 18% GST on brokerage, a DP charge on each sell, stamp duty on buys and one tick of spread. For a monthly rotator holding cash for a month or more this is small above about Rs 1 lakh; for sub-Rs 25,000 sleeves or holds of a few days it eats most of the yield.
- **Why growth type suits a bot and a backtest.** Price history is itself the total-return series; position size is a whole number of units that never changes between trades; there are no dividend-unit credits to reconcile, no fractions to redeem off-market, and no dividend entries or TDS. For LIQUIDBEES the traded price is flat at about Rs 1,000, so a price-only backtest shows zero return and a live bot's holdings drift as units are credited.
- Aggregator "growth"/"IDCW" labels are not reliable enough to pick a fund: HDFC's and Kotak's ETFs predate LIQUIDCASE yet Zerodha's is described as the first growth one, and DSP's LIQUIDETF is described both ways. Confirm from each scheme's SID or from whether its NAV sits at 1,000.000 or climbs.

## Practical traps: premium/discount, thin volume, tracking, unit splits and Yahoo data

### Takeaway
Two things changed in 2026 that a bot must be built around: SEBI's new ETF price-band framework (reported effective 1 Sep 2026) anchors the band and "closing price" to the last 30 minutes' VWAP rather than to NAV, and exchanges are openly warning that some ETFs trade far above NAV. Separately, the December 2019 Nippon unit splits (NIFTYBEES 10:1, GOLDBEES 100:1, record date 20 Dec 2019, ex-date 19 Dec 2019) are confirmed and will corrupt any unadjusted price series.

### Cited Findings
**New price-band and closing-price framework**
- SEBI circular dated **15 Jun 2026**: ETF base price moves from T-2 NAV to the ETF's own T-1 closing price, defined as the last 30 minutes' volume-weighted average price; if no trade in the last 30 minutes, the day's last traded price; if no trade all day, the latest closing NAV — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-revises-etf-price-band-and-pre-open-auction-framework/); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/sebi-etf-trading-norms-dynamic-price-bands/); [NSE circular CMTR76101](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR76101.pdf)
- The fixed plus or minus 20% band is replaced by dynamic bands, with an initial band of plus or minus 10% of base price reported for ETF units; a pre-open call auction is introduced — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/sebi-dynamic-price-bands-etfs); [Bajaj Broking](https://www.bajajbroking.in/share-market-news/sebi-introduces-dynamic-price-bands-for-etfs-from-sept-2026)

### Inferences
- **Decision at 15:10, trade before 15:30.** Under the new framework the ETF's official close is the 15:00-15:30 VWAP, so a bot trading in that window transacts close to the price that will be recorded as the close, which keeps backtest (close-to-close) and live fills aligned. The cost is that this is also when any premium is baked in; the bot should compare its limit price with iNAV and skip or defer if the gap exceeds a threshold (for example 0.5% for domestic equity/gold, tighter for liquid).
- Because the band now follows the ETF's own price, nothing mechanical stops a premium from compounding at up to about 10% a day (the Nasdaq Q50 case). A hard rule to refuse any buy more than X% above iNAV is now a safety requirement, not a refinement.
- When the base price moves to T-1 NAV on 1 April 2027 (if it does), ETFs at large premiums could gap down to within the band of NAV; a bot holding one would take that loss in a day.
- **Detecting split damage:** flag any one-day price change beyond about minus 50% or plus 100% with no matching index move; expected signatures are a drop to one-tenth (NIFTYBEES and, likely, other Nippon equity ETFs on 19 Dec 2019) or one-hundredth (GOLDBEES). Repair by dividing pre-split prices by the ratio, or avoid the issue by building the backtest on AMFI NAV history, cross-checked against NSE bhavcopy closes.
- Ticker renames (ICICI, HDFC, Kotak, Mirae, Motilal) are a second source of broken history: the new symbol's series may start at the rename date.
