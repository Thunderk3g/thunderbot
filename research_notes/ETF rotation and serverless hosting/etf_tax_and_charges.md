## 1. Tax treatment by ETF category for units sold in FY 2026-27

### Takeaway
For sales in FY 2026-27 the working matrix is: equity ETFs 20% short-term / 12.5% long-term above ₹1.25 lakh after 12 months; gold, silver and India-listed international ETFs slab-rate short-term / 12.5% flat long-term after 12 months with no ₹1.25 lakh exemption; liquid, gilt and other debt ETFs always slab rate when bought on or after 1 April 2023. Gold, silver and international ETFs are **not** "specified mutual funds" for any sale on or after 1 April 2025, because the definition now covers only funds with more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments; secondary sources that say otherwise are using the pre-amendment 35%-equity test.

Summary matrix (sale in FY 2026-27; resident individual; rates before 4% cess and surcharge):

| | Equity ETF (≥65% domestic equity) | Gold / silver ETF | India-listed international ETF | Liquid / gilt / debt ETF |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long-term after | > 12 months | > 12 months | > 12 months | Never, if bought on/after 1 Apr 2023 |
| Short-term rate | 20% | Slab | Slab | Slab |
| Long-term rate | 12.5% | 12.5% | 12.5% | n/a (slab) |
| Indexation | No | No | No | No |
| ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption | Yes | No | No | No |
| STT on delivery sell | 0.001% | Nil | Nil ("some international ETFs" per Zerodha) | Nil |
| Section, 1961 Act | 111A (ST), 112A (LT) | 112 (LT); slab (ST) | 112 (LT); slab (ST) | 50AA |
| Section, 2025 Act | 196 (ST), 198 (LT) | 197 (LT); slab (ST) | 197 (LT); slab (ST) | 76 |
| Purchase date matters? | No | No, for sales from 1 Apr 2025 | No, for sales from 1 Apr 2025 | Yes: 1 Apr 2023 cutoff |

### Inferences
- **Why sources disagree on "1 April 2025" vs "1 April 2026" vs "AY 2026-27":** the 1961 Act dates amendments by assessment year. "w.e.f. 1-4-2026" means assessment year 2026-27, which is income of FY 2025-26, so the new definition governs transfers from 1 April 2025. Freefincal's expert quote and the Income Tax Department's footnote are the same date expressed two ways.
- **Which reading is authoritative for units bought 1 April 2023 – 31 March 2025 and sold later:** the sale-date reading. Section 50AA (now 76) bites only on "a unit of a Specified Mutual Fund acquired on or after 1 April 2023". A gold, silver or overseas-equity ETF holds almost no debt, so for any transfer in FY 2025-26 or later it is not a specified mutual fund, whatever the purchase date. The purchase-date framing in Arihant and Aditya Birla gives the same answer for new purchases but is wrong for old lots. I rank the statute definition and the AMC reckoners above broker blogs; I could not open either to quote the exact row.
- For a bot starting in 2026, every lot is bought after 1 April 2025, so both framings give the same result and the conflict is moot in practice.
- The ₹1.25 lakh exemption belongs only to section 112A / 198 (STT-paid equity shares and equity-oriented fund units). Gold, silver and international ETF long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% from the first rupee under section 112 / 197.
- Silver ETFs are not named in Zerodha's STT exemption list. They are not equity-oriented funds, so by the same logic STT should be nil; treat as inferred.
- Rate comparison for a rotation bot holding under 12 months (30% slab investor, before cess): equity ETF gain 20%; gold, international and liquid ETF gain 30%. For a 5–15% slab investor the order reverses: slab-rate assets are taxed lower than the flat 20% on equity ETFs.
- Holding period is counted lot by lot. Zerodha's reports use FIFO, so a partial sell consumes the oldest lot first.
- Effective rates with 4% cess and no surcharge: equity STCG 20.8%; all 12.5% LTCG 13.0%; slab-rate gains 5.2% / 10.4% / 15.6% / 20.8% / 26.0% / 31.2% across the six new-regime bands.

The 196 / 197 / 198 mapping rests on search excerpts, and one excerpt was ambiguous about which of 197 and 198 is the STT-paid equity section. The mapping used here (112 → 197, 112A → 198) is supported by two excerpts and should be checked against the Act.

## 2. Did Budget 2025 or Budget 2026 change capital-gains rates, holding periods, the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, the rebate, surcharge or cess?

### Takeaway
No change to any capital-gains rate, holding period or the ₹1.25 lakh exemption in either Budget; the last change was 23 July 2024. Budget 2025 changed the slabs and rebate and, from FY 2025-26, confined the new-regime rebate to slab-rate tax, so it cannot reduce tax on 111A / 112 / 112A (196 / 197 / 198) gains. Budget 2026 left all of that alone and added three investor items: buybacks taxed as capital gains, higher F&O STT, and removal of the interest deduction against dividend and mutual-fund income.

- Budget 2026 made no changes to capital-gains tax rates. — [equitiesindia](https://equitiesindia.com/learn/taxation/budget-2026-impact-investors) (search excerpt); earlier note citing [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/what-is-ltcg-tax-rate) and [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/news/taxation/union-budget-2026-ltcg-tax-unchanged-at-12-5-stt-on-commodity-futures-hiked)

## 3. Loss set-off, carry-forward, wash sales and harvesting

### Takeaway
Short-term capital loss offsets any capital gain; long-term capital loss offsets only long-term gains; unabsorbed losses carry forward eight tax years (sections 108 and 111 of the 2025 Act). India has no wash-sale rule, so harvesting is allowed, but Zerodha warns that an immediate repurchase "could be questioned" in scrutiny. The ₹1.25 lakh gain-harvesting allowance applies only to equity ETFs.

### Cited Findings
- Section 108 (2025 Act): a short-term capital loss can be set off against any other capital gain of the year; a long-term capital loss only against long-term capital gains. Section 111 covers carry-forward. — [Income Tax Department: section 108](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-108-103); [section 111](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-111-102) (search excerpts)
- No loss can be carried forward for more than eight tax years after the year it was first computed. — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/?p=27898) (search excerpt)
- An earlier draft of the 2025 Act had a one-time provision letting brought-forward long-term losses offset short-term gains; the final Act dropped it. — [caalley](https://caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/one-time-capital-gains-set-off-dropped-in-latest-version-of-income-tax-act-2025-how-it-impacts-your-tax-bill) (search excerpt)
- "India has no explicit regulation that disallows tax loss harvesting"; the US 30-day wash-sale rule has no Indian equivalent; but if you "immediately repurchase the same stock to save on taxes", tax authorities "could question this during tax scrutiny". — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/what-is-tax-loss-harvesting) (fetched)
- "The first ₹1,25,000 of long-term gains is tax-free each year"; Console computes on FIFO, so selling a holding to book a short-term loss also books any long-term gain on older lots of the same scrip; "no tax loss harvesting opportunity in the case of synchronised trades". — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/what-is-tax-loss-harvesting) (fetched)
- The Income Tax Act has no provision disallowing a capital loss because the same security is repurchased within any time frame; a repurchase starts a fresh 12-month clock. — [Kotak Securities](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/share-market/what-is-wash-sale-rule/) (search excerpt)
- Anti-abuse limits cited by commentators: GAAR, and section 94(7) / 94(8) (dividend and bonus stripping). — [equitiesindia](https://equitiesindia.com/learn/taxation/tax-harvesting-stocks); [incorpx](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/tax-loss-harvesting-india-strategy-investors) (search excerpts)

### Inferences
- All four ETF categories produce the same two loss types, so they mix freely:
  - A loss on a liquid ETF or on gold held under 12 months is a short-term loss. It can offset equity ETF STCG (20%), gold STCG (slab) or any LTCG (12.5%).
  - A loss on an equity ETF held under 12 months can offset slab-rate gold or liquid ETF gains.
  - A long-term loss (equity or gold ETF held over 12 months) offsets only 12.5% gains.
- The Act does not fix an order when a short-term loss could go against several gains, and the common practice is to apply it to the highest-taxed gain first. For a 30% slab investor that order is: slab-rate STCG, then 20% equity STCG, then 12.5% LTCG. Not sourced in this session.
- Capital losses cannot offset salary, business income or liquid ETF dividends (dividends are "other sources"). General rule, not sourced here.
- Gain harvesting: an equity ETF lot held over 12 months can be sold and rebought to reset cost, tax-free up to ₹1.25 lakh of gain a year (shared with equity shares and equity mutual funds). Round-trip cost is ₹83–306 (section 6). A bot that rotates in under 12 months will rarely have such lots; a core NIFTYBEES holding would.
- Gold, silver and international ETF long-term gains have no exemption, so gain harvesting does nothing for them.

### Gaps
- The condition that the return be filed by the due date to carry losses forward was not sourced in this session (it is in the earlier note for business losses).
- Same-day sell-and-rebuy mechanics were not sourced: a sell from holdings and a buy of the same scrip on the same exchange on the same day may be netted as an intraday trade, leaving the delivery holding and its cost unchanged. Verify with Groww before building a same-day harvest routine; the safe design is to rebuy on the next trading day.
- GAAR's monetary threshold and its practical application to retail harvesting were not sourced.
- No court or CBDT source on harvesting was found; all sources are broker pages.

## 4. New-regime slabs for FY 2026-27 (to compute "slab rate")

### Takeaway
Unchanged from FY 2025-26: nil to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% in ₹4 lakh steps, and 30% above ₹24 lakh. Rebate of up to ₹60,000 makes slab-rate tax nil up to ₹12 lakh of total income; standard deduction is ₹75,000 for salaried taxpayers. The new regime is section 202 of the 2025 Act and the rebate is section 156.

### Cited Findings
- New-regime slabs for FY 2026-27: nil up to ₹4 lakh; 5% on ₹4–8 lakh; 10% on ₹8–12 lakh; 15% on ₹12–16 lakh. - Section 202 of the 2025 Act is the new-regime provision, replacing section 115BAC from tax year 2026-27. — [ebizfiling](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/section-202-of-the-income-tax-act/) (search excerpt)
- Rebate: a resident individual with total income up to ₹12 lakh gets the whole tax or ₹60,000, whichever is less. With the ₹75,000 standard deduction a salaried person can earn ₹12.75 lakh and pay no tax. — [BankBazaar](https://bankbazaar.com/tax/income-tax-slabs.html); [Latestly](https://www.latestly.com/business/budget-2026-what-is-the-section-87a-rebate-limit-under-the-new-income-tax-regime-7296995.html) (search excerpts)

## 5. Dividends from daily-dividend liquid ETFs (LIQUIDBEES)

### Takeaway
Liquid ETF dividends arrive as extra units, are taxed at the slab rate as income from other sources, and have 10% TDS only if one fund house pays more than ₹10,000 in the year (raised from ₹5,000 from FY 2025-26, unchanged for FY 2026-27). The dividend units take their allotment-date value as cost, and their sale is a section 50AA / 76 short-term gain.

### Cited Findings
- Liquid ETFs and Liquid BeES "may distribute income to you in the form of dividend units instead of cash payouts"; these are "treated as dividend income and is taxable under the head Income from Other Sources" at "your applicable income tax slab rate". — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees) (fetched)
- "If the aggregate dividend paid by a fund house exceeds the prescribed threshold during the financial year", TDS "may be deducted before the dividend units are credited to your account". — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees) (fetched)
- "the value of the dividend units on the date of allotment is treated as their cost of acquisition", so "the same amount is not taxed twice". — [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees) (fetched)
- The section 194K threshold was raised to ₹10,000 from ₹5,000, at a 10% rate; these thresholds continue for tax year 2026-27 because Budget 2026 did not alter them. In the 2025 Act the provision is section 393(1), table entry 4(i). No TDS applies where the income is capital gains. — [taxguru TDS chart](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/income-tax-act-2025-tds-rates-thresholds-compliance-ty-2026-27.html); [TDSMAN](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/03/tds-tcs-rate-chart-fy-2026-27/); [studycafe FY 2025-26](https://studycafe.in/updated-tds-rates-and-provisions-for-fy-2025-26-everything-you-need-to-know-399034.html) (search excerpts)
- HDFC's reckoner ties the resident 10% withholding to section 393(1) of the 2025 Act, corresponding to section 194K. — [HDFC MF Tax Reckoner TY 2026-27](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/2026-06/Tax%20Reckoner%20TY%202026-27_0.pdf) (search excerpt)
- **Conflict:** one guide says the 194K threshold was *reduced* from ₹10,000 to ₹5,000 — [themunim](https://themunim.com/blog/latest-tds-rate-chart/) (search excerpt; attribution uncertain); contradicted by the three sources above.
- From 1 April 2026 no interest expense can be deducted against dividend or mutual-fund income. — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/budget-2026-stock-and-mutual-fund-dividends-to-be-taxed-without-interest-deductions/) (search excerpt)
- Zerodha users report broker tax P&L mis-handling LIQUIDBEES. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/zerodha-tax-p-l-for-liquidbees/150874) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
- The ₹10,000 TDS threshold is per fund house per year. At a 6% yield that is roughly ₹1.67 lakh held all year in one AMC's dividend-paying schemes. TDS is a prepayment, not a final tax.
- A dividend-paying liquid ETF is taxed every year at the slab rate whether or not the bot sells. A growth-style liquid ETF (NAV accrues, no dividend) is taxed only on sale, also at the slab rate, and its gain is a short-term capital gain that capital losses can offset; dividends cannot be offset by capital losses. For a rotation bot with occasional losses, the growth-style liquid ETF is the more tax-efficient parking vehicle.
- Each dividend credit creates a small separate lot, so a daily-dividend ETF generates many lots to track; the gain on those lots is close to zero because the price stays near ₹1,000.
- Dividend income is slab-rate income, so the new-regime rebate can cover it.

### Gaps
- Zerodha's page does not state the TDS rate, threshold or section; those come from search excerpts of TDS charts.
- How fractional dividend units are credited or paid out, and whether LIQUIDBEES's distribution mechanics changed in 2025–2026, was not found.
- No source was found comparing dividend and growth liquid ETFs on tax; the comparison above is my inference.
- Nippon India's own LIQUIDBEES documents were not opened.

## 7. Could 12–20 delivery ETF switches a year be taxed as business income?

### Takeaway
The risk is low but not zero. CBDT Circular 6/2016 gives a guaranteed capital-gains choice only for listed shares and securities held more than 12 months; lots held for less fall under the older facts-and-circumstances test (Circular 4/2007), which has no numeric threshold. Practitioners treat delivery-based, own-funds, low-frequency activity as capital gains as long as the taxpayer is consistent year to year.

### Cited Findings
- Circular 6/2016 (29 February 2016), three limbs: (a) where the taxpayer opts to treat listed shares and securities as stock-in-trade, gains are business income irrespective of holding period; (b) for listed shares and securities held more than 12 months, if the assessee desires capital-gains treatment the Assessing Officer shall not dispute it, and the stand must be kept in later years; (c) other cases continue to be decided on facts and earlier guidance. — [itatonline](https://itatonline.org/info/?p=6734); [abcaus](https://abcaus.in/income-tax/cbdt-instruction-sale-of-shares-securities-capital-gain-or-business-income-circular-06-2016.html); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/income-tax/497496/clarification-by-cbdt-on-taxability-of-income-arising-out-of-transfer-of-shares) (search excerpts)
- Freefincal's summary of the circular: a taxpayer choosing capital gains for shares held over 12 months "will not be allowed to take a different stand in subsequent years"; other cases turn on "the concept of 'significant trading activity'" and "the intention of the taxpayer to hold shares as 'stock' or as 'investment'"; "the stand taken by the taxpayer needs to be consistent and should not be changed at a later date". — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/stock-trading-taxation/) (fetched; article dated April 2020)
- Freefincal's practical line: business income is advisable if you are "indulging in significant trading activity with multiple trades in a day" or if share trading is a major income source. — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/stock-trading-taxation/) (fetched)
- Circular 4/2007 lets a taxpayer keep two portfolios (investment and trading) and gives no numeric frequency or volume threshold; ICAI criticised it for "not providing any objective guidelines". — [taxheal](https://taxheal.com/circular-no-4-2007-income-tax.html); [cainindia](https://www.cainindia.org/news/7_2007/tax_circular_on_shares_does_not_provide_any_objective_guidelines_.html) (search excerpts)
- An ITAT decision applied the circular to accept declared capital-gains treatment where the transactions were genuine and delivery was taken. — [taxtmi case note](https://www.taxtmi.com/caselaws?id=360531) (search excerpt)
- One ETF tax guide says frequent ETF trading is "more likely business income based on the volume, frequency, and intention of trading" and would be non-speculative business income. — [carajput](https://carajput.com/learn/taxation-of-exchange-traded-funds-etfs-in-india.html) (search excerpt; single commercial source)

### Inferences
- A rotation bot holds most lots under 12 months, so limb (b)'s safe harbour does not protect it; it sits in limb (c). On the usual factors it looks like investment: delivery only, own funds, no leverage or intraday, 12–20 trades a year, holding periods of weeks to months, a salaried owner whose main income is elsewhere.
- Listed ETF units are "listed securities", so the circular's wording covers them; no source applies it to ETFs by name.
- What classification changes, by asset:
  - Equity ETF gains: capital-gains treatment gives 20% / 12.5%; business treatment gives slab rate. Business treatment is worse above the 20% band and better below it.
  - Gold, international and liquid ETF gains held under 12 months: slab rate either way, so the label changes little except loss rules and the ITR form (ITR-2 vs ITR-3).
- Risk reducers: declare capital gains every year without switching; keep the bot in a demat account used only for investment; avoid intraday and BTST exits; do not claim trading expenses as business deductions.
- Automation itself is not a listed factor in either circular. Whether an Assessing Officer would read "algorithmic" as "systematic business activity" is untested in anything I found.

### Gaps
- The circular's text was not opened (itatonline.org blocked); wording above is from search excerpts and Freefincal's 2020 summary.
- No ruling, CBDT statement or practitioner article was found on ETF rotation or algorithmic delivery trading specifically.
- Whether the 2025 Act or any 2026 CBDT circular re-issued or superseded Circulars 4/2007 and 6/2016 was not checked.
- No source gives a trade-count or turnover threshold; "12–20 trades a year is safe" is my reading of the factors, not a cited rule.
