# Hold three ETFs; let Supabase wake Vercel

Rotating between Indian ETFs does not beat simply holding them, and this session's re-run makes that plainer than [the report of 10 November 2026](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md) could. On Yahoo prices to 11 November 2026, with the engine now in the repository, a fixed one-third mix of NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES and GOLDBEES rebalanced each March made **12.5% a year after Groww costs and FY 2026-27 tax with a 23% worst fall since 2011**, the trend rotation of the same three ETFs made **8.8% with a 15.5% worst fall**, and buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES made 9.4% after exit tax. The earlier report's 9.3% for a four-sleeve rotation is reproduced within half a point (9.0%), but its half-point lead over buy-and-hold is gone now that the data include the Nifty's 10% rebound of late October and early November, which every trend rule sat out. This report therefore reverses the earlier order of preference, as the implementation does. The passive three-ETF mix is the default (plan on **7–10% a year after tax and a worst fall of 25–45%**); the trend rotation of the same ETFs into LIQUIDCASE is the alternative for an owner who wants a smaller fall (**5.5–8.5% and a worst fall of 18–33%**); and momentum stays outside the bot as an optional satellite in a Nifty200 Momentum 30 index fund, on the earlier report's evidence (7–12%, with falls of about 30% in a correction and 65–70% in a 2008-type crash). MID150BEES, the earlier fourth sleeve, is left out because Yahoo serves only a few weeks of its history. Gold's 19.5%-a-year run since 2019 explains nearly every rotation result that looks good, published tactical rules lost about half their return after publication, and none of these ranges is a promise. There is still no honest evidence that LLMs, news sentiment or machine learning make a small intraday account profitable: a new walk-forward ML market filter beat buy-and-hold before tax and lost to it after. On hosting, what the earlier report designed on paper is now built: one Python function beside the Next.js dashboard on Vercel Hobby in Mumbai, woken every five minutes by Supabase pg_cron and pg_net with the secret in Vault and all state in Postgres, and Vercel's legacy daily cron as the backup. A local Vercel build did bundle the Python function and the sibling `worker/` package beside the Next.js app, which the earlier report listed as untested, but **nothing has been deployed to a real Vercel or Supabase project**, and moving the backup to Vercel Schedules, the beta the earlier report prefers, is an open follow-up. What stays impossible without a static IP registered with Groww is placing, changing or cancelling orders by API; the serverless function trades on paper and shows the exact orders for the owner to enter by hand.

A note on sourcing before the detail. This report builds on [the report of 10 November 2026](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md), whose verification pass read the live Vercel, Supabase, GitHub, Google Cloud, Groww, SEBI, NSE and Zerodha pages directly and rebuilt its return tables from fund NAVs. This session's web researchers again worked behind blocked hosts, so where the earlier report read a primary page and this session has only a search snippet, the earlier figure is used and cited as "10 November report"; where the two disagree, both are given with a view on which rests on better evidence. Elsewhere, "search snippet" marks a claim whose page was not read, "own computation" a figure calculated for this research from public price or NAV data, and "recalled" a number from a primary paper that could not be re-opened. Subjects the earlier report settles are summarised briefly; the space goes to what this session adds: the re-run backtests, the machine-learning test, the design as built and checked, and the points where the new notes differ. Pre-launch index backtests and live fund results are kept apart throughout, and every backtest below shows what a rule did on past prices and nothing more.

## Tactical rules lost most of their edge once they were published

### Faber, Antonacci and AQR: the live record is half the backtest or worse

Meb Faber's rule holds each of five asset classes only while it is above its 10-month average, checked at month-end, and otherwise holds T-bills ([Faber, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=962461), recalled and not re-opened). Its original 1972–2005 backtest showed **11.7% a year with a Sharpe ratio of 0.81**; the same rule run without re-tuning from January 2006 to March 2025 made **6.05% with an 11.7% worst drawdown** ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/global-tactical-asset-allocation/), search snippet). A replication computed for this research on SPY, EFA, IEF, VNQ and DBC from December 2006 to November 2026, before costs and tax, gives the fuller picture ([Yahoo Finance data](https://query2.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/SPY?period1=1100000000&period2=1794500000&interval=1mo), own computation):

| Portfolio, Dec 2006 – Nov 2026 | CAGR | Worst drawdown (monthly) |
|---|---|---|
| Faber five-asset rule, 10-month average | 5.4% | -11.2% |
| Same five assets, bought and held | 6.0% | -43.5% |
| 60/40 balanced fund (VBINX) | 7.7% | -32.6% |
| S&P 500 (SPY) | 10.8% | -50.8% |

The rule kept its promise on drawdown and broke it on return: it trailed the 60/40 fund in **14 of 19 calendar years, including nine in a row from 2009 to 2017**. The live fund did worse than the paper model from its first full year, losing 7.29% in 2011 while the model gained 2.49% ([Mutual Fund Observer](https://www.mutualfundobserver.com/?p=4056), search snippet), and Cambria's later trend-plus-momentum ETF compounded at 7.9% from December 2018 to November 2026 against 16.8% for the S&P 500 ([Yahoo Finance, GMOM](https://query2.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/GMOM?period1=1100000000&period2=1794500000&interval=1mo), own computation).

Gary Antonacci's dual momentum holds one asset at a time: US or non-US equities, whichever has the higher 12-month return, or bonds when US equities trail T-bills ([Antonacci, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2042750), recalled). Its 1974–2013 backtest of about 17% a year has not repeated. Three independent trackers put its shortfall against the S&P 500 since publication at **5.4, about 5 and 4.8 points a year** ([AlgoStrategyLab](https://github.com/AlgoStrategyLab/dual-momentum); [Quant for Free](https://quant4free.com/analysis/dual-momentum/); [Petit, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7427878), all search snippets), and this research's own replication agrees at 8.2% against 13.4% from 2014 to November 2026, with a worst drawdown of 19.5% that was no better than a 60/40 fund's. The rule is also fragile. ReSolve tested 1,225 parameter variants and found the original specification's out-of-sample result "regressed to the median" ([ReSolve](https://files.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/05/06/global-equity-momentum-a-craftsmans-perspective), search snippet), Newfound wrote that "the simplicity of GEM belies its inherent fragility" ([Newfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/tag/gem/), search snippet), and in the replication a one-month change in lookback moved the 2010 result from +11.2% to -8.3%. No audited live account tracking the rule was found; every post-2014 number is a tracker.

AQR's century-long simulation shows diversified trend following positive in every decade from 1903 ([AQR paper, eCapital copy](http://www.ecapital.ch/downloads/AQR_A%20Century_of_Evidence_on_Trend-Following.pdf), search snippet), but the live record is a decade of roughly nothing, one spectacular year and then a deep drawdown. AQR's own managed-futures fund compounded at **4.3% a year from January 2010 to November 2026**, lost about 17% across 2016–2021 and then made 35.5% in 2022 ([Yahoo Finance, AQMIX](https://query2.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/AQMIX?period1=1100000000&period2=1794500000&interval=1mo), own computation); the SG Trend Index then fell 20.4% from May 2024 to May 2025, its second-largest drawdown since 2000 ([Cambridge Associates](https://publishedresearch.cambridgeassociates.com/?p=19140), search snippet). That evidence is about long-short futures across fifty-plus markets. A long-only rotation among three correlated Indian equity indices, gold and cash has far fewer independent bets, so the century-long Sharpe ratios do not transfer; what does transfer is the pattern of many small losing years paid for by one crisis year.

### Indian evidence: lower risk, not higher return, and mostly before costs and tax

Indian published evidence is thin, blog-level and mostly ignores costs and tax. Freefincal's rolling-window tests of a 10-month average on the Sensex conclude "The backtest says yes but keep in mind the assumptions made, real-life limitations, the fact that in the recent past results are not that good and that it is only a back-test", and its follow-up states the lesson plainly: "Time the market because you desire lower risk and not because you desire a higher return" ([Freefincal, Part 2](https://freefincal.com/market-timing-with-ten-month-moving-average-tactical-asset-allocation-backtest-part-2/); [Freefincal, Part 3](https://freefincal.com/tactical-asset-allocation-backtest-part-3-short-term-vs-long-term/)). Its 2022 test of a 6-month-against-12-month average found the strategy "fails badly" on the Sensex over 15 years because the March 2020 crash was too short for the averages to cross back in time ([Freefincal, May 2022](https://freefincal.com/a-risk-in-market-timing-that-122-years-of-backtesting-failed-to-reveal/)). Capitalmind's Nifty-or-gold dual momentum (hold whichever had the higher one-year return, 23 trades in about 17 years from 2005) turned ₹1 lakh into ₹8.4 lakh against ₹7.7 lakh for holding the Nifty, "comparable returns to the Nifty with lower volatility", with costs and taxes not mentioned ([Capitalmind, June 2022](https://www.capitalmind.in/insights/should-gold-be-in-your-long-term-portfolio)). The headline-grabbing results come from short, gold-friendly windows: Momentum Lab reports 18.79% a year for Nifty/gold/fixed-deposit dual momentum over 2014–2024 with no costs, taxes or switch counts ([Momentum Lab](https://momentumlab.substack.com/p/backtesting-dual-momentum-strategy), search snippet), and Weekend Investing's "Mi EverGreen" claim of 21.1% is a mostly pre-launch, promotional figure for a stock-momentum portfolio with a gold sleeve, not ETF rotation ([smallcase blog](https://www.smallcase.com/blog/mi-evergreen-your-affordable-companion-for-financial-independence/), search snippet). No usable figures were retrieved from PrimeInvestor or from Weekend Investing's ETF-only smallcases, whose sites were blocked or gated, and no peer-reviewed Indian study of asset-class rotation was found. That is the largest gap in the published evidence.

No live, rule-based Indian ETF-rotation product with a public record was found either. The nearest live evidence is discretionary multi-asset mutual funds, measured here from direct-plan NAVs ([AMFI NAVs via mfapi.in](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/120334), own computation): since January 2013 the two best made 15.6–15.9% a year against 11.9% for a Nifty 50 index fund, the median roughly matched the index with smaller falls, and one 2020 launch made 5.5% against 14.7%. The funds with gold made 17–25% in calendar 2025, the year GOLDBEES rose 72%. These funds switch internally without triggering the investor's capital-gains tax, which a do-it-yourself bot cannot do.

## In Thunderbot's own tests no rotation rule beat the fixed mix after tax

### Sixteen years of ETF prices, Groww costs and FY 2026-27 tax

The primary evidence for the recommendation is this repository's own backtester, run on 11 November 2026 on Yahoo daily closes from 2009 with the December 2019 unit-split errors repaired, decisions on one day's close filled at the next day's close, the full Groww delivery charge schedule plus 0.05% slippage a side, and FY 2026-27 tax rules (30% slab, 4% cess, first-in-first-out lots, loss set-off) applied to the whole history ([Thunderbot backtests](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_backtests.md)). The liquid ETF is modelled from the Nifty 1D Rate less 0.30% a year, because no growth-type liquid ETF existed before 2024.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NIFTYBEES bought and held | 10.1% (pre-tax) | 9.4% | -36.3% | 1,019 days | 2 | — |

| Fixed 75% equity, 25% gold | 12.3% | 11.7% | -25.3% | 442 days | 65 | ₹54,378 |
| Fixed NIFTYBEES and JUNIORBEES, no gold | 11.4% | 10.8% | -34.9% | 775 days | 30 | ₹0 |
| **Trend rotation: the same three ETFs, each held only above its 200-day average, otherwise liquid ETF** | **8.8%** | 8.8% | **-15.5%** | 749 days | 268 | ₹1,59,470 |
| Trend rotation without gold | 7.8% | 7.8% | -18.9% | 825 days | 142 | ₹1,21,980 |

Rotation does beat what Thunderbot has today: trend rotation of the three ETFs made 8.8% with a 15.5% worst fall against 7.1% and 22.1% for the Nifty-only trend rule. But neither beats holding. The fixed mix won against every rotation variant in 2010–2018, in 2019–2026, in 2016–2020 and in 2021–2026; the only window where a rotation rule won was 2011–2015. What timing bought was a shallower worst fall at a price of **3 to 4 points a year**, and a better worst three-year stretch (+2.6% a year for trend rotation against +0.9% for the mix and -3.5% for NIFTYBEES). Costs and tax took about a point a year from the rotation rules (trend rotation made 9.6% before tax) and almost nothing from the mix (12.7% before tax). Parking idle money in a liquid ETF is worth about 1.2 points a year to the Nifty trend rule, which sits out of the market roughly 23% of the time.

Three robustness results matter for design. Parameters are fragile: top-1 rotation ranged from **3.7% to 14.4%** depending only on which ETFs were on the list, top-2 from 8.4% to 10.0% across seven lookbacks, and the Nifty trend rule from 6.3% to 7.6% across twelve average-and-buffer settings. Timing of the fill matters little: trading the same day, the next day or two days later moved trend rotation from 9.1% to 8.8% to 8.6%, so a missed 15:10 run that trades the next session is tolerable. And capital hardly matters between ₹1 lakh and ₹20 lakh (the mix made 12.46%, 12.48% and 12.42%), because fixed charges shrink with size while the ₹1.25 lakh exemption covers less of a larger account's gains.

Two engines on two data sources agree on every trend-rotation variant to within half a point, which is the best cross-check either report has; most of the remaining gap on the three-sleeve rule is the fill assumption, since filled the same day this session's rule made 9.1%. What moved is everything that stays invested. NIFTYBEES rose from 8.8% to 9.4% and the passive four-ETF mix from 11.7% to 12.0%, because this session's data run sixteen days longer and contain the rebound in which NIFTYBEES gained about 10% while the trend rules sat in the liquid ETF. The earlier report foresaw this "by this arithmetic and not by a re-run"; the re-run confirms it. **Trend rotation now trails plain NIFTYBEES by about half a point a year after tax, where on data ending sixteen days earlier it led by half a point**, and its deficit to the passive mix widened to about three points. A conclusion that flips on sixteen days of data was never strong, and the comparison is a fair measure of how much any of these figures can be trusted. Dual momentum fell from 11.5% to 9.4% for a different reason: the two reports score it differently (6- and 12-month returns gated by the 200-day average, against a 3-, 6- and 12-month blend that must beat the liquid ETF), which is the specification fragility both reports describe.

Three of the earlier report's findings were not repeated here and still carry weight. Moving the decision from month-end to the fifteenth trading day cut the Nifty rule's return from 7.6% to 3.9% and deepened the four-sleeve rotation's worst fall to 31%, so the month-end results above sit near the lucky end of the specifications. Its bhavcopy prices reach back to 2008, when the passive four-ETF mix fell 49% and the rotation about 21%. And its check against fund NAVs found that closing prices understate panic-day falls by about two points (NIFTYBEES's worst fall since 2011 is 38.4% on NAV against 36.3% on closes) and that NIFTYBEES's price history omits cash payouts before 2016, worth about 0.3 points a year to buy-and-hold ([NAV verification notes](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/verification_nav_returns.md)). This session adds direct support for that last inference: NSE's corporate-action record lists NIFTYBEES dividends of ₹7.5, ₹7 and ₹8 in March 2013, March 2014 and February 2015, which Yahoo neither lists nor adjusts for ([NSE corporate actions](https://www.nseindia.com/api/corporates-corporateActions?index=equities&symbol=NIFTYBEES)). Neither backtest adds them back, so both understate buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES slightly.

### Against this session's NAV-based test

The web research and the first-hand tests agree on direction and differ on several numbers, and the differences are instructive. A NAV-based test built for this research from December 2007, which unlike the Yahoo ETF data includes all of 2008, puts the one-third mix at 12.3% before tax and 11.4% after, with a worst fall of **-37% on monthly data** rather than -23%, and a Faber-style 10-month rule with gold at 10.5% before tax, 9.0–9.3% after, with a worst fall of -13.7% ([AMFI NAVs via mfapi.in](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/100822), own computation). That test trades on the signal day at NAV, uses a liquid mutual fund that yields more than a liquid ETF, and omits the ₹1.25 lakh exemption; Thunderbot's trades the next day at exchange prices. The first-hand numbers are the better guide to what the bot would have earned since 2011, and the NAV test is the only evidence on 2008, when the mix fell 30% in the calendar year and the trend rule 12%. In the same NAV test, 12-month top-1 dual momentum made 8.1% with a 47% worst fall, while a 9-month lookback made 14.8%; Thunderbot's blended 3/6/12-month top-1 rule made 12.0% on a later window. That spread is specification luck, not signal. Finally, the earlier Thunderbot report put the Nifty trend rule at 8.07% after tax; the new run gives 7.1% with the liquid ETF, because the earlier run began after January 2011's 10% fall, a start-date effect worth about 0.8 points a year to every rule, and credited idle cash with an untaxed 6%.

## One BeES ticker per asset class, a growth liquid ETF, and a tax code that punishes switching

### The instruments: liquidity and data history decide, not expense ratio

In each asset class one ETF is far more liquid than its peers, and it is almost always Nippon's "BeES" product. Fund size is a poor guide: SBI's Nifty 50 ETF holds ₹2.07 lakh crore but trades about ₹33 crore a day, and UTI's holds ₹71,671 crore and trades under ₹2 crore, against ₹190–280 crore for NIFTYBEES ([Groww ETF list](https://groww.in/etfs)). The figures below are from Groww's ETF pages read on 11 November 2026, which carry no as-of date and give launch as a year only, plus daily traded value computed from Yahoo's chart data; no AMC factsheet could be opened, so expense ratios and exact launch dates should be confirmed before they are relied on, and tracking difference (the actual return gap to the index) was not found for any of these funds.

### Liquid ETFs: the growth kind, and specifically LIQUIDCASE

### Practical traps: price is not NAV, and Yahoo's history is corrupt in known places

Since May 2023 every order under ₹25 crore must go through the exchange, so the bot trades with other investors at the market price, not with the fund at NAV ([Business Standard, May 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/after-two-deferments-exchange-route-compulsory-for-sub-rs-25-cr-etf-deals-123050100756_1.html), search snippet). Published tracking error compares NAV with the index and says nothing about that price: MAFANG shows 0.04% beside a 50% premium. Even the largest ETFs dislocate under stress. NIFTYBEES closed **7.1% above its NAV on 18 March 2020** on the earlier report's close-against-NAV measurement, which is the better basis (this session's price-against-index estimate gives 6.5%), although over the year to October 2026 its median closing premium was 0.06% and GOLDBEES's 0.11% ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md); [NAV verification notes](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/verification_nav_returns.md)), Freefincal measured its tracking error for the year to April 2020 at 0.16% on NAV but 5.6% on traded price ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/nippon-india-etf-nifty-bees-vs-uti-nifty-index-fund-which-is-better/)), and silver ETFs were reported 5–12% above fair value in October 2025 during a physical shortage ([Business Standard, October 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/paying-too-much-silver-etfs-look-shiny-but-mind-the-5-12-premium-risk-125101300184_1.html)), a range the earlier report could not reproduce for SILVERBEES at the close, where the largest gap that month was 3.0%; the press figures compare with intraday indicative NAV. Since 7 September 2026 the daily price band is measured from the ETF's own previous close, not its NAV: 10% for equity and debt ETFs, 6% for gold and silver and a fixed 5% for liquid ETFs, so nothing mechanical stops a premium building over successive days ([NSE circular NSE/CMTR/76101](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR76101.pdf), read for the 10 November report; this session's notes had the date and base only from conflicting snippets). Thin tickers are worse: Kotak's Midcap 50 ETF had 74 sessions with no trade at all in the last 250, and six gilt and bond ETFs trade under ₹0.25 crore on a median day ([Yahoo data](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/MIDCAP.NS?period1=0&period2=1900000000&interval=1d&events=div%2Csplits), own computation). The defence is a limit order on a liquid ticker placed away from the open, and a refusal to buy when price is far from NAV. No bid-ask spread or impact-cost data was found for any ETF.

Yahoo's data fails in two different ways around unit splits. NSE Clearing's circular confirms that NIFTYBEES split 1:10 and GOLDBEES 1:100 with ex-date 19 December 2019; JUNIORBEES and LIQUIDBEES did not split ([NSE Clearing circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMPT42889.pdf)). Yahoo back-adjusted the history but left **19 and 20 December 2019 at one-tenth and one-hundredth of the right price** and records no split event, so the series contains a false -90% and +900% pair for NIFTYBEES (129.25, then 13.02 and 13.03, then 129.93); MON100 has the same fault on 17–18 June 2021 ([Yahoo NIFTYBEES](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/NIFTYBEES.NS?period1=0&period2=1900000000&interval=1d&events=div%2Csplits); [Yahoo MON100](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/MON100.NS?period1=0&period2=1900000000&interval=1d&events=div%2Csplits)). For other ETFs (HDFCNEXT50, Kotak's silver and midcap ETFs in February 2026) the split is not applied at all. A filter that deletes every move above 20% is the wrong fix, because every silver ETF genuinely fell about 19% and then 23% on 30 January and 2 February 2026. Ticker renames silently break downloads too: SBI's ETFs were renamed around 30 October 2026 (SETFNIF50 to SBINIFTY50) and their Yahoo history vanished. The two instrument notes disagree on one point that touches the backtest. One found no dividend events for NIFTYBEES on Yahoo and concluded that price equals total return; the other found NSE recording NIFTYBEES dividends in 2012–2015 that Yahoo neither lists nor adjusts for ([NSE corporate actions](https://www.nseindia.com/api/corporates-corporateActions?index=equities&symbol=NIFTYBEES)). Yahoo therefore understates NIFTYBEES's return slightly in those years, and the absence of events in Yahoo's feed is not evidence of anything, since it also missed the split.

### Tax for FY 2026-27: the last change was 23 July 2024

No capital-gains rate, holding period or exemption changed in Budget 2025 or Budget 2026 ([equitiesindia](https://equitiesindia.com/learn/taxation/budget-2026-impact-investors), search snippet). What changed is numbering: the Income-tax Act 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026, so sections 111A, 112A, 112 and 50AA became 196, 198, 197 and 76 at unchanged rates. No statute text, CBDT FAQ or AMC tax reckoner could be opened in this research; the matrix rests on Zerodha's and Freefincal's pages, which were read, on search snippets of fund-house reckoners, and on Groww's own ETF tax article, which the earlier report read directly and which confirms the equity, gold, silver and debt rows ([Groww](https://groww.in/blog/how-are-etfs-taxed); [10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)).

Sources: [ICICI Prudential Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf) (search snippet); [Zerodha: STT on ETFs](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/charges-at-zerodha/articles/stt-etfs); [Zerodha: liquid ETFs](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/dividends-on-liquid-etfs-and-liquid-bees); [Freefincal on the specified-fund definition](https://freefincal.com/?p=214665). The row that sources disagree on is gold and international ETFs. The definition of a "specified mutual fund", whose gains are always short-term, was narrowed from 1 April 2025 to funds holding more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments, which returned gold, silver and overseas-equity ETFs to the ordinary 12-month rule for any sale from that date; pages that still put them at slab rate forever are using the old test. For India-listed international ETFs the earlier report found sources split between 12 and 24 months and left the holding period unresolved; this session's reading of the enacted definition supports 12, but no statute text was opened in either session. Groww's own explainer says liquid ETFs are taxed "similar to stocks", which contradicts the specified-fund rule and should not be relied on ([Groww liquid ETF page](https://groww.in/p/liquid-etf)). Since Budget 2025 the new-regime rebate covers only slab-rate tax, so tax on equity ETF short-term gains and on any 12.5% gain is payable even when total income is under ₹12 lakh ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-87a-rebate-applicable-for-capital-gains-after-budget-2025/)). Short-term losses offset any capital gain, long-term losses only long-term gains, both carry forward eight years, and India has no wash-sale rule ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/what-is-tax-loss-harvesting)).

The consequence for strategy design is direct. For a 30%-slab investor a rotation that holds lots for under 12 months pays 20.8% on equity ETF gains and 31.2% on gold and liquid ETF gains, while the fixed mix realises almost nothing and can use the ₹1.25 lakh exemption on its equity legs each year. The risk that twelve to twenty delivery switches a year are reclassified as business income is low but not zero: CBDT's safe harbour covers only listed securities held more than 12 months, and no source applies it to ETF rotation by name ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/stock-trading-taxation/)).

Charges on ETF delivery trades at Groww are small and unchanged since June 2025: brokerage of ₹20 or 0.1% per order, whichever is lower, a DP charge of ₹20 plus GST per sell, stamp duty of 0.015% on buys, NSE charges of about 0.00307% a side, and STT of 0.001% on equity ETF sales against 0.1% on both legs for shares ([Groww pricing](https://groww.in/pricing); [Zerodha: STT on ETFs](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/charges-at-zerodha/articles/stt-etfs)). A NIFTYBEES round trip therefore costs about **₹83 on ₹50,000 (0.165%), ₹118 on ₹2 lakh (0.059%) and ₹306 on ₹10 lakh (0.031%)**, roughly one-eighth of the same trade in shares. This session's tax researcher found no ETF-specific STT rate on Groww's pricing pages; the earlier report read Groww's STT help page directly, and it gives 0.001% on equity ETF sales and none on gold, liquid and gilt ETFs ([Groww help](https://groww.in/help/stocks/sx-pricing/what-is-stt); [10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). That report also settled a point this session's notes left open: Groww's help page says the DP charge is levied per sell order, not per ETF per day, so a sale split into two orders pays twice.

### Picking 15 stocks against holding the fund, and trend filters on momentum

Free building blocks for an honest stock-level backtest now exist: several 2026 GitHub projects rebuild point-in-time index membership from NSE circulars or archived pages and keep delisted stocks by working from daily bhavcopies ([BKKB20/nse-index-history](https://github.com/BKKB20/nse-index-history); [NegativeZone/nsepit](https://github.com/NegativeZone/nsepit); [abhij1306/NSE-noodles](https://github.com/abhij1306/NSE-noodles)). The earlier report inspected these more closely and its verdicts take precedence: NSE's own inclusion-and-exclusion workbook plus the split-adjusted NSEDATA4ME bhavcopy database (4,583 securities, 1,037 of them delisted) are the pieces to use, nsepit's Nifty 50 membership validated within 0.5 points a year of NSE's equal-weight index, and BKKB20's reconstruction is unusable because its "2004" Nifty 100 contains companies listed a decade later ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). None is a validated, turnkey Nifty 200 history with prices, and EODHD's US$19.99-a-month plan, which lists delisted tickers, is the cheapest paid route though its Indian coverage was not verified ([EODHD plans](https://eodhd.com/help/plans), search snippet). The size of the bias is large, and the earlier report measured it directly: using today's list instead of point-in-time membership added 7.7 points a year to an equal-weight Nifty 50 portfolio over 2011–2026 and 9.1 points to an equal-weight Nifty 100, and a top-15 momentum rule returned 20.4% on today's Nifty 50 list against **10.3% on point-in-time membership** ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Published estimates are smaller: survivor-only tests of the Nifty Smallcap 250 overstate returns by 4.94 points a year ([arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380)), and one open-source backtester reports that "survivorship inflates momentum by roughly +0.4 to +0.9 Sharpe" ([ReaperOAK](https://github.com/ReaperOAK/survivorship-free-backtester)). A cheap sanity check needs none of this: if a hobby backtest of 2021–2026 shows much better than the UTI fund's 14.1% and -31.5%, the difference is bias or luck.

Even granting a do-it-yourself portfolio the same gross return as the index, it should finish **1.0–2.5 points a year behind a momentum index fund after tax** at ₹1–20 lakh, because the investor pays 20% short-term tax at each rebalance while the fund pays none on its internal trades; the central estimate is 1.5 points, from a ten-year model built for this research on Groww's charges and NSE's reported index churn of 101–134% a year, with two rebalances a year and no API fee. The earlier report, assuming quarterly turnover of 120–170%, put the gap at 2–4 points at ₹1 lakh, 1.5–3 at ₹5 lakh and 0.5–2.5 at ₹20 lakh; the two models bracket the same conclusion, that the gap is one to three points and never closes with size ([NSE whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf); [Groww pricing](https://groww.in/pricing), own computation). Fixed charges cost a 15-stock book about 0.6% a year at ₹1 lakh, 0.4% at ₹2–3 lakh, under 0.25% from about ₹5 lakh and 0.1% at ₹10 lakh, so below roughly ₹3 lakh DIY is handicapped on costs alone and above ₹5 lakh tax is the deciding factor. To win, the owner's picks must beat the index's by about 1.5 points a year with half as many names, when the index's own selection has added nothing over a size-matched blend in 5.7 live years. The one argument for DIY is that a delisting-inclusive backtest finds the momentum premium concentrated in less liquid stocks that large funds cannot hold ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). For buy-and-hold on Groww, the direct-plan index fund is simpler than any of the thin ETFs.

A trend filter on a momentum fund has no reliable Indian support. On the UTI fund's live NAV from January 2022 to November 2026, switching to cash when the fund was below its own 200-day average at month-end cut the return from 9.9% to **5.2% (4.0% after tax) without reducing the 31.5% drawdown**, while a 100-day rule made 12.9% and a rule keyed to the Nifty 50's average made 14.4% ([AMFI NAV](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/148703), own computation). A spread that wide between neighbouring rules over 4.8 years is noise, and choosing among them afterwards would be curve-fitting. The 2024–25 loss was a rotation out of momentum while the broad market recovered, which is not the kind of loss a market-level filter is built for, and every exit hands back the tax deferral that is the fund wrapper's main advantage.

### LLMs, news sentiment and machine learning: what the evidence supports

The owner has said he would like Thunderbot to be a profitable intraday, LLM-powered, ML-powered application. The evidence does not support that at retail scale, and it is better to say so plainly than to build toward it.

The earlier report reviewed the LLM literature and reached the same verdict, so only the essentials and this session's additions are given here ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). The best-known result, Lopez-Lira and Tang's GPT-scored news strategy on US stocks, is genuinely out-of-sample, yet its annualised Sharpe ratio fell from **6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 in January–May 2024**, it is **unprofitable at a 20 basis-point round-trip cost**, and its authors say it is "only feasible for market participants whose transaction costs are sufficiently low" ([arXiv 2304.07619v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)); an Indian delivery round trip in shares costs about 30 basis points. Any backtest that asks a 2026 model about 2020–2025 news is contaminated by construction, because "prompts cannot change what information is encoded in the model's parameters" ([arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). Live tests are short and unflattering: four of six frontier models lost real money in a 17-day contest ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/), search snippet), and the one evaluation that runs twenty years with delisted stocks finds buy-and-hold beating LLM agents with statistical significance ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v4)). The more careful the test, the worse the result. News is priced before a retail bot can act: across 1.68 million US news events in 2023–2026 the move by the close of publication day was 2.8 times its value twenty days later ([arXiv 2608.14014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014)). Machine learning fares no better at this scale: order-book models "exhibit a significant performance drop when exposed to new data" ([LOBCAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01915)), daily ML strategies have had "profitability fluctuating around zero after transaction costs" since 2010 ([Fischer and Krauss](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ejores:v:270:y:2018:i:2:p:654-669)), and the only direct Indian test of an ML ranker against plain 12-1 momentum, an unreviewed preprint that was not opened, reports that the ranker does not win after costs ([SSRN 7137361](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=7137361)). No live LLM-trading evidence on Indian equities and no peer-reviewed, cost-inclusive study of intraday ML on NSE stocks at retail scale was found, positive or negative; that is absence of evidence, not proof of impossibility.

Thunderbot's own test gave ML its most favourable honest setup on free data: 216 month-ends of the Nifty, ten price features, three small scikit-learn models refitted each month on earlier months only, 156 out-of-sample decisions from October 2013 to September 2026 ([Thunderbot backtests](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_backtests.md)).

| NIFTYBEES or liquid ETF, Oct 2013 – Sep 2026, ₹5 lakh | CAGR before tax | CAGR after costs and tax | Worst fall | Trades | Tax paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Always hold | 11.4% | 11.4% (10.6% if sold and taxed at the end) | -36.3% | 2 | ₹0 |
| 200-day rule | 9.7% | 9.0% | -15.9% | 76 | ₹94,631 |
| Gradient boosting | 12.2% | 9.4% | -15.0% | 188 | ₹3,45,373 |
| Random forest | 12.6% | 9.7% | -15.0% | 208 | ₹3,69,435 |

Before tax the tree models looked best. But they were right about next month's direction only 52–55% of the time, no better than always guessing "up"; shuffled signals with the same time in the market did as well in 3–5% of draws when six variants had been tried; changing only the random seed moved the pre-tax result by more than three points a year; and **after tax the edge was gone**, with ₹3.5–3.7 lakh of tax paid on ₹5 lakh of capital. The models did keep the worst fall to 15%, the same as the 200-day rule. The filter was not adopted. Intraday ML could not be tested at all, since free 5-minute data covers sixty days.

The role that the evidence does support for an LLM is narrow, and it is the one implemented: an optional reviewer, off by default, that sees the planned orders and the price facts behind them and may approve or postpone the whole rebalance by one session, at most twice in a row. It cannot change, add or enlarge an order, a reviewer error counts as approval, and every verdict is stored with its prompt ([implementation notes](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_implementation_checks.md)). That asymmetry follows the research: damage in LLM trading agents comes when the model can originate or enlarge positions, fake or injected news can steer agents into "runaway exposure" ([TradeTrap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02261v1)), and LLM agents given discretion were "too cautious when risk is rewarded and too aggressive when it is penalised" ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v4)). The last finding predicts the reviewer will postpone too often, so each postponement should be logged with what the trade would have earned. The earlier report makes the sharper point that a veto firing a few times a year cannot be validated on returns within a lifetime, and proposes judging it on precision instead: record whether each claimed anomaly was real and remove the reviewer if false alarms exceed true ones after the first ten ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Deterministic checks (a price more than 15% from the last close, stale history) belong in code, where they are free and reliable. Cost is not the constraint: one daily review is about US$0.001–0.08 at November 2026 list prices, whereas a frontier model on every one-minute bar for fifty stocks would cost on the order of US$28,000–56,000 a year ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), own arithmetic). No published study scores an LLM veto layer on a rules-based strategy, so the design is compatible with the evidence, not proven by it.

## The recommendation: the passive mix by default, trend rotation for a smaller fall, momentum outside the bot

### Where this differs from the 10 November report, and why

The earlier report made a four-sleeve trend rotation (NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, MID150BEES and GOLDBEES, parked in LIQUIDCASE) the core strategy, told an owner who can sit through deeper falls to hold the passive equal-weight mix instead, and kept momentum as an optional satellite through a fund ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). The implementation, and this report, turn the first two around: the passive three-ETF mix is the default and the three-ETF trend rotation is the lower-drawdown alternative. Two things moved the order. The re-run through 11 November removed the rotation's return lead over even plain NIFTYBEES, which leaves a shallower fall as its only measured benefit, bought at about three points a year against the mix. And the fourth sleeve cannot run on the bot's data: Yahoo served 15 daily rows of MID150BEES on 11 November (32 on the earlier report's check a day before), so its 200-day average cannot be computed. MOM100, a Midcap 100 ETF with Yahoo history from 2011 but only about ₹2.7 crore of daily turnover, is offered as an optional sleeve; in the backtest it added 0.2 points a year to the rotation and nothing to its worst fall. The earlier report's remedy, a price table seeded from NSE's bhavcopy archive, would restore MID150BEES and has not been built. On everything else the two reports agree: rotation buys smaller falls and not higher returns, gold supplied the edge, and the 15-stock book should give way to a fund.

### Exact rules as implemented

Both candidates use the same three tickers, work on daily closes, and can be backtested from 2009 on free Yahoo data once the December 2019 errors are repaired. Both are in the repository's backtester and paper-trading worker ([Thunderbot backtests](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_backtests.md); [implementation notes](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_implementation_checks.md)).

| | Strategy 1, the default: passive mix (`core_mix`) | Strategy 2, the alternative: trend rotation (`etf_trend`) |
|---|---|---|
| Tickers | NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, GOLDBEES | NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, GOLDBEES, and LIQUIDCASE for money that is out |
| Target | Fixed weights. The backtest used one-third each; the dashboard default is 32.5% / 32.5% / 35%. Pick one set and do not change it | One-third of the account per ETF |
| Signal | None | On the last trading day of each month, an ETF is "in" if its latest close is above the simple average of its own last 200 daily closes; otherwise that third goes to LIQUIDCASE. No buffer |
| When it trades | On the first run, buy the target weights. Afterwards only on the last trading day of March, when every holding is brought back to its target weight | At month-end, only if the set of holdings changes or any weight has drifted more than 10 percentage points from its target |
| Orders a year in the backtest | About 4 | About 17 |
| Fill assumed in the backtest | Decision on one day's close, fill at the next day's close | Same |
| Guards before any trade | A missing live price, price history more than 7 days old, or a live price more than 15% from the last close postpones the rebalance and raises an alert | Same |

The details differ from the earlier report's specification in small ways that the backtests say do not matter much: thirds instead of quarters, each ETF's own closes as the signal instead of index series, a 10-point no-trade band instead of 5, and a March instead of a December rebalance for the mix. One detail from the earlier report should be carried into live trading whichever rule is used: Groww charges its DP fee per sell order, so each ETF should be exited in a single limit order. The one-third weights and the dashboard's 35% gold default also differ slightly, and the difference is not worth optimising: a 75% equity, 25% gold mix made 12.3% against 12.5% with a worst fall two points deeper.

### One set of planning ranges from the two reports

| | What the backtests showed, after costs and tax | Plan on, a year | Plan on, worst fall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passive three-ETF mix | 12.5% (11.9% if sold at the end), worst fall 23%, since 2011, this session; four-ETF version 11.7% and 27.5% since 2011, and 49% in 2008, 10 November report | **7–10%** | **25–45%** |
| Three-ETF trend rotation | 8.8%, worst fall 15.5%, this session; 9.2–9.3% and 13–18%, and 7.3–9.8% with falls up to 31% across decision days, 10 November report | **5.5–8.5%** | **18–33%** |
| Momentum 30 index fund, outside the bot | Live fund 14.1% a year since March 2021, 7.9% over the last five years, fell 31.5% in 2024–25 | **7–12%** | **About 30% in a correction, 65–70% in a 2008-type crash** |

These ranges are judgements, not forecasts, and each is set below its backtest on purpose. For the rotation the earlier report gave 5.5–9% and 20–33%. The top of the return range comes down half a point because the re-run with year-by-year liquid returns, next-day fills and the November rebound gives 8.8–9.0% where the earlier run gave 9.2–9.3%; the bottom is unchanged, resting on the no-gold versions (5.4–9.1% across the earlier report's specifications, 7.8–8.0% here) and on the decay every published trend rule has shown. The worst-fall range starts at 18%, which is this session's 15.5% on closing prices plus the two points by which closes understated panic-day falls against NAV, and keeps the earlier report's top of 33%, which comes from its decision-day test: the same rule deciding mid-month fell 31%. This session ran no test of the decision day, so the earlier figure stands.

For the passive mix the earlier report gave no range. The return range leans on the periods when gold did not flatter it: 9.7% in 2010–2018, when gold made 6.1% a year, and 6.9% in 2011–2015 in this session's run, and 9.1% over 2008–2018 for the four-ETF mix in the earlier one. The worst-fall range starts from the 23% since 2011, about 25% on NAV, and ends at 45% because of 2008: this session's NAV test shows the three-ETF mix down 30% in that calendar year and 37% peak to trough on monthly data, which understates a daily low, while the earlier report's four-ETF mix with a midcap sleeve fell 49% on daily closes. A mix with a third in gold and no midcaps belongs between the two. The discounts from the backtests rest on three things the evidence supports: gold's 19.5% a year since 2019 is unlikely to repeat and was followed last time by six flat years; sixteen years in one country with one gold bull market and no multi-year equity bear market is a small sample; and tactical rules have earned materially less after publication than before. A range is also not a floor. The mix's worst three-year stretch in the backtest was +0.9% a year, and trend rotation spent more than two years below a previous high.

The choice between the first two rows is a choice about the worst fall, and the backtest offers a pointed comparison: a static one-third mix of NIFTYBEES, GOLDBEES and the liquid ETF, with no timing at all, made **10.0% a year with a 14.4% worst fall**, better on both counts than trend rotation's 8.8% and 15.5%, with a third of the trades and a third of the tax. That echoes Freefincal's finding that "if you simply held lower (30%) equity, you could have got the job done instead of timing" ([Freefincal, Part 3](https://freefincal.com/tactical-asset-allocation-backtest-part-3-short-term-vs-long-term/)). It leans on gold as heavily as everything else here, but it means trend rotation should be chosen because the owner wants a rule that steps aside in a prolonged bear market like 2008, and not because the backtest favours it.

The main risks differ by strategy. For the passive mix they are a deep equity fall held all the way down (JUNIORBEES lost 63% in 2008), a long gold slump (GOLDBEES returned about -3% in total over 2013–2018 on NAV; [AMFI NAV](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/140088), own computation), and the owner abandoning the mix at the bottom. For trend rotation they are whipsaw years in which it loses while the mix gains (-5.8% against +0.9% in 2018, -5.0% against +6.1% in 2022, -5.3% against +3.6% in 2026 to date; its deepest fall in the whole backtest, 15.5%, is the one still running since January 2026), late re-entry after V-shaped recoveries of exactly the kind seen in the last fortnight, a result that depends on the day of the month the crash happens to straddle, short-term tax on most exits, and a parking yield that is now about 5% before slab-rate tax. Common to both are ETF prices that leave NAV under stress, a tax code that can change, and data errors that the 15% price guard is there to catch.

The momentum satellite is the earlier report's recommendation and stands unchanged: a minority of capital, only for money that can sit through a 30% fall and two to six years of lagging the market, held buy-and-hold in UTI's Nifty200 Momentum 30 Index Fund direct plan outside the bot, with the 15-stock rotation retired unless a rebuild on point-in-time Nifty 100 membership shows it beating the index by 1–3.5 points a year before costs ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Nothing in this session's fund data argues for more than that, and the size-matched-blend comparison argues for expecting less.

## A no-server design that works on paper: Supabase schedules, Vercel computes

### The Vercel limits that shape it

All Vercel figures were read from Vercel's documentation on 11 November 2026. Two changes matter against older material: every plan now gets 100 cron jobs per project (since January 2026), and Fluid compute, the default for projects created since April 2025, gives Hobby functions 300 seconds.

| | Hobby | Pro (US$20 a month) |
|---|---|---|
| Cron jobs per project | 100 | 100 |
| Most frequent cron | **Once a day** | Once a minute |
| Cron timing | **Anywhere within the scheduled hour** | Within the scheduled minute |
| Function time limit with Fluid compute | 300 s default and maximum | 300 s default, 800 s maximum, 1,800 s in beta |
| Memory and CPU | 2 GB, 1 vCPU | Up to 4 GB, 2 vCPU |
| Python bundle limit | 500 MB uncompressed | 500 MB |
| Function regions | One, of your choice (Mumbai `bom1` is allowed) | Several |
| Included use each month | 4 CPU-hours, 360 GB-hours of memory, 1 million invocations | US$20 credit |
| Runtime logs kept | 1 hour | 1 day |
| Static outbound IPs | Not available | US$100 a month per project, shared pool |

Sources: [cron usage and pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing); [function limits](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations); [regions](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/configuring-functions/region); [Hobby plan](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby); [limits](https://vercel.com/docs/limits); [Static IPs](https://vercel.com/docs/networking/static-ips). A Hobby cron set for 15:10 IST "will trigger anywhere between" the start and end of that hour, expressions that fire more than once a day fail at deploy, and on every plan "Vercel will not retry an invocation if a cron job fails", delivery is "best effort", and the same run can occasionally be invoked twice ([managing cron jobs](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/manage-cron-jobs)). A cron is a plain HTTP GET to the production deployment, in UTC, with user agent `vercel-cron/1.0` and, when the `CRON_SECRET` environment variable is set, the header `Authorization: Bearer <CRON_SECRET>`; without that check "anyone can call it". Vercel now labels cron jobs a legacy feature. Its replacement, Schedules, has been in public beta since 4 November 2026 and is materially better on Hobby: a schedule may run as often as every 15 minutes, in an IANA timezone such as `Asia/Kolkata`, inside a jitter window that is always 15 minutes, up to 5,000 runs a billing period and with no retries ([Schedules limits](https://vercel.com/docs/schedules/limits); [10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md), which read the pages and the announcement directly). A Hobby schedule of `10 15 * * 1-5` in `Asia/Kolkata` should therefore start between 15:10 and 15:25 IST, an inference from the jitter rule that nobody has tested. Hobby still cannot wake the worker every five minutes or hit 15:10 to the minute, but with Schedules it can land a daily run inside a known quarter-hour, which the legacy cron cannot. The two sessions' sources disagree on where the legacy cron lands. The documentation as read on 10 November says it fires "at any point within the specified hour", which for `40 9 * * 1-5` means 09:00 to 09:59 UTC, or 14:30 to 15:29 IST; this session's implementation notes assume 15:10 to 16:09 IST. The documentation reading is the better evidence, and it matters, because a wake-up that arrives before 15:10 finds the decision not yet due and does nothing. The time limit without Fluid compute could not be confirmed: current pages publish only Fluid figures, and the older Hobby figure of 10 to 60 seconds does not appear in them. The Hobby plan is "restricted to non-commercial personal use" and defines commercial use as any deployment "used for the purpose of financial gain of anyone involved" ([fair use guidelines](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)); every listed example concerns websites taking money from visitors, and whether a personal trading bot falls inside is ambiguous enough to ask Vercel or budget for Pro before real money moves.

A Python function can live in the same project as a Next.js App Router app. A `.py` file in an `api` directory at the project root becomes its own function served at its file path, so `dashboard/api/worker.py` serves `/api/worker` beside the app's own `app/api/*` route handlers ([Python functions in the /api directory](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/python/api-directory)). Vercel documents that layout as supported "for existing projects" and now recommends Services, in beta, for pairing Next.js with a Python backend; no current prose page says outright that Python files in `api/` work in a Next.js project, and the evidence is the builder's source, which says "Other languages (Python, Go, etc) should still go in the `api` directory" ([vercel/vercel builder source](https://github.com/vercel/vercel/blob/main/packages/fs-detectors/src/detect-builders.ts)). Which side wins if a Next.js route and a Python file claim the same path is undocumented, so the two must not collide. The Next.js middleware does run in front of the Python function: the builder places middleware routes ahead of filesystem and function resolution ([builder source](https://github.com/vercel/vercel/blob/main/packages/next/src/server-build.ts)), and Thunderbot's local build confirmed a middleware route that matches every path. Because HTTP Basic Auth and the cron secret both use the single `Authorization` header, the middleware must let a correct bearer token through. Configuration sits in `dashboard/vercel.json`:

```json
{
  "regions": ["bom1"],
  "functions": { "api/worker.py": { "maxDuration": 300 } },
  "crons": [{ "path": "/api/worker", "schedule": "40 9 * * 1-5" }]
}
```

pandas, numpy and yfinance fit but are not needed. Measured unzipped sizes are 4 MB for `requests`, 153 MB for pandas with numpy, 224 MB for yfinance with its dependencies (including a 41 MB `curl_cffi` binary), and 418 MB for those plus scikit-learn, against the 500 MB limit ([Python runtime](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/python); sizes measured by the researcher from PyPI wheels). The earlier report measured 168 MB for the yfinance stack and found in the builder's source a 225 MB threshold above which packages are installed at each cold start, which the documentation does not mention ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)); neither figure binds a 4 MB worker. Thunderbot's worker calls Yahoo's chart endpoint directly and needs only `requests`; a 15-year trend-rotation backtest takes under a second of compute plus one to three seconds to load prices, so on-demand backtests fit comfortably inside 300 seconds.

### Supabase as the scheduler

Supabase Cron is the `pg_cron` extension, and no documentation page restricts it, `pg_net` or Vault by plan; that they work on the free plan is an inference from the absence of a restriction, not an explicit statement. Schedules are five-field cron expressions in UTC with a one-minute minimum, or `'N seconds'` from 1 to 59; Supabase recommends at most eight concurrent jobs of under ten minutes each; and `cron.job_run_details` "is never cleaned up automatically", so a pruning job is required ([Supabase Cron](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/cron); [quickstart](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/cron/quickstart)). `pg_net` can call any outside HTTPS URL on a schedule or from a trigger on row insert. It only queues the request and returns an id; a background worker sends it after the transaction commits, the response is kept for six hours, there are no retries, and the queue lives in unlogged tables that a crash can empty ([pg_net](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/extensions/pg_net)). The default timeout is 2,000 ms in Supabase's page and 5,000 ms in the extension's README, so it must always be passed explicitly; the README bounds it at 600,000 ms ([pg_net README](https://github.com/supabase/pg_net)). For a call on row insert, the documented choice is a Database Webhook or an own trigger function that calls `net.http_post`, and Supabase recommends the latter when a secret must come from Vault, because a webhook "saves the key in its trigger definition" ([Database Webhooks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/webhooks)).

The official SQL for keeping the secret in Vault and reading it at call time is this, verbatim from Supabase's page on scheduling functions ([Scheduling Edge Functions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/schedule-functions)): The earlier report checked the same blocks against the live pages and found them identical word for word ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)).

```sql
select vault.create_secret('https://project-ref.supabase.co', 'project_url');
select vault.create_secret('YOUR_SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY', 'secret_key');

select
  cron.schedule(
    'invoke-function-every-minute',
    '* * * * *', -- every minute
    $$
    select
      net.http_post(
          url:= (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'project_url') || '/functions/v1/function-name',
          headers:=jsonb_build_object(
            'Content-type', 'application/json',
            'apikey', (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'secret_key')
          ),
          body:=concat('{"time": "', now(), '"}')::jsonb
      ) as request_id;
    $$
  );
```

Thunderbot adapts that pattern for a non-Supabase target by putting the Vault secret in an `Authorization: Bearer` header; the adaptation is the repository's own and has run only against a local stand-in. Vault encrypts the secret at rest and in backups, but "any role that can log in to your database can read the secret key while the request waits" in pg_net's queue.

A free project is still paused after a week of low activity. Supabase defines inactivity as too little "user database activity", says "a few user requests to the database each day" usually prevent it though "that isn't a guarantee", and sends a warning email about a week ahead ([project pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)). **Whether pg_cron jobs running inside the database count is not stated anywhere official**, and one experienced community member argues they cannot; a paused project runs no jobs and cannot wake itself. The safe design is real Data API requests from outside every day, including weekends and holidays. The earlier report adds a caution from a November 2026 community thread, in which a free-plan user received a pause warning despite 87 successful keep-alive API calls that touched no table, so the requests should read and write real rows; Supabase Pro, from US$25 a month, is the only guaranteed route ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). The restore window is now one year in the documentation, where older material says 90 days. Free projects get 500 MB of database, shared CPU with 0.5 GB of memory, one-day log retention and no downloadable backups ([billing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/billing-on-supabase)). New projects have only `sb_secret_` keys, which go on the `apikey` header and not in `Authorization: Bearer`, and the legacy keys are due for removal by the end of 2026 ([API keys](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/getting-started/api-keys)).

Edge Functions are the wrong place for the strategy: a Deno runtime with 256 MB of memory, **2 seconds of CPU time per request**, a 150-second wall-clock limit on the free plan, a 20 MB bundle and no Python ([Edge Function limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits)). They can be pinned to Mumbai with an `x-region: ap-south-1` header, and Supabase states that neither they nor the database have static outbound addresses ([static egress note](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/why-supabase-edge-functions-cannot-provide-static-egress-ips-for-whitelisting-3d78b0)).

### Price data from a cloud function

Yahoo's v8 chart endpoint still answered from a cloud host on 11 November 2026 with nothing but a browser User-Agent: no cookie, no crumb, seventeen years of daily bars per request, and twelve symbols in five seconds ([Yahoo chart endpoint](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/NIFTYBEES.NS?period1=0&period2=1794500000&interval=1d&events=div%2Csplits), direct test). The "429 Too Many Requests" that people report was, in that test, a client-fingerprint rejection on the very first request for the default User-Agents of curl, `python-requests` and Deno, not a counted rate limit. Two traps: `range=max` silently returns monthly bars, so requests must use `period1` and `period2`, and the v7 quote endpoint still needs a crumb. That test ran from one research sandbox, not from a Vercel or AWS address. The public record contains one report of the error on AWS Lambda while the same script worked locally, a user blocked for more than 24 hours, and the yfinance maintainer's advice to "stop spamming Yahoo, fetch smarter" ([yfinance issue 2422](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2422); [yfinance discussion 2431](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/discussions/2431)); no confirmed report says Vercel or AWS ranges are blocked wholesale. Yahoo publishes no limit and its terms prohibit automated collection ([Yahoo terms](https://legal.yahoo.com/xw/en/yahoo/terms/otos/index.html), search snippet). The realistic risk is a shared datacenter address that other tenants have already burned, which is why the prices should be cached in Postgres and a second source kept.

### The design as built, and what has not been verified

The repository implements one Python function, `dashboard/api/worker.py`, served at `/api/worker` in region `bom1` with a 300-second limit. `op=tick` runs one pass of the daily worker and exits, `op=job` runs a queued backtest, and `op=health` checks configuration; all state is in Supabase. A Supabase migration creates `thunderbot_wake()`, which reads the worker URL and secret from Vault and queues `net.http_get` with a 290,000 ms timeout, and five pg_cron jobs: every five minutes on weekdays, gated in SQL to 09:15–15:35 IST; a 17:00 IST catch-up; a weekend tick so the project sees real API requests every day; a watchdog at 15:05 and 15:20 IST that posts to ntfy.sh if the heartbeat is more than 20 minutes old; and a nightly prune of the cron history. A Vercel cron at `40 9 * * 1-5` (15:10 IST), which on Hobby can fire up to 59 minutes off the mark, is the backup. Duplicate and late wake-ups are harmless by construction: a `daily_runs` table with a unique key on kind and day makes the second claim fail with HTTP 409, a failed run releases the lock, and a dead one can be taken over after 15 minutes. A paper run that starts late still runs that evening on closing prices and is flagged; a live run that starts after 15:25 IST does not trade and is retried on the next trading day, which the fill-delay backtests show costs these monthly rules little. The middleware admits a request carrying the bearer secret and the function checks it again ([implementation notes](../research_notes/ETF%20rotation%20and%20serverless%20hosting/thunderbot_implementation_checks.md)).

What was checked, all on a development machine on 11 November 2026: `vercel build` with CLI 65.14.0 completed and produced the Python function beside the Next.js routes, with the `thunderbot` package and `requests` vendored in (375 files); the built function returned 401 without credentials, bought the three-ETF mix in paper mode on live Yahoo prices in 2.0 seconds and did nothing on a second tick the same day; the SQL migrations applied on PostgreSQL 16 against stand-in `cron`, `net` and `vault` schemas; and 137 worker tests pass. One build detail is load-bearing: a `requirements.txt` entry of `../worker` failed, and a `pyproject.toml` path source worked.

Set beside the earlier report's design, the build confirms two things that report listed as untested and departs from it in four ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Confirmed locally: the Python builder does bundle a `worker/` package that sits outside the project's root directory, through a `pyproject.toml` path source, and the Next.js middleware does appear in the build output as a route in front of the Python function. Both come from Vercel's CLI on a development machine, not from Vercel's hosted builder. The SQLite-first store that the earlier report flagged has also given way to state held in Supabase. The departures: the function is file-based at `/api/worker`, where the earlier report preferred the Services beta and a distinct `/api/py/` prefix; the middleware admits a bearer token, where the earlier report excluded the path from the matcher; prices are fetched from Yahoo on each run, where the earlier report wanted a Supabase price table seeded from NSE's bhavcopy archive and a second live source; and the backup clock is Vercel's legacy daily cron, where the earlier report recommends Vercel Schedules. The last is the one to change first. On the documentation's wording the legacy cron may arrive before the decision is due, whereas a Schedules entry like the one below should arrive between 15:10 and 15:25 IST. **Switching the backup to Schedules is an open follow-up that was not done, because nothing could be deployed to test a beta configuration**; the snippet is the earlier report's untested template pointed at the implemented path. Vercel's migration page warns that keeping both a `crons` and a `schedules` entry for one path runs the route twice, which the `daily_runs` lock already makes harmless.

```json
{
  "schedules": {
    "daily-decision-backup": { "schedule": "10 15 * * 1-5", "timezone": "Asia/Kolkata", "path": "/api/worker", "requestMethod": "GET" }
  }
}
```

**Nothing was deployed to a real Vercel or Supabase project, because no credentials were available.** The following are therefore not verified, and each needs a real deployment to settle:

## Live orders need an address that only you use

The Groww Trade API costs **₹499 a month or ₹4,999 a year, plus GST**, and a ₹0 tier can place, modify and cancel orders and read holdings but has no live data or historical candles ([Groww blog](https://groww.in/blog/api-trading-on-groww)). Groww's blog, read directly for the earlier report, is explicit that "the difference between the free and paid plans is data access, not order placement", though it calls the plan a "Free Trial" with no stated length ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Access tokens expire daily at 06:00. Groww documents a manual token, a key-and-secret flow that "requires daily approval", and a TOTP flow meant for unattended use, but its two documentation pages contradict each other on the last: the Python SDK page labels TOTP "No Expiry" and the cURL page says it "requires daily approval" ([Python SDK docs](https://groww.in/trade-api/docs/python-sdk); [cURL docs](https://groww.in/trade-api/docs/curl)). If the cURL page is right, a fully unattended bot is not possible on Groww and the fallback is one approval tap each trading morning; this must be tested on the live account. The earlier report read both pages and judged the cURL line a copying slip, with TOTP the only unattended login. Limits are 10 orders a second and 250 a minute, and 150 token requests in 24 hours, none of which a monthly rebalancer approaches. Every API order is treated as an algo order, and "algo orders with order type as Market Order are not permitted" ([NSE FAQ, November 2025](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail_Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf)), so the bot must send limit orders.

SEBI's circular of 4 February 2025 allows API access "only through a unique vendor client specific API key and static IP whitelisted by the broker" ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/feb-2025/1738665456458.pdf)), and after two extensions the framework applied to all brokers from 1 April 2026 ([SEBI circular, September 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf)). NSE's implementation standards supply the detail: one primary address and an optional secondary; changes "not more than once a calendar week"; and **"a static IP can only be mapped to one client at a time"**, with sharing only inside a family ([NSE circular NSE/INVG/67858](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG67858.pdf)). There is no order-count threshold: the ten-orders-a-second line decides only whether an algo must be registered, so one API order a month still needs the whitelisted address. Groww registers the primary and secondary at its API Keys page and says "order placement must originate from a static IP" ([Groww blog](https://groww.in/blog/static-ip-api-trading-setup)); it does not publish its change frequency, IPv6 stance or rejection message, and no search found any relaxation after April 2026. Reads are exempt on the exchange's own wording, which the earlier report read directly: "Client static IP will be required only in case of Tech savvy Investor using API for placing orders" ([NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail_Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf); [10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)). Zerodha says the same of its data, order-book and positions calls, and Groww implies it without stating it. This session's researcher, working from the annexure, had read the exemption as broker practice that could be tightened; the FAQ is the better source, though the annexure does tie the address to API access as a whole. Either way a successful holdings call proves nothing about whether an order will pass.

Sending broker orders through a proxy is not banned as such; what the rule tests is which public address the broker sees and whether it maps to one client. A proxy on a VPS the owner rents passes. A shared pool does not, on regulatory rather than cryptographic grounds: with an HTTPS `CONNECT` tunnel the proxy cannot read the token or the order, but Vercel itself says "other customers' traffic can come from the same addresses", QuotaGuard warns a shared address can be rejected if another customer registered it with the same broker, and Zerodha's staff say surveillance may suspend API access where unrelated accounts share an address ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/50587/), search snippet). Zerodha's FAQ, read for the earlier report, accepts a static IP from "an ISP, a cloud provider (such as AWS or GCP), or a VPN/VPC service", so routing as such is allowed; no regulator text rules on shared proxy pools by name, and that part of the verdict is an inference from the one-client-per-address rule. One option in the earlier report is absent from this session's notes and may matter most: Groww Cloud, the script runner inside Groww's own API console, included in the ₹0 plan. Nothing read says whether a script deployed there is exempt from the client static-IP requirement, and NSE's FAQ points both ways; a logged-in look costs nothing and could remove the problem ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)).

Without a static IP the following stays impossible: placing, modifying or cancelling any order through the Groww API, including smart orders, from Vercel, Supabase, GitHub Actions or a laptop on an ordinary dynamic connection. Everything else stays possible: backtests, paper trading, signal generation on Vercel and Supabase, alerts, and by broker practice reading holdings and positions. Manual entry in the Groww app is a separate, non-algo channel outside the rule. That is the design Thunderbot implements: the serverless function runs paper trading and shows the exact orders for the owner to enter by hand, and live API orders are confined to a machine whose public address is the one registered with Groww, behind three explicit opt-ins. For four to seventeen orders a year, a static address buys only the removal of that one manual step.

## Fallback runners: precise schedulers are free, fixed addresses are not

No free serverless platform gives a fixed outgoing address; only an always-on VM or a paid NAT does. For timing, the options split cleanly, and the ranking matches the earlier report's, which read GitHub's and Google's pricing pages directly ([10 November report](Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting.md)).

| Runner | Cost a month | Fixed outgoing IP | Timing | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supabase pg_cron and pg_net (already in the stack) | ₹0 | No | To the minute | Fire-and-forget; free project can pause |
| GitHub Actions `schedule` | ₹0 | No | Poor: documented delays and drops | 5-minute minimum; auto-disabled after 60 idle days in public repositories |
| GitHub Actions via `workflow_dispatch` from pg_cron | ₹0 | No | Good once called; pickup time unmeasured | Needs a token stored in Vault |
| Cloud Run job with Cloud Scheduler | ₹0 within the free tier | Paid add-on | Good: `Asia/Kolkata` schedules, retries | Billing account required; retries default to zero |
| Oracle Always Free VM | ₹0 | **Yes** | To the second (cron or systemd) | Reclamation and termination anecdotes |
| Cloudflare Workers cron | ₹0 | No | Good, UTC only | 10 ms of CPU on the free plan; Python support for `requests` disputed |
| Laptop | ₹0 | Only with an ISP static IP | Exact when awake | Sleep, lid, battery, network |

GitHub's own documentation says the schedule event "can be delayed during periods of high loads", that "some queued jobs may be dropped", and that the shortest interval is five minutes; since March 2026 schedules accept an IANA `timezone` such as `Asia/Kolkata` ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/events-that-trigger-workflows)). One 2026 comparison measured its median scheduled run about 26 minutes late ([josephhall.org](https://josephhall.org/blog/small-bake-off/), search snippet). Public repositories run free and a private one gets US$12 of compute a month, about 2,000 Linux minutes ([GitHub billing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)); runner addresses change weekly and GitHub advises against allow-listing them. Cloud Scheduler gives three free jobs per billing account and tz-database time zones ([Cloud Scheduler pricing](https://cloud.google.com/scheduler/pricing), search snippet). Oracle halved its free Arm allowance to 2 OCPUs and 12 GB in mid-2026 and may reclaim instances idle over seven days unless the account is converted to pay-as-you-go ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/07/oracle-cloud-free-tier-limits/), search snippet). Cloudflare's free plan allows five cron triggers but 10 ms of CPU, so it suits a tiny pinger and not a Python job ([Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/), search snippet). Thunderbot's `thunderbot tick` command runs one pass and exits, so any of these can call it against the same Supabase state.

On a laptop the dependable pattern is the same on all three systems: a scheduler that catches up after sleep, a second trigger ten minutes later, and the worker's own cutoff. On Windows, `Register-ScheduledTask` with `-WakeToRun -StartWhenAvailable -AllowStartIfOnBatteries` and weekday triggers at 15:10 and 15:20 is the right tool, since plain `schtasks` cannot set those options; wake timers must be enabled in the power plan, and forum reports show that laptops using Modern Standby often ignore them on battery or with the lid closed ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/scheduledtasks/new-scheduledtasksettingsset); [Dell community](https://www.dell.com/community/Latitude/Wake-Timers-and-Task-Schedules-do-not-trigger-in-S0-Power-State/m-p/8310145), search snippets). On macOS a LaunchAgent with `StartCalendarInterval` is right because, "unlike cron which skips job invocations when the computer is asleep, launchd will start the job the next time the computer wakes up" ([launchd.plist manual](https://opensource.apple.com/source/launchd/launchd-258.1/launchd/src/launchd.plist.5.auto.html), search snippet), with `sudo pmset repeat wakeorpoweron MTWRF 15:05:00` to wake it; scheduled wake on a closed-lid Apple Silicon laptop on battery is unconfirmed. On Linux a systemd timer with `OnCalendar=Mon..Fri *-*-* 15:10:00 Asia/Kolkata`, `Persistent=true` and `AccuracySec=1s` is exact and catches up, and `WakeSystem=true` in a system-level timer can wake a suspended machine where the hardware cooperates, which bug reports show is not always ([systemd.timer manual](https://manpages.debian.org/systemd.timer); [ArchWiki](https://wiki.archlinux.org/title/Systemd/Timers), search snippets). These commands were composed from documented syntax and not executed in this research. All three schedulers will run a missed 15:10 job whenever the lid next opens, which is why the worker refuses to trade live after 15:25 IST, claims the day atomically in Supabase, and why the watchdog that alerts by 15:20 runs in Supabase and not on the laptop. A laptop is a fine primary only for someone at the desk at 15:10.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this session adds is a demonstration more than a number. Two independent engines on two data sources agree on every rotation variant to within half a point, and sixteen more days of prices were enough to turn rotation's half-point lead over buy-and-hold into a half-point deficit. Both facts point the same way: the measurements are sound and the edge was never there. Thunderbot's value is therefore not a rule that outsmarts the market, because none of the rules tested across two reports, published or home-grown, did that after Indian tax; it is that the bot holds a sensible mix, rebalances it on schedule, refuses to trade on corrupt prices, and takes the owner's hand off the decision. On that view the strategy choice is a choice of temperament, between a mix that earns more and falls further and a rotation that earns less and steps aside, and the plain one-third mix of equity, gold and a liquid ETF, which matched rotation's shallow fall with no timing at all, deserves a place in the dashboard beside them. The same logic disposes of the intraday, LLM and ML ambition: every strong result found was a pre-cost backtest, the careful tests were negative, and the bot's own ML experiment showed in one table how an apparent edge of one to two points a year becomes a deficit once it is taxed.

On hosting, the earlier report's paper design is now code that builds and passes its tests, and the constraint that matters is unchanged: not compute, memory or schedule, which the free tiers cover, but identity. Indian regulation ties an API order to an address that belongs to one person, and serverless platforms are built on shared addresses, which makes "compute anywhere, alert the owner, enter the order by hand" the natural design for a strategy that trades a handful of times a year. The remaining work is a short list of things neither session could do from a sandbox. Deploy once to Vercel and Supabase and clear the not-verified table. Move the backup clock from the legacy cron to Schedules and watch where both land. Seed a price table from NSE's bhavcopy archive, which the earlier report called the first engineering task and which would also bring MID150BEES back within reach. Log Yahoo's answers from Vercel's Mumbai addresses at 15:10 for a week. Read one contract note for Groww's charges on an ETF and a liquid ETF sale. And take one logged-in look at Groww's TOTP login and at Groww Cloud, the only option found that might make the static address unnecessary.
