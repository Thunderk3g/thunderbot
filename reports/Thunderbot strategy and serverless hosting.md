# Rotate ETFs, rent momentum, drop the server

Thunderbot's best realistic upgrade is modest. Replace the single Nifty 200-day switch with a four-sleeve month-end trend rotation (NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, MID150BEES and GOLDBEES, each held only while above its own 200-day average, idle sleeves parked in the growth-type liquid ETF LIQUIDCASE), and hold momentum through a Nifty200 Momentum 30 fund instead of picking 15 stocks. In this session's own single-run backtest on exchange closing prices the rotation returned **9.3% a year after Groww costs and FY 2026-27 tax over 2011–2026 with an 18% worst fall**, against 6.6–7.4% for the reader's rule on the same engine and 8.8% for simply holding NIFTYBEES. That lead is fragile: **about half of it since 2011, and all of it since 2019, came from gold**, and a plain equal-weight mix of the same four ETFs rebalanced once a year returned more (11.7%) with a deeper fall (27.5%, and 49% in 2008). The backtests were not re-run after a check against fund NAVs on 10 November 2026, and that check trims the case further: idle cash now earns about 5% and not the 6% assumed, buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES was understated by about 0.3 points a year, closing prices hide about 2 points of each panic-day fall, and the Nifty's 10% rebound between 26 October and 9 November, which the rule sat out, roughly cancels the rotation's lead over buy-and-hold. The planning ranges that follow from all this are about **5.5–9% a year after costs and tax with a worst fall of 20–33% for the rotation**, and about 7–12% a year for a momentum fund held through falls of 30% in an ordinary correction and 65–70% in a 2008-type crash; neither is a promise. Rotation buys smaller drawdowns, not higher returns, and the published global record says the same while showing returns roughly halving after publication. Real momentum funds have earned about half their pre-launch premium (+2.8 points a year over the Nifty 200 in 2021–2025 against +5.6 in the 2006–2019 backtest); UTI's fund fell 31.5% in 2024–25 and was still 20% below its peak on 9 November 2026; a self-run 15-stock book needs 1–3.5 points of extra gross return a year just to tie a fund after tax, and honest point-in-time tests show 0–2. The bot can run with no server: Supabase's pg_cron and pg_net wake a Python function in the same Vercel project to the minute, and Vercel Schedules, in public beta since 4 November 2026, now lets the free Hobby plan fire the same function inside a 15-minute window in Indian time, which makes it a usable backup where Hobby's legacy daily cron, firing anywhere within its hour, was not. What stays impossible without a broker-registered static IP is placing, changing or cancelling Groww orders by API, where exchange rules also bar market orders; with two to four orders a month, the bot should decide and notify and the owner should place a limit order in the Groww app. Evidence quality is uneven but better than in the first draft: on 10 November 2026 the Vercel, Supabase, GitHub, Google Cloud pricing, Groww, SEBI, NSE and Zerodha pages were read directly and the return tables were rebuilt from fund NAVs, while the global strategy literature, the tax statutes, fund costs and sizes and most fixed-IP vendors still rest on search summaries, and every backtest is one unreviewed run.

Four of the reader's own assumptions do not survive checking. First, the 8.5% result for the 200-day rule **already assumes idle cash earns 6% a year**: the project's backtest note says so and the job runner defaults `cash_yield_pct` to 6.0 ([Thunderbot backtests](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20strategies/thunderbot_backtests.md); `worker/thunderbot/jobs.py`). Re-run with cash at 0%, the same rule returns **5.3–6.4% after costs, not 8.5%** [computed] ([rotation notes](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/computed_rotation_backtests.md)). A liquid ETF therefore does not add two points on top of 8.5%; it is what makes the backtested figure reachable. Second, **equity ETF units pay STT of 0.001% on the sell side only**, and gold, liquid and gilt ETFs pay none ([Groww help](https://groww.in/help/stocks/sx-pricing/what-is-stt); [Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/stt-etfs)) [doc]. The daily backtester already models this (`etf_sell_stt_pct` in `worker/thunderbot/daily.py`), but the general cost module (`worker/thunderbot/costs.py`) and the earlier cost note apply the share rate of 0.1% on both legs, which is 200 times too high for ETF units. Third, the worker does not keep all its state in Supabase: `worker/thunderbot/store.py` treats a local SQLite file as the source of truth and Supabase as a best-effort mirror, which cannot work on an ephemeral serverless filesystem. Fourth, the momentum ETF tickers differ from the brief: `MOMENTUM` is Aditya Birla Sun Life's ETF, ICICI's is `MOM30IETF` and Kotak's is `MOMENTUM30` ([ABSL AMC](https://mutualfund.adityabirlacapital.com/empower/Passive-ETFs/Nifty-200-Momentum-30-ETF.html); [TradingView](https://in.tradingview.com/symbols/NSE-MOM30IETF/analysis); [Kotak factsheet](https://www.kotakmf.com/factsheet/sept_2026/kotak/Download_pdf/Kotak%20Nifty200%20Momentum%2030%20ETF_Factsheet%20One%20Pager.pdf)) [summary]. The ICICI mapping is confirmed by ISIN against the fund's NAV record ([mfapi 150455](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/150455)) [computed]; the other two were not re-checked.

## A four-ETF trend rotation beat the 200-day rule by two points after tax, and gold supplied half of it

### Published rules kept their drawdown control and lost about half their return

All figures in this subsection are [summary]. Faber's tactical allocation paper showed about **10.5% a year against 9.0% for buy-and-hold over 1973–2012, with a worst fall of 12% against 45%**, before tax ([EdgeTools](https://edgetools.pages.dev/portfolio-models/gtaa5-live)). A vendor's replication of the same five-asset rule over January 2006 to March 2025, the period after publication, found **6.05% a year with an 11.7% worst fall** ([Concretum Group](https://concretumgroup.com/global-tactical-asset-allocation/)). Real money did worse than the replication. The ETF Cambria ran on the strategy lost its sub-advisor in 2014 and was liquidated in May 2017 ([ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/2017/05/advisorshares-to-close-active-etfs-give-gtaa/)), and Cambria's successor trend fund GMOM has compounded at **5.69% a year from November 2014 to March 2026 with a 25% worst fall**, against a 10-year figure of 3.52% a year beside 11.24% for the S&P 500 ([Cambria fact sheet](https://www.cambriafunds.com/assets/docs/GMOM-FactSheet.pdf); [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/symbol/GMOM)).

Antonacci's dual momentum followed the same path. The book's gross backtest showed **17.4% a year against 9.6% for world equities over 1974–2013** with a worst fall near 23% ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/a-few-notes-on-dual-momentum-investing)). The one quantified post-publication test found returns at **about a third of the pre-publication level from 2014 to March 2023, with a drawdown as deep as the S&P 500's** ([Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=68274)). ReSolve found the published 12-month look-back "regressed to the median" of 1,226 equally reasonable variants out of sample ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/05/06/global-equity-momentum-a-craftsmans-perspective)). That critic's test is a single vendor simulation with no independent replication, so treat its size as indicative.

AQR's century study is the strongest positive evidence, at **11.2% a year net over 1880–2013**, but it describes a leveraged long-short futures strategy across 67 markets ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). A long-only switch among Indian equity ETFs and gold has two effective bets and cannot profit from falls. The live record of professional trend funds is lumpy: the SG Trend Index earned **4.9% a year from 2000 to May 2025**, gained 27% in 2022, then posted its worst-ever 12-month loss of 18.7% to April 2025 ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/is-this-time-different); [AdvisorAnalyst](https://advisoranalyst.com/?p=167192)). The sharpest academic caution is Huang, Li, Wang and Zhou, who find that time-series momentum profits are "virtually the same" as a strategy that simply holds assets with positive long-run returns ([WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/time-series-momentum-is-it-there/)). A study of real tactical allocation mutual funds over 1994–2016 found they underperformed every benchmark ([USM repository](https://aquila.usm.edu/fac_pubs/20187)).

### Indian published evidence is thin, gross of tax and drawn from a gold bull market

The Indian results found are blog and forum backtests, all [summary], none live, none after costs or tax. The best known shows a Nifty-or-gold dual momentum rule earning **18.8% a year against 12.1% for the Nifty and 10.9% for gold over 2014–2024**, with an 11.4% worst fall ([Momentum Lab](https://momentumlab.substack.com/p/backtesting-dual-momentum-strategy)). A forum version on NIFTYBEES and GOLDBEES shows 16.1% over about 14 years ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/dual-momentum-strategy-with-niftybees-goldbees/174470)). Freefincal's own caveat on its timing backtests is that they "do not factor in future market movements, human emotions, taxation and exit loads" ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/investment-strategy-analysis-idfc-dynamic-equity-fund/)). No public Capitalmind rotation backtest with returns and drawdowns was found, and Weekend Investing and PrimeInvestor could not be researched at all, so this report says nothing about them. The project's earlier test adds one India-specific warning: a 12-month absolute-momentum signal on the Nifty earned only **4.4% a year from July 2008 to October 2026**, because the V-shaped recoveries of 2009 and 2020 punish slow signals ([earlier notes](../research_notes/Evidence%20based%20NSE%20trading%20strategies/momentum_factor_evidence.md)).

### Own backtest: the rotation beats the rule, and a passive mix beats the rotation

Because nothing published is usable, the rotation was tested directly. Everything in this subsection is [computed] from [NSEDATA4ME](https://github.com/ancap97/NSEDATA4ME) exchange closing prices to 26 October 2026 ([rotation notes](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/computed_rotation_backtests.md)). The "four sleeves" rule puts 25% in each of NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, a midcap leg and GOLDBEES when that sleeve's signal closes above its 200-day average at month-end, and holds the sleeve in a liquid ETF otherwise. "Dual momentum" holds the two strongest of the four by average 6- and 12-month return, each only if above its 200-day average. Costs follow Groww's schedule with ETF STT at 0.001%, slippage of 5–10 basis points a side, and tax at 20.8% short-term and 13% long-term on equity ETFs, slab rate (31.2%) on short-term gold and on the liquid ETF. Cash earns a flat 6%, which the NAV check described under the table shows is fair for 2011–2026 as a whole and too high for 2019–2026. The midcap leg is synthetic before February 2011 and a price index after, so 2008–2010 midcap results are approximate.

The first is year-specific cash returns. A growth liquid ETF would have earned roughly an overnight fund's return less 0.2–0.35 points: about 5.8–5.9% a year over 2011–2026, so the flat 6% is right to within 0.2; about 4.7–4.85% over 2019–2026, so it is 1.1–1.3 points too high; and about 6.3–6.5% over 2008–2018, so it is 0.3–0.5 too low. The rules sit in cash 24–35% of the time, so the likely effect is 0.3–0.4 points a year off the 2019–2026 column for both the rotation and the reader's rule, 0.1–0.2 added to 2008–2018, and less than 0.1 either way over 2011–2026. The engine's own run with the overnight index from mid-2016 had already shown 0.2 off the rotation and 0.3 off the rule for 2019–2026. The second is distributions before 2016. NIFTYBEES's NAV fell 0.9–1.9% against a Nifty 50 index fund on six single days between 2008 and 2015, the pattern of cash payouts, and the price data do not add them back. Restoring them lifts buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES from 9.5% to 9.8% a year before costs over 2011–2026 and from 5.6% to 6.4% over 2008–2018. The reader's rule, fully in NIFTYBEES when invested, gains most of that; the rotation, with a quarter of its money there, gains little. The payouts are inferred from NAV behaviour and not confirmed from an AMC notice. The third is drawdowns measured on NAV. ETFs closed 1.5–3.8% above NAV on 23 March 2020, so NIFTYBEES's worst fall since 2011 is 38.4% on NAV against the 36.3% shown, and JUNIORBEES's is 40.5% against 38.7%; every worst-fall figure that bottoms in a panic is probably about 2 points too shallow. Taken together the three leave the rotation ahead of the reader's rule, by a slightly smaller margin, and narrow its lead over simply holding NIFTYBEES.

Three results matter. The four-sleeve rotation beat the reader's rule after costs and tax by **1.6 to 2.7 points a year in every sub-period**, with a similar or shallower worst fall. The dual-momentum version added more return but fell 25–37%, deeper than the reader's rule in every period. And **the passive equal-weight mix out-returned both rotations in most periods**; the rotation's contribution was cutting the worst fall from 27.5% to 18% since 2011 and from 49% to about 21% in 2008. The reader's rule also needs restating. The earlier comparison set the rule's after-tax 8.1% against a pre-tax 9.5% for buy-and-hold; the fair benchmark is NIFTYBEES after tax at 8.8% (about 9.1% once the inferred pre-2016 distributions described above are added back), and the rule still trailed it in all 30 specifications tested over 2011–2026.

Account size barely matters for ETFs. Costs took about 0.6 points a year from the rotation at ₹1 lakh, 0.4 at ₹5 lakh and 0.3 at ₹20 lakh, while tax drag ran the other way (0.3, 0.7 and 1.1 points) because the ₹1.25 lakh exemption covers less of a larger account's gains.

### The day of the month moved the reader's rule more than the choice of rule did

Timing luck is the largest single source of dispersion [computed]. Moving the reader's decision from month-end to the 15th trading day cut its 2011–2026 return from **7.6% to 3.9% and deepened its worst fall from 18% to 43%**, because a mid-month check sat through most of March 2020. Across 30 near-equivalent specifications (five decision days, three look-backs, two buffers) the rule's after-tax return ranged from 3.0% to 8.4% with a median of 6.0%; the reader's month-end, 200-day, 1% buffer choice sits near the top. The four-sleeve rotation was steadier, at **7.3% to 9.8% with a median of 8.9%**, because four signals rarely flip on the same day, though its worst fall still reached 31% with a mid-month decision. The rotation beat the rule in all 30 matched specifications in every period; the no-gold rotation won in only 19 of 30 over 2019–2026. This matches the global finding that shifting Faber's rule by a few days moved its worst fall from about 19% to about 30% ([Newfound Research](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2013/08/the-luck-of-the-rebalance-timing/)) [summary].

Two smaller findings shape implementation. Trading at the next day's close instead of the decision day's close cost 0.3–1.4 points a year, so a 15:10 decision executed before 15:30 is worth keeping. And the 1% buffer had no consistent sign: it helped the rule in 2008–2018 and hurt it in 2019–2026.

At the end of the data every trend variant was fully in cash from 30 September 2026, and the Nifty 50 closed about 4% below its 200-day average on 30 October on a third-party file not cross-checked against NSE, so the reader's rule stayed out; the other sleeves' state on that day was not computed. The Nifty then rose about 10% between 26 October and 9 November, which fund NAVs confirm: **NIFTYBEES +10.1%, JUNIORBEES +9.5%, MID150BEES +7.7%** [computed]. That is the rule working as designed, and it is what lagging a V-shaped recovery feels like. It also means every "to 26 October" figure in this report was struck at a local low that favours the trend rules. To 9 November, NIFTYBEES is down 3.2% for 2026 and not 12.2%, and its NAV has compounded at 10.1% a year since 2011 and 12.5% since 2019, not 9.5% and 11.1%. A rule sitting in cash earned almost nothing over those days, so the rotation's after-tax lead of half a point a year over buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES since 2011 is, by this arithmetic and not by a re-run, roughly gone.

## Five ETFs are liquid enough to trade; momentum, gilt and overseas ETFs are not

### The instrument list

"First trade" is the first date each ticker appears in NSE bhavcopy data and median traded value is NSE-only for the 12 months to 26 October 2026, both [computed] from [NSEDATA4ME](https://github.com/ancap97/NSEDATA4ME). ### Liquid ETFs: use the growth kind

LIQUIDBEES declares a dividend every day so that its NAV falls back to ₹1,000, and credits the income as new units ([Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2016/04/premium-understanding-liquid-bees-an-etf-for-safe-parking-of-funds-and-to-optimize-margins/)) [summary]. Its price stayed between ₹969.64 and ₹1,006.53 across the whole data set, its NAV is ₹1,000.0000 on every date and Yahoo quotes it at a flat 1,000 [computed], so a price-only backtest shows zero return and a live holding drifts as units arrive. Fractional units cannot be sold on the exchange and must be redeemed off-market ([Zerodha Support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/mutual-funds/understanding-mutual-funds/articles/redeeming-fractional-units)), each dividend is taxable income with 10% TDS above ₹10,000 a year ([Quicko](https://learn.quicko.com/section-194k-tds-dividend-mutual-funds)), and brokers have struggled to report it correctly ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/inaccurate-capital-gains-for-liquidbees-in-zerodha-tax-p-l/169419)) [summary].

LIQUIDCASE and the other growth-type ETFs pay no dividend: the price itself climbs a paisa or two a day, the unit count never changes, and nothing is taxed until sale ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/liquidcase-vs-liquidbees/159606)) [summary]. A backtest on these prices also overstates returns, because the price history contains the premium: MON100 returned 56.8% on price in 2024 against 28.6% on NAV.

### Practical traps

Three traps deserve code. First, unit splits: Nippon split NIFTYBEES 10-for-1 and GOLDBEES 100-for-1 with a record date of 20 December 2019 and ex-date of 19 December ([NSE circular CML42888](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CML42888.pdf); [Trendlyne](https://trendlyne.com/equity/Split/GOLDBEES/1582/nippon-india-etf-gold-bees-split)) [summary]. The raw NAVs confirm both ratios, stepping from ₹1,300.31 to ₹129.94 and from ₹3,358.64 to ₹33.73 between 20 and 23 December [computed]. The bhavcopy-derived series used here is correctly adjusted across those dates and agrees with back-adjusted NAV within 0.3% on every day from 17 to 23 December 2019. Yahoo's series, tested directly on 10 November 2026, are back-adjusted but **each carries two corrupt bars: NIFTYBEES.NS shows 13.02 and 13.03 on 19 and 20 December 2019 between closes near 129, and GOLDBEES.NS shows 0.336 on both days between closes near 33.6**; Yahoo returns no split event for either ([direct checks](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/verification_direct_checks.md)) [computed]. Left alone, those bars create a false 90% or 99% crash and rebound in any 200-day average or drawdown. The bot must repair them, by multiplying the two NIFTYBEES bars by 10 and the two GOLDBEES bars by 100 or by replacing them from the bhavcopy series, and should block trading on any one-day move beyond −50% with no matching index move. Second, ETF closes misprice on panic days [computed]: JUNIORBEES fell 1.3% on 4 June 2024 when its index fell 9.3%; MID150BEES rose 15.8% on 19 March 2020 when its index fell 4.0%; and on 23 March 2020 NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES and MID150BEES closed 3.45%, 3.79% and 1.48% above NAV. Signals and drawdowns computed on ETF prices inherit that noise. Third, premiums: silver ETFs were reported 5.7% to 15% above fair value in October 2025 during a physical shortage ([Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/markets/silver-etf-prices-hit-record-kotak-mutual-fund-puts-fresh-investments-on-hold-articleshow-c4rbhio); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/why-mfs-are-freezing-silver-etf-investments-and-what-it-means-for-you-125101400231_1.html)) [summary]. That range was not reproduced for SILVERBEES on a close-to-NAV basis, where the largest gap that month was +3.0% and the largest ever +5.4% on 26 December 2025, so the reports may describe other funds, intraday prices or an international fair value. The fall that followed is confirmed: SILVERBEES's price fell 23.2% on 2 February 2026 and its NAV 25.0%, or 33.8% over three sessions ([mfapi 149758](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/149758)) [computed]. A rule that buys what rose last month buys silver exactly when its premium is widest.

The ETF price-band framework also changed in 2026, and its terms are now read from NSE's own circular [doc]. From **7 September 2026**, after a SEBI extension on 28 August, the base price is the ETF's own previous-day closing price, defined as the volume-weighted average of the last 30 minutes, falling back to the day's last traded price and then to the latest closing NAV ([NSE/CMTR/76101](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR76101.pdf); [NSE/CMTR/76041](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR76041.zip)). The first draft gave one band of 10% from 1 September; the band in fact depends on the type of ETF. **Equity and debt ETFs start at 10% and flex by 5% after a cooling-off period, up to 20%. Gold and silver ETFs start at 6% and flex by 3%. Overnight and liquid ETFs have a fixed 5% band.** The circular's extracted text prints these as plus figures, read here as bands either side of the base price. Gold and silver ETFs also get a pre-open session. Because the base is the ETF's own last price and not NAV, nothing mechanical stops a premium building over successive days, so the bot should keep its limit prices inside the day's band and refuse any buy more than a set margin above indicative NAV.

Under the Income-tax Act 2025 the equity provisions are reported as sections 196 and 198, the general long-term provision as 197 and the debt-fund rule as section 76 ([ebizfiling](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/section-196-and-198-short-term-and-long-term-capital-gains/)) [summary].

Four consequences follow for a monthly rotation. Nearly every exit is short-term, so the asset class sets the rate: a ₹10,000 gain costs ₹2,080 in an equity ETF and ₹3,120 in GOLDBEES at the top slab, and the backtester must apply the rate by instrument. The ₹1.25 lakh exemption applies only to long-term equity gains, so a rotation almost never uses it. Under the new regime the section 87A rebate cannot be used against the 20% and 12.5% rates since FY 2025-26 ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-87a-rebate-applicable-for-capital-gains-after-budget-2025/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/section-87a-rebate-stcg-111a-tax-planning-fy-2025-26.html)). It does shelter gains taxed at slab rates, which Freefincal states for debt-fund gains in FY 2025-26, with the catch that the ₹12 lakh threshold counts special-rate income too ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/how-are-debt-fund-gains-taxed-in-fy-2025-26/)) [doc]; extending that to short-term gold ETF gains and to FY 2026-27 is still an inference. And at today's liquid ETF yield of about 5%, the cash leg nets about 3.4% at the 30% slab, 4.0% at 20% and 4.7% at 5%.

Short-term losses offset any capital gain, long-term losses offset only long-term gains, and unused losses carry forward eight years if the return is filed on time ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/glossary/capital-loss-carryover)). One conflict is moot here: sources disagree on gold ETF units bought between April 2023 and March 2025, but units bought now are outside it on every reading. One residual risk is not moot: CBDT Circular 6/2016 protects capital-gains treatment only for holdings over 12 months ([circular text](https://finodha.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/CBDT-Circular-No.-62016.pdf)), so a monthly rotator relies on facts and consistency, not a safe harbour. The reclassification risk for a few delivery ETF trades a month with own money looks low but is not zero.

Groww bills ETFs like shares apart from STT, and the schedule below was read on Groww's pricing and help pages on 10 November 2026 [doc]. Brokerage is the lower of ₹20 or 0.1% per executed order with a ₹5 minimum, itself capped at 2.5% of the trade, which matters only below ₹200 an order ([Groww Pricing](https://groww.in/pricing); [Groww help](https://groww.in/help/stocks/searchable/how-much-is-the-brokerage)). The DP charge is ₹20 plus GST (₹23.60), made up of ₹3.50 for the depository and ₹16.50 for Groww. The first draft called it a charge per ETF per sell day; Groww's help page says it is charged **per sell order**: "Selling 5 units of Stock A in two separate orders → 2 DP charges" ([Groww help](https://groww.in/help/stocks/searchable/are-dp-charges-per-stock-or-per-order)). Groww waives its ₹16.50 when the sale is worth less than ₹100. ### An honest backtest is now free, and it shows survivorship adds 8–10 points a year

A hobbyist can rebuild point-in-time data in about a day plus a weekend, from pieces inspected directly this session ([factor notes](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/factor_indices_survivorship_diy_vs_etf.md)). NSE's own inclusion and exclusion workbook, `IndexInclExcl.xls`, lists every change to the Nifty 50 from 1996, Nifty 100 from 2003, Nifty 200 from November 2011 and Nifty 500 from 1998, up to July–September 2020, by company name ([copy in a public repo](https://github.com/rishabhjain0295-web/nifty500-momentum-backtest)). After 2020 the changes come from archived constituent lists and the half-yearly press releases. The project [nsepit](https://github.com/NegativeZone/nsepit) offers validated point-in-time Nifty 50 membership from 2005; an equal-weight portfolio built from it tracked NSE's official equal-weight index within 0.5 points a year. For prices, [NSEDATA4ME](https://github.com/ancap97/NSEDATA4ME) publishes a split- and bonus-adjusted bhavcopy database from 2005 with **4,583 securities, of which 1,037 are delisted or inactive**, including DHFL, Ranbaxy, HDFC Ltd and Satyam. It does not adjust for dividends, rights or small demergers, and 409 implausible one-day jumps had to be neutralised. Yahoo and broker APIs do not carry delisted names. One reconstruction, [BKKB20/nse-index-history](https://github.com/BKKB20/nse-index-history), is unusable: its "2004" Nifty 100 contains companies that listed a decade later.

Measured directly on that data [computed], using today's list instead of point-in-time membership added **7.7 points a year to an equal-weight Nifty 50 portfolio over 2011–2026** (16.4% against 8.7%) and **9.1 points to an equal-weight Nifty 100 since June 2013** (21.2% against 12.1% for NSE's official equal-weight index). For momentum the inflation was larger: a top-15 quarterly rule returned 20.4% on today's Nifty 50 list and **10.3% on point-in-time membership**. On honest membership the same rule beat simple equal weighting by −0.4 to +1.9 points a year, not the 5–7 points the reader's control suggested. The cause is index churn: 24 of the 50 Nifty members of January 2011 have since left, and those 24 had a median return of −0.1% a year. Published estimates are far smaller, at 4.94 points for Indian small caps ([Ranse, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380)), and the commonly quoted US fund figure of about 1 point is [recalled]. The momentum test used point-in-time Nifty 50 membership, not Nifty 100, which remains to be built.

### No account size from ₹1 lakh to ₹20 lakh makes 15 stocks beat the fund

At the same gross return, a momentum fund beats a self-run 15-stock book by roughly **2–4 points a year at ₹1 lakh, 1.5–3 at ₹5 lakh and 0.5–2.5 at ₹20 lakh** [computed from the Groww tariff and tax rules]. Fixed charges explain the small-account gap: quarterly turnover of 120–170% costs 1.1–1.6% a year at ₹1 lakh on Groww, 0.6–0.9% at ₹5 lakh and 0.45–0.65% at ₹20 lakh, and the ₹5,899 annual API fee (₹4,999 plus 18% GST) adds 5.9%, 1.2% and 0.3% if the paid plan is taken; Groww says its ₹0 plan also places orders, so a bot that takes its prices elsewhere may avoid that fee. Tax explains the rest and does not shrink with size. A fund rebalances its 100–135% annual churn without tax and the holder pays 12.5% only on exit, often nothing inside the ₹1.25 lakh exemption; the DIY investor pays 20.8% on most gains every year. The reader's own backtests showed that drag at 1.9–3.4 points a year.

So the question "at what capital does DIY win" has no answer in rupees. Fixed costs stop mattering by ₹10–20 lakh, but the DIY book then still needs **+1 to +3.5 points a year of gross return over the Momentum 30 index** to tie, and the evidence for that is absent: the index's own live edge over the Nifty 200 is +0.4 to +2.8, NSE found quarterly rebalancing worse than half-yearly (17.1% against 19.2% since 2005, backtested; [NSE whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf)), and the point-in-time test shows 0–2 points. The fund's weaknesses are real, namely up to about 1% a year of tracking difference (0.0–0.5 points by calendar year for UTI's direct plan and 0.5–0.9 for ICICI's ETF on this session's NAV check) and about ₹8,000 crore of one-way trading forced onto pre-announced dates ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/nifty200-momentum30-a-flip-of-index-switch-jolts-16k-cr-across-the-grid-125121400388_1.html)) [summary]. It is the better vehicle for the same strategy, not a promise of the backtested return.

### A trend filter on momentum helps only if it follows the market, and tax eats part of it

New tests [computed, price indices, month-end check, cash at 6.5%] give a mixed answer, and the cash rate flatters the filter: overnight funds earned about 5.0% a year over 2019–2026 and a liquid ETF about 4.7–4.85%, so with a quarter or more of the time in cash the filtered returns since 2020 are overstated by roughly 0.4–0.5 points a year. These tests were not re-run. Switching the live Momentum 30 index to cash whenever the **Nifty 50** closed below its 200-day average raised the 2020–2026 return from 14.5% to 19.5% and cut the worst fall from 31.8% to 18.1%; after tax it was 16.8% against 13.0%. That is six years and nine switches, and the same rule **cost Alpha 50 about three points a year after tax over thirteen years** and cost a point-in-time momentum portfolio 1.6 points a year after tax over 2011–2026 while cutting its worst fall from 38.8% to 16.2%. Filtering on the momentum index's **own** 200-day average was worse in every test: 6.0% against 8.7% on Momentum 30 since July 2021, with a deeper drawdown. Tax adds 0.6–1.2 points a year to the cost of filtering, because each exit crystallises short-term gains and resets the holding clock. The filter's return benefit comes almost entirely from 2008, when it lifted the point-in-time portfolio from 11.3% to 20.4% a year over 2006–2010. It is insurance against a 60% fall, with a real premium.

## No live evidence shows an LLM adding return at this trading frequency

All findings here are [summary]. The positive academic results describe something Thunderbot cannot trade. Lopez-Lira and Tang's ChatGPT headline strategy shows a pre-cost Sharpe ratio above 3, but it is a daily long-short portfolio with roughly full turnover; at 25 basis points a trade its cumulative return collapses, the authors say it is feasible only for participants with very low costs "such as market makers", and returns have declined as LLM use spread ([arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)). Backtests over dates inside a model's training window prove nothing: models recall exact past values, and instructions to ignore later data do not prevent it ([Lopez-Lira, Tang and Zhu](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). When five well-known LLM trading agents were re-tested past the model cutoff, all dropped significantly ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920)), and a 2000–2024 re-evaluation found the reported advantages "often vanish" ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3)).

Forward and live results are mostly unflattering. In StockBench, covering March to July 2025, most agents including GPT-5 failed to beat equal-weight buy-and-hold ([arXiv 2510.02209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209v1)); DeepFund found nine leading models made net losses on post-cutoff data ([arXiv 2505.11065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11065)). In Alpha Arena's first season, **four of six frontier models lost between 31% and 63% of real money in 17 days** ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). The longest audited records trail a cheap index: the AI-run AIEQ ETF has lagged the S&P 500 since 2017 ([M1](https://pages.m1.com/invest/stocks/AIEQ)), the sentiment ETF BUZZ returned about 8.3% a year since March 2021 against 14.6% ([VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/social-sentiment-etf-buzz/performance/)), and the Eurekahedge AI hedge fund index earned 9.8% against 13.7% for the S&P 500 ([HedgeCo](https://www.hedgeco.net/news/?p=58736)). The positive exceptions are institutions with proprietary data and staff, such as Bridgewater's AIA fund at about 11% in 2025 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/02/top-hedge-fund-performers-2025-bridgewater-de-shaw-citadel-millennium/?rand=8593)), or short samples concentrated in a few stocks. The nuance on the other side is that purpose-built leak-free models achieve similar news-sentiment results, so look-ahead is not the whole story ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v2)); the binding problem for retail is horizon and cost. The Indian sentiment studies found are small, correlational and untested after costs.

The LLM should therefore have no role in generating or approving signals. Three roles have reasonable support: offline review of code and backtests, which is how Man Group uses its research agent under line-by-line human inspection ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/what-ai-can-do-for-alpha)); writing a plain-language log of each decision from the bot's own computed numbers; and, at most, a one-directional veto that can only defer a trade and alert the owner, placed after deterministic checks for splits, bad ticks and suspensions. Even that veto carries risk: injected text cut one simulated agent's return from 7.8% to 0.9% ([TradeTrap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02261)), and benchmarks show LLM agents are too conservative in rising markets, which is the failure mode of a veto on a trend system. A veto that fires three times a year cannot be validated on returns within a lifetime; by illustrative arithmetic it would take about 64 vetoes to detect a 2% average benefit. Judge it on verifiable precision instead, by recording whether each claimed anomaly was real, and pre-commit to removing it if false alarms exceed true ones after the first ten. Keeping the LLM out of order decisions also keeps the bot plainly "white box" under SEBI's retail algo framework, a point no source addressed directly.

Duration is not the constraint: a 10–120 second backtest fits in 300 seconds on either plan with Fluid compute, the default for projects created since 23 April 2025 ([Fluid compute](https://vercel.com/docs/fluid-compute)). Scheduling was the constraint in the first draft of this report, and it has eased. The legacy Cron Jobs feature still gives Hobby one run a day, fired "at any point within the specified hour". A once-a-day expression such as `40 9 * * 1-5` deploys on Hobby, contrary to the first draft, and by the docs' wording runs somewhere between 09:00:00 and 09:59:59 UTC, which is 14:30 to 15:29 IST, though every example the docs give uses minute 0. That is too loose for a 15:10 decision. **Vercel Schedules, in public beta since 4 November 2026, is tighter: Hobby may schedule as often as every 15 minutes, in an IANA timezone, with a jitter window that is always 15 minutes** ([Schedules limits](https://vercel.com/docs/schedules/limits); [announcement](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-vercel-schedules)). A Hobby schedule of `10 15 * * 1-5` in `Asia/Kolkata` should therefore start between 15:10 and 15:25 IST, an inference from the jitter rule that has not been tested. The first draft's statements that Hobby "cannot wake the worker every few minutes" and that its own cron is "useless for this job" no longer hold as written. Hobby still cannot tick every five minutes or hit 15:10 to the minute, but it can tick every 15 minutes and land a daily run inside a known quarter-hour. The cautions are real. Schedules is a beta whose "behavior, APIs, and limits may change before general availability"; a path target is not retried if the route errors or times out; a Hobby team is capped at 5,000 runs per billing period, against roughly 700 a month for a 15-minute tick through market hours; and after an outage Vercel delivers every run that has not expired, so the handler must compare the scheduled time with the clock and return early if the run is stale.

Both features call the function the same way. Vercel sends an HTTP request to the deployment with the user agent `vercel-cron/1.0`, and if a `CRON_SECRET` environment variable is set it arrives as `Authorization: Bearer <secret>`. A cron job uses GET and runs only on production; a Schedules path target defaults to POST and adds a `ce-id` header that stays the same if a run is delivered twice, which is the documented idempotency key ([Managing Cron Jobs](https://vercel.com/docs/schedules/cron-jobs/manage-cron-jobs); [Schedules configuration](https://vercel.com/docs/schedules/configuration); [Handle runs](https://vercel.com/docs/schedules/handle-runs)). The behaviours the first draft could only recall are confirmed word for word: "Vercel will not retry an invocation if a cron job fails", delivery "can also occasionally invoke the same scheduled run more than once", runs can be missed, and a slow job can overlap the next. Four smaller traps sit on the same page. Cron requests do not follow redirects, so a trailing-slash redirect silently kills the job. The secret is captured when a deployment builds, so rotating it needs a redeploy, and a copy held in Supabase Vault must be rotated with it. A firewall rule that challenges traffic can block the scheduler. And Hobby keeps one hour of runtime logs, so the function must write its own status row. Whether Schedules' private function target works with a Python function is unconfirmed, since the documented helper is JavaScript; a path target works with any route. Whether a personal trading bot counts as "non-commercial" is still a grey area: Vercel's definition turns on "financial gain of anyone involved", and none of its examples covers personal investing.

### A Python function can sit beside the Next.js app, and Vercel now points to Services for it

There are two ways to put Python in the same project, and Vercel's advice shifted between them in 2026 [doc]. The older one is a file-based function. With the project's Root Directory set to `dashboard`, each `.py` file under `dashboard/api/` becomes a function served at its file path and must define a top-level `app`, `application` or `handler` ([Python api directory](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/python/api-directory)). Vercel now describes this as support for "existing projects" and warns that if it detects a Python framework preset, the files under `/api` stop being separate functions. Two things about this layout are still documented nowhere: which wins when `api/*.py` and a Next.js `app/api/**/route.ts` handler claim the same path, and how the Python builder bundles a `worker/` folder that sits beside the Root Directory, although a build can read files above the root by default ([Configure a build](https://vercel.com/docs/builds/configure-a-build)). A distinct prefix such as `/api/py/...`, the one Vercel's Next.js-plus-FastAPI template uses ([nextjs-fastapi](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/digitros/nextjs-fastapi/main/api/index.py)), avoids the first problem, since it collides with none of the dashboard's six handlers.

The newer way is **Services, in beta on all plans**, and the Python page now says: "To run a Python backend alongside another framework, use Services" ([Services](https://vercel.com/docs/services); [Python runtime](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes/python)). A root `vercel.json` declares each service with its own `root`, "relative to `vercel.json`", and an ordered rewrite table decides routing: "Vercel evaluates your top-level rewrites in order and routes each request to the first matching service", and "routing into a service is final" ([Services routing](https://vercel.com/docs/services/routing)). On paper that settles both open questions: precedence is the order of the rewrites, and `worker/` is simply a second service root. Each service is billed and limited like an ordinary function. The older `experimentalServices` key is rejected for projects created on or after 6 July 2026. Python itself has been generally available on Vercel since 15 October 2026, with 3.12 the default and 3.13 and 3.14 offered, and dependencies install from `pyproject.toml`, `requirements.txt` or a `Pipfile` ([changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/python-on-vercel-is-now-generally-available)).

The dependencies fit. Installing the real wheels for yfinance, pandas, numpy and requests measured **168 MB unpacked** in the first pass, with pandas at 45 MB, curl_cffi at 40 MB and numpy at 57 MB including its libraries. Vercel documents a 500 MB uncompressed limit for Python functions and a large-functions beta up to 5 GB ([Functions limitations](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations)). Its builder's source code ships bundles up to **225 MB** as an ordinary zip and above that defers installing packages to every cold start ([dependency-externalizer.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel/vercel/main/packages/python/src/dependency-externalizer.ts)); the documentation mentions neither the threshold nor the mechanism, so 225 MB is an unconfirmed soft limit. Setting `VERCEL_ANALYZE_BUILD_OUTPUT=1` prints each function's size in the build log, which settles the question for a real build. The Yahoo-only worker has about 55 MB of headroom under that figure; the `groww` and `llm` extras were not measured, and one attempt to resolve `growwapi` with the rest failed. Cold-start time for this stack was not measured anywhere.

The dashboard's middleware will block the scheduler as written. Its matcher covers every `/api/...` path and answers 401 to anything without Basic credentials (`dashboard/middleware.ts`). Next.js documents that middleware runs for "every route in your project" ahead of filesystem routes ([Next.js docs source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel/next.js/v15.5.0/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/03-file-conventions/middleware.mdx)), and Vercel says middleware without a matcher runs on every request ([Routing Middleware](https://vercel.com/docs/routing-middleware)). No official sentence says it also fronts non-Next `api/*.py` functions, so in the file-based layout that stays very likely and untested. Under Services the dashboard's middleware lives inside the dashboard service, and since routing into a service is final, a request sent to the worker service should never reach it; that too is an inference to test. The fix is the same in every case: keep the scheduler path out of the matcher, using the negative-lookahead pattern Vercel's own docs show, and make the Python handler verify the bearer secret itself. The three snippets below are assembled from the documented schemas and were **not deployed**.

```json
{
  "services": {
    "dashboard": { "root": "dashboard/" },
    "worker": { "root": "worker/", "entrypoint": "main:app" }
  },
  "rewrites": [
    { "source": "/api/py/(.*)", "destination": { "service": "worker" } },
    { "source": "/(.*)", "destination": { "service": "dashboard" } }
  ]
}
```

That is the Services layout, in a `vercel.json` at the repo root. The worker receives the original path, `/api/py/daily` and not `/daily`, and `main:app` assumes a small ASGI or WSGI app in `worker/main.py` that does not exist yet. In Services mode the `functions` key must move inside a service; the docs do not say whether top-level `regions`, `crons` and `schedules` stay valid there. The file-based layout, in `dashboard/vercel.json`, with the backup schedule discussed in the recommendation, looks like this:

```json
{
  "regions": ["bom1"],
  "functions": {
    "api/py/*.py": { "maxDuration": 300, "excludeFiles": "**/tests/**" }
  },
  "schedules": {
    "daily-decision-backup": {
      "schedule": "10 15 * * 1-5",
      "timezone": "Asia/Kolkata",
      "path": "/api/py/daily",
      "requestMethod": "GET"
    }
  }
}
```

```ts
// dashboard/middleware.ts: exclude the scheduler path; the Python handler must check its own secret
export const config = {
  matcher: ["/((?!api/py/|_next/static|_next/image|favicon.ico).*)"],
};
```

Two pieces of real work remain. Whichever layout is chosen must be proved by one deployment: for the file-based layout, that `worker/` is bundled and that middleware and path precedence behave; for Services, that the region, schedule and middleware settings carry over. Vercel's own guide says that "for an app that one framework already handles, such as Next.js with API routes", the project should be deployed as usual ([Vercel Services guide](https://vercel.com/kb/guide/vercel-services)). This project is a Next.js app plus a Python worker in a sibling folder, which is the mixed-language case Services documents, so this report prefers Services and keeps the file-based layout as the fallback if the beta misbehaves. And the worker's entry points are loops: the serverless wrapper should call `InvestRunner.tick()` once and `JobRunner.run_once()` once, against a Supabase-only store.

### Supabase: pg_cron, pg_net and Vault, with the official SQL

Everything here is [doc]. The SQL was first read from the source files of Supabase's documentation and then checked against the live pages on 10 November 2026: **every block below matches the live docs word for word**. Supabase Cron is pg_cron inside the project's Postgres; jobs "can run anywhere from every second to once a year", schedules are in GMT, and Supabase recommends at most 8 concurrent jobs of at most 10 minutes each ([Cron](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/cron)). No page read restricts pg_cron, pg_net or Vault to paid plans, and the plan comparison on the pricing page has no row for them, so free-plan availability is very likely, but **no official sentence says "available on Free"**, on the live site either. Enable the extensions:

```sql
create extension pg_cron with schema pg_catalog;

grant usage on schema cron to postgres;
grant all privileges on all tables in schema cron to postgres;
```

```sql
create extension pg_net with schema "extensions";
```

pg_net queues an asynchronous HTTP request that is sent after the transaction commits ([pg_net](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/extensions/pg_net)). The official pattern for a scheduled call with its secret in Vault is ([Scheduling Edge Functions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/schedule-functions)):

```sql
select vault.create_secret('https://project-ref.supabase.co', 'project_url');
select vault.create_secret('YOUR_SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY', 'secret_key');
```

```sql
select
  cron.schedule(
    'invoke-function-every-minute',
    '* * * * *', -- every minute
    $$
    select
      net.http_post(
          url:= (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'project_url') || '/functions/v1/function-name',
          headers:=jsonb_build_object(
            'Content-type', 'application/json',
            'apikey', (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'secret_key')
          ),
          body:=concat('{"time": "', now(), '"}')::jsonb
      ) as request_id;
    $$
  );
```

The official template for calling out when a row is inserted, with the URL and key read from Vault, is ([Database Webhooks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/webhooks)):

```sql
create function public.call_function_name()
returns trigger
language plpgsql
security definer
set search_path = ''
as $$
begin
  perform net.http_post(
    url := (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'project_url') || '/functions/v1/function-name',
    headers := jsonb_build_object(
      'Content-Type', 'application/json',
      'apikey', (select decrypted_secret from vault.decrypted_secrets where name = 'secret_key')
    ),
    body := jsonb_build_object(
      'type', tg_op,
      'table', tg_table_name,
      'schema', tg_table_schema,
      'record', to_jsonb(new),
      'old_record', null
    )
  );
  return new;
end;
$$;

create trigger call_function_name
after insert on public.my_table
for each row execute function public.call_function_name();
```

Run history is not cleaned automatically; the official cleanup job is:

```sql
select cron.schedule(
  'job-run-details-cleanup', -- name of the cron job
  '0 0 * * *', -- daily at midnight (GMT)
  $$ delete from cron.job_run_details where end_time < now() - interval '7 days' $$
);
```

For Thunderbot the adaptation is small and is **not from the docs**: store the Vercel endpoint URL and a long random bearer secret in Vault (the Supabase secret key is not needed for a call to Vercel), point `net.http_post` at them, and use UTC schedules. `40 9 * * 1-5` is 15:10 IST on weekdays; `*/5 3-10 * * 1-5` ticks every five minutes from 08:30 to 16:25 IST, with the worker ignoring ticks outside market hours and on NSE holidays, which cron cannot express. The existing `public.jobs` table is the natural target for the insert trigger. Job names are case sensitive, and scheduling a second job under an existing name overwrites the first ([Cron quickstart](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/cron/quickstart)).

Four behaviours shape the design. pg_net **does not retry**, and its queue lives in unlogged tables that do not survive a crash ([pg_net README](https://github.com/supabase/pg_net/blob/master/README.md)). Its default timeout is 2,000 ms in Supabase's docs, confirmed on the live page, and 5,000 ms upstream, so always pass `timeout_milliseconds` explicitly, as Supabase's own Cron quickstart example does; upstream allows up to 600,000 ms. Responses are kept for six hours in `net._http_response`. And the decrypted secret sits briefly in `net.http_request_queue`, where "any role that can log in to your database can read the secret key while the request waits". Whether a Vercel function keeps running after pg_net times out and disconnects is still documented nowhere. The supporting evidence is that Vercel's request cancellation is opt-in and "only available when you're using the Node.js runtime" ([vercel.json reference](https://vercel.com/docs/project-configuration/vercel-json)), so a Python function should run on; test it. The safe pattern is to treat the insert trigger as a latency optimisation and let every periodic tick also sweep for jobs still marked `queued`.

### The free project still pauses, and scheduled activity may not prevent it

The docs are plain that "Supabase pauses Free Plan projects that show low activity over a 7-day period", that the test is "sufficient user database activity", and that "a few user requests to the database each day" is typically enough; paid projects are never paused ([Project Pausing](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/free-project-pausing)) [doc, confirmed on the live page]. A warning email arrives roughly a week before the pause, and a paused project can be restored for up to one year. **Supabase has not said whether pg_cron activity counts**: the page speaks only of user activity and names dashboard visits and API calls as the remedies. In a community thread summarised in the first pass, a free-plan user received a pause warning on 5 November 2026 despite 87 successful keep-alive API calls that touched no table ([discussion #53090](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/53090)); an older thread reports that a daily insert, update and delete kept a project alive where a bare connection did not ([discussion #13121](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/13121)). Thunderbot's worker makes real reads and writes from outside on every tick, which is the pattern most likely to count, and a Vercel-side schedule that touches the database every day, weekends included, covers the days pg_cron's weekday jobs leave quiet. The free plan still gives no guarantee, and if the project pauses, pg_cron stops with it, silently. The free plan also has no automatic backups. For real money, Supabase Pro, "from $25 / month", is the only guaranteed route ([pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)).

Edge Functions cannot host the strategy. They are TypeScript on a Deno-compatible runtime with 256 MB of memory, 2 seconds of CPU per request and a wall-clock limit of 150 seconds on Free and 400 seconds on paid plans; Free includes 500,000 invocations a month ([Edge Functions limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/limits); [pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)) [doc]. That Mumbai is a supported Edge Functions region comes from the first pass and was not re-checked. Supabase offers no static outbound address either: its IPv4 add-on is for inbound connections, and "the outbound IP address is not static and cannot be guaranteed" ([IPv4 address](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/ipv4-address)).

## Yahoo answered a cloud address that sent a browser User-Agent, but it cannot supply every history, so prices need a cache and a fallback

The first pass could test nothing, because its sandbox refused every market-data host. On 10 November 2026 the checks were run directly from this cloud session ([direct checks](../research_notes/Thunderbot%20strategy%20and%20serverless%20hosting/verification_direct_checks.md)) [computed]. **They are one data point: a single datacentre address, about 25 requests to Yahoo spaced a second apart, on one evening.** They do not show how Vercel's Mumbai addresses are treated, and they do not rule out rate-limit waves.

Yahoo's v8 chart endpoint, which yfinance 1.8.0 (released 3 November 2026) still uses for price history ([history.py](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/blob/main/yfinance/scrapers/history.py); [data.py](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/blob/main/yfinance/data.py)) [doc], returned **HTTP 200 on every request sent with a desktop Chrome User-Agent and no cookie or crumb**, for NIFTYBEES.NS, JUNIORBEES.NS, MID150BEES.NS, GOLDBEES.NS, LIQUIDCASE.NS, LIQUIDBEES.NS, MOM30IETF.NS and ^NSEI, and **HTTP 429 to the same URL sent with curl's default User-Agent**. The header decided it that evening. Quotes were fresh, stamped 15:30 IST on 9 November, the latest trading session. This fits the issue tracker's history without overturning it: Yahoo has returned 429 in waves since November 2024, sometimes to a single request; changing IP address did not help several users; and from spring 2025 browser impersonation through `curl_cffi` did, which points to client fingerprinting more than per-IP limits ([issue #2422](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2422); [issue #2518](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2518)). **Whether Vercel's Mumbai addresses are served reliably can still only be settled by testing from the deployed function.** The repo's dependency floor of `yfinance>=0.2.54` predates the fingerprinting fix and should be pinned to a current release.

The histories are the bigger problem, and four findings bear on the strategy. Yahoo's NIFTYBEES.NS and GOLDBEES.NS series are back-adjusted for the December 2019 unit splits, but each has **two corrupt bars, on 19 and 20 December 2019**, ten and a hundred times too low, and no split event is returned; this supersedes the project's earlier note that the NIFTYBEES split was simply unadjusted. The ETF histories start on **1 January 2009**, so Yahoo cannot supply 2008. **MID150BEES.NS returns only 32 daily bars, from 22 September 2026**, although its metadata gives a first trade date of 31 January 2019, so Yahoo cannot currently provide the 200 closes the midcap signal needs, let alone a backtest. And MOM30IETF.NS starts on 24 June 2024, not at its August 2022 listing. LIQUIDCASE.NS is present from 17 January 2024 and usable as a rising-price cash series; LIQUIDBEES.NS is a flat 1,000. Yahoo's NSE data has other documented defects, such as an abrupt wrong price for Raymond in May 2025 ([yfinance issues](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues?q=is%3Aissue+NSE+OR+%22.NS%22+OR+NIFTYBEES+OR+BSE+OR+India+sort%3Acreated-desc)).

History for MID150BEES and MOM30IETF must therefore come from elsewhere. **The source to use is the NSE bhavcopy archive**, either the exchange's daily files or the split-adjusted [NSEDATA4ME](https://github.com/ancap97/NSEDATA4ME) database built from them, cross-checked against back-adjusted AMFI NAVs from mfapi.in (scheme [146271](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/146271) for MID150BEES and [150455](https://api.mfapi.in/mf/150455) for MOM30IETF). For the midcap signal alone a midcap index series is an alternative, which is what the backtest used; NSE's daily file of all index closes should carry it, though that was not checked.

The workload is tiny, which is the saving grace. Ten symbols need 10–25 requests a day if history is cached in a Supabase table and each run fetches only the latest bars; today the worker re-downloads full history into a local pickle a serverless function cannot keep. The robust layout is a price table in Supabase seeded once from the bhavcopy archive and appended daily; a primary live source, Yahoo with a browser User-Agent or a current yfinance; a second source, Upstox's public candles, when the primary fails or its last bar is stale; a repair for the four known corrupt bars and a refusal to trade on any split-sized jump against stored history; nightly reconciliation against the exchange file, which was available from about 16:45 IST; and a status row per run. Adjusted history from one source must never be mixed with raw closes from another.

## API orders need a broker-registered static IP and a limit price; reads, signals and alerts do not

### The Groww Trade API today

Everything in this subsection is [doc], read on Groww's pages and in its SDK on 10 November 2026. The API costs **₹499 plus taxes a month, or ₹4,999 a year**, which with 18% GST is ₹588.82 and ₹5,898.82 ([Groww Trade API](https://groww.in/trade-api); [Groww blog, modified 30 September 2026](https://groww.in/blog/api-trading-on-groww)). **Groww's ₹0 plan does allow order placement**, a point the first draft left open. The blog says: "The difference between the free and paid plans is data access, not order placement", and lists authentication, instruments, placing, modifying and cancelling orders, smart orders, positions, holdings and margin as included; live quotes and historical candles are not. A bot that takes its prices elsewhere may therefore not need the paid plan, with two cautions: the plan is called a "Free Trial" with no stated length, and the developer docs' prerequisites still ask for "an active Trading API Subscription". Normal per-order brokerage applies either way. The Python SDK `growwapi` has been at version 1.5.0 since 6 December 2025 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/growwapi/)), and Groww's developer changelogs stop that month, with no word on the static-IP regime that began in April 2026.

Login has three flavours: a token generated by hand that "expires daily at 6:00 AM", an API key plus secret that "requires daily approval" on Groww's site, and an API key plus TOTP ([Groww docs](https://groww.in/trade-api/docs/curl)). Every flow is one HTTPS POST to `/v1/token/api/access`, with either a six-digit TOTP code or a SHA-256 checksum of the secret and a timestamp. **TOTP is the only unattended login.** The Python docs describe it as "No Expiry"; one line in the cURL docs labels the same flow as needing daily approval, which looks like a copying slip, so test it before relying on it. The flow is stateless, so a serverless function can mint the day's token itself. The token endpoint is capped at 150 requests per 24 hours, a limit the repo held only as a code comment and that Groww's docs confirm. Documented rate limits are 10 a second and 250 a minute for orders, 20 a second and 500 a minute for non-trading calls such as holdings and order status, 10 and 300 for live data, and 5 and 30 for authentication ([Groww docs](https://groww.in/trade-api/docs/python-sdk)). Each order takes a reference id of 8 to 20 alphanumeric characters; a duplicate is rejected with error GA007 and an order can be looked up by its id, which makes it a usable idempotency key, though how long uniqueness lasts is not stated ([orders docs](https://groww.in/trade-api/docs/curl/orders)). Orders have day validity only. There is no sandbox, so the only end-to-end test is a very small real order. There is no after-market flag either, but every order response carries an `amo_status` field, "Status of the order placed after market", so after-hours orders are probably queued; the docs never say so in a sentence, and the first draft's "orders must be sent during market hours" is replaced by "test it with a small order".

**API orders must be limit orders.** NSE's rules treat every order arriving through a client API as an algo order and state: "Algo orders with order type as Market Order are not permitted" ([NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf); [consolidated circular NSE/INVG/73992](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG73992.zip)). Groww's docs still list MARKET as an order type, and its sample strategy places one, without explaining how the two fit together. The bot should send LIMIT orders only, which is what the strategy wanted anyway.

### What SEBI's static-IP rule requires

The circulars and the FAQ were read directly on 10 November 2026 [doc]. The framework stems from SEBI's circular of 4 February 2025, which told brokers to allow API access "only through a unique vendor client specific API key and static IP whitelisted by the broker" ([SEBI, February 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/feb-2025/1738665456458.pdf)), and it has applied to all brokers since **1 April 2026** ([SEBI, September 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf)). No later SEBI circular on the subject appears on SEBI's listing as of 10 November 2026, by a title search that is not exhaustive, and NSE restated the standards unchanged in its consolidated circular of 30 April 2026. The operating rules are in NSE circular NSE/INVG/67858 of 5 May 2025. Clients "must mandatorily provide the stockbroker with a static IP address(es) which will be mapped to the API keys"; there is **one primary address and an optional secondary** "for the purpose of connectivity redundancy"; and clients may update the mapped addresses "as needed, but not more than once a calendar week" ([NSE/INVG/67858](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG67858.pdf), Annexure A.1, A.2 and A.6). The first draft attributed the weekly limit to NSE's FAQ, which does not contain it. Zerodha documents the same limit, as do Upstox and Dhan on the first pass's summaries ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/developer/api-documentation/announcements/static-ip-apis/); [Dhan](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/authentication/)). A self-built algo for one's own account needs exchange registration only above 10 orders a second, but Zerodha is explicit that the static IP is required "regardless of your order rate" ([Zerodha FAQs](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/kite-connect-api-faqs)). An address may be shared only within a family, defined as self, spouse, dependent children and dependent parents.

On Groww the addresses are entered on the API Keys page as a primary and a backup, and "without this, your orders won't go through" ([Groww guide, modified 2 September 2026](https://groww.in/blog/static-ip-api-trading-setup)) [doc]. Groww's own change-frequency limit, the error it returns for an unregistered address and whether it accepts IPv6 are stated nowhere: its developer docs never mention static IPs at all. Assume the exchange's once a calendar week. **Reads are exempt on the exchange's own wording.** NSE's FAQ says "Client static IP will be required only in case of Tech savvy Investor using API for placing orders" ([NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf)), and Zerodha says its market data, order book and positions APIs "can continue to be accessed from any IP address" ([Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip)). For Groww this is implied, by "Groww whitelists this IP to authorize API order placement", and not stated; NSE's Annexure A.1 literally ties the address to API access as a whole, so a broker could lawfully check every call. Test one holdings read from an unregistered address before relying on it. Zerodha's FAQ adds that the same address may be registered with two different brokers.

Routing orders through a third-party address is acceptable in principle and unsafe in one specific form. Zerodha's FAQ says a static IP may come from "an ISP, a cloud provider (such as AWS or GCP), or a VPN/VPC service" [doc], so a proxy is not prohibited as such. The problem is sharing: NSE's Annexure A.7 maps an address to one client or one family, Zerodha warns that sharing outside the family "may lead to suspension", and a proxy vendor itself concedes that a shared address "can be rejected if another QuotaGuard customer has already registered it with the same broker" ([QuotaGuard](https://www.quotaguard.com/blog/kotak-neo-api-static-ip)). On security, a standard HTTPS forward proxy sees only the destination and timing, because TLS runs end to end with Groww; that protection vanishes with any product that asks for its own certificate to be installed or terminates TLS itself. On a shared address the IP check adds nothing against the service's other users, so security rests entirely on the API key and TOTP secret. Only a dedicated address should be registered, with a second address from a different provider in the backup slot, because a failed proxy cannot be swapped for a week. Groww's API terms were not read.

On a laptop, the scheduler is easy and the wake is not. The templates below were assembled from documented options and **not executed**. Linux is the most capable: a system-level systemd timer accepts an IANA timezone, catches up missed runs and can wake from suspend, though not from power-off ([systemd.time](https://www.freedesktop.org/software/systemd/man/latest/systemd.time.html); [systemd.timer](https://manpages.debian.org/systemd.timer)).

```ini
# /etc/systemd/system/thunderbot.timer (system unit, so WakeSystem has the needed privilege)
[Timer]
OnCalendar=Mon..Fri *-*-* 15:10:00 Asia/Kolkata
Persistent=true
WakeSystem=true
AccuracySec=1s
```

On macOS, a LaunchAgent with `StartCalendarInterval` runs a job missed during sleep when the Mac wakes ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/library/mac/documentation/MacOSX/Conceptual/BPSystemStartup/Chapters/ScheduledJobs.html)), and `pmset` can schedule the wake, but a 2026 developer-forum report says a lid-closed MacBook will not run the job until opened ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/815034)).

```bash
sudo pmset repeat wakeorpoweron MTWRF 15:08:00   # wake two minutes early on weekdays
# LaunchAgent: StartCalendarInterval with Weekday 1-5, Hour 15, Minute 10; wrap the command in /usr/bin/caffeinate -i
```

On Windows, Task Scheduler can wake, catch up and retry, but wake timers are widely reported as unreliable on Modern Standby laptops, especially lid-closed or on battery ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1660115/regarding-waking-up-from-sleep-on-a-modern-standby); [Dell community](https://www.dell.com/community/Latitude/Wake-Timers-and-Task-Schedules-do-not-trigger-in-S0-Power-State/m-p/8310145)). The cmdlet syntax is [recalled].

```powershell
$trigger  = New-ScheduledTaskTrigger -Weekly -DaysOfWeek Monday,Tuesday,Wednesday,Thursday,Friday -At 3:10PM
$settings = New-ScheduledTaskSettingsSet -WakeToRun -StartWhenAvailable -AllowStartIfOnBatteries `
            -RestartCount 3 -RestartInterval (New-TimeSpan -Minutes 2) -MultipleInstances IgnoreNew
```

A missed run is recoverable for this strategy because it decides monthly; what must be bullet-proof is not trading twice. Every trigger, whichever it is, should first claim a row keyed on strategy and IST trade date in Postgres and exit if another trigger already holds it. A run arriving before about 15:25 IST proceeds normally; a later one acts at the next session and logs the slip. Since the decision matters on about twelve days a year, a calendar reminder on the last trading day of each month plus a dead-man alert buys more reliability than any wake-timer tuning. India has no daylight saving, so 09:40 UTC is 15:10 IST all year.

## Recommendation: one rotation, one fund, two free schedulers, and a human finger on the order

### Strategy 1, the core: a four-sleeve trend rotation with a liquid-ETF cash leg

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Instruments | NIFTYBEES, JUNIORBEES, MID150BEES, GOLDBEES; cash leg LIQUIDCASE (backup LIQUIDADD) |
| Target weights | 25% per sleeve |
| Signal | For each sleeve, the 200-day simple average of daily closes, including the decision day |
| Signal series | As backtested: Nifty 50 index, Nifty Next 50 index, the midcap index and the GOLDBEES price. Using each ETF's own split-adjusted close is simpler; choose one and freeze it before testing |
| Price history | Seed a Supabase price table once from the NSE bhavcopy archive (or the split-adjusted NSEDATA4ME database built from it) and append each day. | Decision | Last trading day of each month, at 15:10 IST, from an NSE holiday table and not the calendar |
| Hold or park | A sleeve holds its ETF if its signal series is above its 200-day average; otherwise that 25% sits in LIQUIDCASE |
| Trading | Trade only when a sleeve switches, and once at the December month-end; touch an asset only if it is opened, closed, or more than 5 percentage points from target; one sell order per ETF, because Groww charges its DP fee per sell order |
| Execution | **Limit orders only**, between 15:10 and 15:30 the same day; exchange rules do not permit market orders through the API, and the same discipline applies to orders placed by hand. Skip a buy priced more than about 0.5% above indicative NAV for the three equity ETFs and about 1% for GOLDBEES, whose close sat more than 0.7% above NAV on one day in twenty; refuse to act on a split-sized price jump |
| Activity | About 15–17 orders a year including the liquid ETF; in cash about 28% of the time |

This can be backtested from 2008 on free bhavcopy prices for three sleeves and from 2011–2012 for midcaps. **Realistic range: about 5.5–9% a year after costs and tax, with a worst fall of about 20–33%.** The first draft said 6–9.5% and 15–30%, and the NAV check moved both. The derivation is as follows. The upper end starts from the backtest itself, 9.3% at month-end and 7.3–9.8% across 30 specifications, and takes off about 0.2–0.3 points because idle cash, held about 28% of the time and taxed at slab rate, now yields about 5% and earned about 4.8% over 2019–2026, not the 6% modelled. The lower end sits near the bottom of the no-gold version, which returned 5.4–9.1% across specifications with a median of 7.3%, less the same cash adjustment, with some allowance for the decay every published trend rule has shown. The worst-fall range takes the closing-price backtest's 18% at month-end and 31% at its worst decision day and adds the roughly 2 points by which closing prices understated panic-day falls against NAV; without gold the worst falls ran from 22% to 44% across specifications. The 1% gold tolerance in the execution rule is this report's own suggestion from one year of closing premiums. All of it comes from one history and one unreviewed run that was not repeated after the verification, and none of it is a promise.

The main risks are specific. Half or more of the edge is a gold bet that may not repeat. The Nifty signal was still out of the market on 30 October 2026 while NIFTYBEES's NAV rose 10.1% between 26 October and 9 November, and the rule will lag every V-shaped recovery in the same way; counting that fortnight and the pre-2016 distributions, buy-and-hold NIFTYBEES has probably matched or beaten the rotation on return since 2011. A different decision day produced a 31% fall in the backtest. Gold and liquid gains are taxed at slab rate. And a passive equal-weight mix of the same four ETFs, rebalanced each December, returned 11.7% after tax since 2011 in the same unrepeated backtest: an owner who can sit through a 27% fall on closing prices, probably two points more on NAV, or the 49% of 2008, should hold that instead and needs no bot. The bot should paper-track that mix as its benchmark and the three-sleeve Nifty 50, Next 50 and gold variant as a cross-check built wholly on real ETF data.

### The no-server setup

Run the dashboard and a Python worker in one Vercel project. Deploy them as two Services from a root `vercel.json`, the dashboard from `dashboard/` and the worker from `worker/` with `/api/py/` rewritten to it, or, if the Services beta misbehaves, as file-based functions under `dashboard/api/py/`. Either way the middleware matcher excludes that prefix and the handler checks a bearer secret.

**Use two schedulers, with Supabase pg_cron as the primary and Vercel Schedules as the backup.** pg_cron is the primary because it is the only free option that fires to the minute and it is not a beta: `40 9 * * 1-5` starts the decision at 15:10 IST, a five-minute tick sweeps queued backtests, and an insert trigger on `public.jobs` makes dashboard backtests start promptly, all through pg_net with the secret in Vault and an explicit timeout. Its weaknesses are that pg_net never retries and that a paused free project takes pg_cron down silently. Vercel Schedules on Hobby is the backup because it lives outside the database: a `10 15 * * 1-5` schedule in `Asia/Kolkata` calls the same path somewhere between 15:10 and 15:25 IST, and a second, daily schedule that also runs at weekends touches the database and raises an alert if Supabase does not answer. It is not the primary because it was announced as a beta on 4 November 2026, its limits may change, it does not retry, and a 15-minute window can leave the owner as little as five minutes before the close. Hobby's legacy once-a-day cron, which fires anywhere within its hour, is the stand-in if the beta is withdrawn. Neither scheduler is trusted alone: every trigger first claims an idempotency row keyed on strategy and IST trade date, so whichever arrives first does the work and the other exits.

Replace the SQLite-first store with a Supabase-only one, cache prices in a Supabase table seeded from bhavcopy, and ping an external dead-man check on every run including no-ops. On a rebalance day the bot sends the exact sells and buys with limit prices within a couple of minutes of 15:10; the owner places them as limit orders in the Groww app; after 15:30 the bot mints a TOTP token, reads holdings and the order book, and confirms the portfolio matches.

The limits that shape it are few. Functions get 300 seconds with Fluid compute. The Python bundle should stay under 225 MB, a threshold taken from the builder's source that the docs do not state. Hobby keeps one hour of logs, so the function writes its own status row. pg_net does not retry, so the periodic tick must double as a sweeper. A free Supabase project can pause after a quiet week, so paper trading can run free but real money justifies Supabase Pro at $25 a month, and Vercel Pro at $20 removes the fair-use question and brings to-the-minute scheduling. A third scheduler outside both vendors is now optional: Cloud Scheduler if punctuality matters, GitHub Actions if not.

**What stays impossible without a registered static IP:** placing, modifying or cancelling Groww orders through the API, and by assumption API-created GTT and OCO orders, from Vercel on Hobby, from Vercel Pro (whose $100 add-on gives addresses shared with other customers, which should not be registered), from Supabase, from GitHub's standard runners, or from a home connection with a changing address. Market orders through the API are not permitted with or without one. Everything else works on the exchange's own wording, which requires the static IP "only in case of Tech savvy Investor using API for placing orders": signals, paper trading, price fetching, token minting, reading holdings and order status, writing to Supabase and sending alerts. Groww implies the same without stating it, so one read from an unregistered address should be tested. If full automation is wanted later, the order of preference is Groww Cloud, if a logged-in check shows that deployed scripts need no client address; then a home static IP at ₹300–1,000 a month; then a dedicated egress address on a scale-to-zero container, a Cloud NAT gateway or a small VPS, with a second address registered as backup. Never a shared address.

## Conclusion

The research inverted the reader's starting questions. The idle-cash problem was already solved inside the backtest and only needed solving in the account; the search for a better timing rule ended at a passive four-ETF mix that beat every rule on return; and the momentum question turned out to be about tax wrappers, not stock selection. Verification then pushed every number that mattered the same way: cash pays a point less than assumed, buy-and-hold earned more than the price data showed, panics cut deeper than closing prices admit, and the products that track factor indices keep less of the premium than the indices advertise. What a bot adds for this investor is therefore narrow and real: drawdown control, executed without emotion, at ETF costs low enough that the control is nearly free, and worth little or nothing in extra return. Whether that is worth having depends on one honest answer from the owner about sitting through a 30–50% fall.

The hosting answer has the same shape, and it improved while the report was being checked. The always-on server was only ever needed for two things, a punctual clock and a fixed address. A database cron supplies the first for free, and since 4 November Vercel's own beta scheduler supplies a second, looser one, which turns the design question from which scheduler to use into what happens when two of them fire. The fixed address is needed for about a dozen order days a year, too little to justify renting one or trusting a shared one, and the one option that could remove the need, Groww's own hosted runner, costs nothing to inspect. The first engineering task is still not a scheduler. It is to seed a price table from the exchange's own files, because Yahoo answered a cloud address politely and then turned out to hold 32 days of the midcap ETF, and to log what the deployed function receives from Yahoo for a week before trusting it at 15:10.
